# Black Swan

A concept from [Nassim Taleb](../people/nassim-taleb.md): an event that is rare, high-impact, and — crucially — **rationalised as predictable in hindsight**, even though it was not predictable beforehand.

## Three Criteria

1. **Outlier** — outside the realm of regular expectations; nothing in the past pointed to it
2. **Extreme impact** — massive consequences, positive or negative
3. **Retrospective predictability** — after the fact, we construct explanations that make it seem obvious

The third criterion is the most insidious. We are story-making creatures. We cannot tolerate randomness, so we build narratives that explain the past as inevitable — and then mistakenly believe we could have predicted it.

## Why It Matters

Black Swans dominate history. The outlier events — wars, technological revolutions, financial crises, pandemics — shape the world far more than the "normal" flow of events. Yet most planning, forecasting, and risk management ignores them.

> "Black Swans dominate society and history and people, because of ex post rationalisation, think themselves capable of understanding them."

## The Turkey Problem

A turkey is fed every day for 1,000 days. Each day adds confidence that the farmer is benevolent. On day 1,001 — Thanksgiving — the turkey's model is perfectly refuted. The more data you have from a stable period, the more confident you are — and the more exposed you are to a sudden reversal.

This maps to any fragile system that has been shielded from volatility: the longer the calm, the more catastrophic the eventual shock.

## Relationship to [Antifragility](./antifragility.md)

You cannot reliably predict Black Swans. The correct response is not better forecasting — it is better **structure**:

- Use the [Barbell Strategy](./barbell-strategy.md) to cap your downside
- Seek [antifragility](./antifragility.md) — position yourself to benefit from the unexpected
- Build optionality — when you don't know what will happen, preserve the ability to act

## Positive vs Negative Black Swans

Not all Black Swans are catastrophes. Some are windfalls:
- A book going viral
- A startup becoming a category-defining company
- A scientific breakthrough

The barbell positions you to be exposed to **positive** Black Swans while insulating against negative ones.
