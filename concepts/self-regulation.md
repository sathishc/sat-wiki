# Self-Regulation

## One-Line Definition

The ability to manage disruptive emotions and impulses — staying calm, adaptable, and trustworthy under pressure.

## In Goleman's EQ Model

Second domain of [Emotional Intelligence](../concepts/emotional-intelligence.md). Where [Self-Awareness](../concepts/self-awareness.md) is *noticing*, Self-Regulation is *managing*.

### Components

- **Emotional self-control** — keeping disruptive emotions and impulses in check
- **Transparency** — honesty and integrity; acting consistently with your values
- **Adaptability** — flexibility in handling change and challenges
- **Achievement orientation** — drive to meet a personal standard of excellence
- **Initiative** — readiness to act on opportunities
- **Optimism** — seeing setbacks as manageable, not permanent

## The Amygdala Link

Self-regulation is literally neurological management. The prefrontal cortex (reason) must override the amygdala (emotion) when triggered. High self-regulators:
- Have shorter amygdala hijacks (faster cortisol clearance)
- Can pause between stimulus and response
- Rarely blow up in public — or if they do, recover quickly

## Why Leaders Need It Most

Emotions are contagious. A leader who can't regulate their own emotional state broadcasts anxiety, anger, or chaos to the whole team. Self-regulation is the minimum entry fee for effective leadership.
