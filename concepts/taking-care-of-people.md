# Taking Care of Your People

> "The most important thing you can do as a CEO is take care of your people." — Ben Horowitz

## One-Line Definition

The foundational obligation of leadership: ensuring your people are set up to succeed, treated fairly, and know where they stand — before optimising for the company, the customers, or the shareholders.

## The Argument

Horowitz makes this claim not as a moral statement but as a practical one. The logic:

1. **The company's performance is a function of its people's performance**
2. **People's performance is a function of how well they are managed, developed, and treated**
3. **Therefore: taking care of people is the CEO's highest-leverage activity**

This sounds obvious. In practice, CEOs routinely deprioritise people issues — they feel urgent but not important during a crisis. Horowitz's argument: they are always urgent, because a team that is mismanaged, uninformed, or unfairly treated starts to fall apart at exactly the moment you need it most.

## What "Taking Care" Actually Means

It is **not** about being nice, avoiding hard conversations, or making people comfortable. It means:

- **Clarity** — people know what they're responsible for, how they're performing, and what success looks like
- **Fairness** — compensation, promotions, and recognition are applied consistently and transparently
- **Honesty** — people hear the truth about their performance; not after the fact at a firing, but continuously
- **Development** — managers actively help people grow; not just get through the current quarter
- **Respect for their time and effort** — not burning people out with poor prioritisation or directionless work

## The Three Imperatives

Horowitz frames it as three concentric obligations:
1. **Take care of your people** — first
2. **Then take care of your company**
3. **Then take care of your shareholders**

In that order. Not because shareholders don't matter, but because the causal chain runs in that direction.
