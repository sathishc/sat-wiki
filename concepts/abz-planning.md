# ABZ Planning

**Have three plans running in parallel: A (current path), B (pivot), Z (lifeboat).**

A career planning framework from [Reid Hoffman](../people/reid-hoffman.md)'s [*The Start-up of You*](../sources/hoffman-startup-of-you.md). Applies startup pivoting strategy to individual careers.

## The Framework

### Plan A: Your Current Path

What you're doing right now to build [Competitive Advantage](competitive-advantage.md).

- Your current job, role, skills you're developing
- The bet you're actively making on your future
- Where you're investing most of your time and energy

**Most people have only Plan A.** This is dangerous in a changing world.

**Plan A is always a hypothesis:**
- You're testing whether this path leads where you want to go
- You're gathering data: Is this working? Am I building the right skills? Is the market rewarding this?
- You should be willing to pivot when data says Plan A isn't working

### Plan B: Your Pivot

An alternative path if Plan A isn't working or a better opportunity emerges.

- **Not a backup plan** (that's Plan Z) — it's an alternative strategy
- An adjacent possible: close enough to Plan A that you can pivot quickly
- Kept warm through side projects, learning adjacent skills, building connections in adjacent fields

**Why Plan B matters:**
- The world changes; what works today may not work tomorrow
- New opportunities emerge that are better than Plan A
- Rigidity kills careers — pivoting is adapting, not failing

**How to develop Plan B:**
- Explore adjacent industries/roles
- Build skills that apply to multiple paths
- Network with people in adjacent fields
- Run "identity small projects" to test alternatives
- Pay attention to what energizes you

**Examples:**
- Software engineer → product manager
- Consultant → operator at a startup
- Corporate lawyer → venture capitalist
- Academic → industry researcher

Pivoting is not failure. It's intelligence.

### Plan Z: Your Lifeboat

The fallback that gives you stability if Plans A and B crater.

- **Not your dream scenario** — it's your acceptable worst case
- Provides safety net so you can take intelligent risks with A and B
- Reduces fear paralysis

**Examples:**
- Move in with parents temporarily
- Savings cushion that covers 12 months expenses
- Freelance/contract work in your base skill
- Take any job to pay bills while you regroup
- Go back to school
- Move to a low cost-of-living location

**The paradox:** Having Plan Z lets you take bigger swings with Plans A and B. Without a safety net, you're paralyzed by fear and can't take necessary risks.

**Plan Z is not giving up.** It's your temporary landing pad for regrouping and relaunching.

## The Core Insight

**Most career failures come from:**
1. **No Plan Z:** Risk paralysis. Too scared to take necessary risks because you have no fallback.
2. **No Plan B:** Rigidity. When Plan A fails (and it often does), you're stuck. No alternative strategy.

**The goal:** Intelligent, calculated risk-taking. Not reckless gambling, not fearful stagnation.

## How ABZ Planning Works Together

```
Plan A (active)
     ↓ [gather data, test hypothesis]
     ↓
     ├─→ working? keep going
     ├─→ not working? pivot to Plan B
     ├─→ better opportunity? pivot to Plan B
     └─→ disaster? retreat to Plan Z, regroup, relaunch

Plan B (warm, ready)
     ↓ [kept alive through side projects, networks, learning]
     ↓
     └─→ becomes new Plan A when pivot happens

Plan Z (insurance)
     ↓ [documented, ready, provides psychological safety]
     └─→ enables risk-taking with A and B
```

## Connection to Startup Strategy

Startups use ABZ thinking:
- **Plan A:** Launch with an MVP, test the market
- **Plan B:** Pivot when the data says the original idea isn't working (Instagram started as Burbn; Slack started as a gaming company)
- **Plan Z:** Founder has savings, can move back with parents, has skills to get a job if startup fails

**Famous pivots:**
- Twitter (podcasting platform → microblogging)
- YouTube (video dating site → general video sharing)
- Nintendo (playing cards → toys → video games)
- Netflix (DVD by mail → streaming)

Pivoting is not failure; it's learning and adapting.

## Practical Steps

### 1. Document Your Current Plan A
- What's your current bet?
- What skills are you building?
- What market are you positioning for?
- Is it working? What data would tell you it's not?

### 2. Develop Plan B
- What are 2-3 adjacent paths?
- What side projects test Plan B?
- Who in your network is in Plan B territory?
- What skills bridge A and B?

### 3. Establish Plan Z
- What's your acceptable worst case?
- How much runway do you have? (savings, family support, freelance income)
- Document it: knowing you have a parachute lets you jump

### 4. Gather Data on Plan A
- Set checkpoints: "In 6 months, I should see X. If not, I'll pivot."
- What signals tell you Plan A is working? Not working?
- Stay flexible; don't fall victim to sunk cost fallacy

### 5. Keep Plan B Warm
- Invest 10-20% of time in Plan B learning/networking
- Don't let it go cold
- Attend conferences, take courses, build side projects

## Connection to Other Concepts

- **[Barbell strategy](barbell-strategy.md):** Taleb's barbell is similar — safe base + aggressive risk. Plan Z is the safe base; Plan A/B are the aggressive bets.
- **[Antifragility](antifragility.md):** Plan B is an option. Options are valuable in uncertain environments.
- **[Permanent beta](permanent-beta.md):** ABZ planning embodies permanent beta — you're always ready to iterate and pivot.
- **[Systems thinking](systems-thinking.md):** ABZ is adaptive planning in a complex, changing system. You respond to [Feedback Loops](feedback-loops.md).

## When to Pivot from A to B

**Signals that Plan A isn't working:**
- Skills you're building aren't valued by the market
- Industry is declining (see [disruptive innovation](innovators-dilemma.md))
- You're not learning anymore
- Better opportunity emerges in Plan B territory
- Passion has died; work feels like a grind with no purpose

**Don't pivot too quickly:**
- Give Plan A time to compound (see [compounding](compounding.md))
- Some struggles are growth; some are signals to pivot
- Distinguish temporary setbacks from structural problems

**Don't pivot too slowly:**
- Sunk cost fallacy: "I've invested so much in Plan A"
- The world doesn't care about your sunk costs
- Clinging to a failing Plan A is career death

## Risk vs. Volatility

[Reid Hoffman](../people/reid-hoffman.md) distinguishes:
- **Volatility:** Short-term ups and downs, unpredictability
- **Risk:** Permanent loss of capital (career capital in this case)

**Insight:** Take on more **volatility** (trying new things, changing jobs, moving cities) early in your career when you can absorb it. Plan Z gives you the safety net to handle volatility without risking permanent career death.

## ABZ in Action: A Career Example

**Example: Software Engineer**

**Plan A:** Staff engineer at BigCo, building infrastructure
- **Hypothesis:** Deep technical skills + BigCo brand → high career capital
- **Data collection:** Am I still learning? Is this valued externally? Am I energized?

**Plan B:** Technical founder at startup
- **Kept warm:** Side project building SaaS product, angel investing, networking with founders, attending startup events
- **Pivot trigger:** If Plan A stagnates or side project gets traction

**Plan Z:** Freelance consulting as senior engineer
- **Fallback:** $150/hr consulting, enough to cover expenses, buys time to regroup
- **Safety net:** 12 months savings + ability to get consulting clients quickly

**Result:** Knowing Plan Z exists, engineer can take calculated risks with Plan A (negotiate aggressively, take on hard projects, ship side project publicly). If Plan A fails or stagnates, pivot to Plan B. If both fail, retreat to Plan Z and relaunch.

## Key Insight

**The biggest career mistake is having only Plan A.** The world is too uncertain, too fast-changing. You need:
- **Plan A:** Where you're going
- **Plan B:** Where you might go
- **Plan Z:** Where you land if A and B fail

This isn't pessimism. It's intelligent risk management. ABZ planning lets you take **bigger, smarter risks** because you've thought through the downside.
