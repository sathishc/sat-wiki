# Happiness Formula: H = S + C + V

## One-Line Definition

Haidt's synthesis of wellbeing research into three components: **Set point** (genetic baseline) + **Conditions** (life circumstances) + **Voluntary activities** (what you choose to do).

## The Formula

**H = S + C + V**

### S — Set Point (~50% of variance)

Your genetic baseline for happiness. Twin studies show that about half the variation in long-run happiness is heritable — some people are naturally more positive, resilient, or content than others.

**Key insight:** The set point is remarkably stable. Lottery winners return to their baseline within a year or two. So do accident victims who become paraplegic (though it takes longer). This is **hedonic adaptation** — the mind recalibrates to circumstances with surprising speed.

**Implication:** Chasing happiness through achievement is largely ineffective because you adapt to each new level. The set point reasserts itself.

**Can it change?** Slightly, over long periods, through deliberate practice — especially meditation. But it's the lowest-[Leverage](leverage.md) lever.

---

### C — Conditions (~10–15% of variance)

The actual circumstances of your life. Haidt's research summary:

| Condition | Effect on happiness |
|---|---|
| **Noise / long commute** | Significant negative effect — hard to adapt to |
| **Lack of autonomy / control** | Significant negative effect |
| **Chronic illness or pain** | Significant negative effect — hard to adapt to |
| **Close relationships** | Strong positive effect — most important condition |
| **Income** (above poverty line) | Surprisingly small effect; diminishes rapidly above ~$75k (Kahneman's original finding; revised upward by Killingsworth 2021, but still with diminishing returns) |
| **Marriage / stable partnership** | Moderate positive effect; quality matters more than status |
| **Short commute / walkable neighbourhood** | Underrated positive effect |

**The adaptation asymmetry:** We adapt quickly to *gains* (new job, new house) and more slowly to *losses* and *aversives* (noise, bad commute, painful relationships). This means conditions you suffer through daily matter more than one-time achievements.

**Most underrated condition:** **Quality of close relationships.** This is the most consistent finding across happiness research — more predictive than income, achievement, or almost anything else. The Harvard Study of Adult Development (75 years) concludes: good relationships keep us happier and healthier. Period.

---

### V — Voluntary Activities (high leverage)

What you choose to do with your time and attention. These are the highest-leverage variables because:
1. You can change them directly
2. Adaptation is slower than for conditions
3. The right activities compound

**Highest-return voluntary activities (evidence-based):**

| Activity | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Meditation / mindfulness** | Trains attention; reduces reactivity; raises set point over time |
| **Physical exercise** | Strongest single intervention; immediate and long-term mood effects |
| **Acts of kindness** | Reliably produces wellbeing; "doing good feels good" is empirically robust |
| **Gratitude practice** | Redirects attention from what's missing to what's present |
| **Flow activities** | Absorbed challenge; [Csikszentmihalyi](../people/mihaly-csikszentmihalyi.md); highest-quality positive experience |
| **Nurturing close relationships** | Active investment in the highest-value condition |
| **Purposeful work** | Engagement + meaning → both hedonic and eudaimonic wellbeing |

## The Critical Insight: We Mispredict

We are systematically wrong about what will make us happy:
- We overweight income, status, and achievement (fast adaptation)
- We underweight relationships, commute, and chronic conditions (slow adaptation)
- We overweight future achievements and underweight present engagement

This is **affective forecasting error** — we imagine how we'll feel in a future state and we're consistently wrong in predictable directions.
