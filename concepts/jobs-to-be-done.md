# Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

The most influential concept from [Clayton Christensen's](../people/clayton-christensen.md) *The Innovator's Solution* (2003). Customers don't buy products — they **hire** products (and services, and people) to do a *job* they need done.

> "People don't want a quarter-inch drill. They want a quarter-inch hole." — Theodore Levitt

Christensen extended this: the job has functional, emotional, and social dimensions. Understanding the job — not the customer's demographics or stated preferences — is the most reliable basis for innovation.

## The Core Insight

Traditional market research segments customers by demographics, psychographics, or product attributes. JTBD reframes the unit of analysis: what *situation* is the customer in, what *progress* are they trying to make, and what are they currently using (or not using) to make that progress?

A product gets **hired** when it does the job better than the alternative (including doing nothing). It gets **fired** when something else does the job better.

## The McDonald's Milkshake Study

Christensen's canonical example. McDonald's wanted to improve milkshake sales. Conventional research (segment by demographics, ask customers what they want) produced nothing useful.

A different question: *What job are customers hiring a milkshake to do?*

Observation revealed two completely different jobs:
- **Morning commuters** hired the milkshake to make a long, boring drive go faster. Needs: one-handed, lasts the whole commute, slowly satisfying, doesn't make them hungry before lunch. Competition: bagels (messy), bananas (gone too fast), coffee.
- **Afternoon parents** hired milkshakes as a treat for their kids. Needs: child-sized, fast, fun. Competition: candy, other treats.

Same product. Same demographic (McDonald's customer). Entirely different jobs — requiring completely different product improvements.

Asking customers "what do you want in a milkshake?" would never surface this. Observing the job does.

## The Three Dimensions of a Job

| Dimension | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Functional** | The practical task to be done (commute without hunger, drill a hole) |
| **Emotional** | How the customer wants to *feel* while doing it (confident, calm, excited) |
| **Social** | How the customer wants to be *perceived* by others (competent, caring, innovative) |

Strong products address all three. Most product development focuses only on functional. Emotional and social dimensions often determine which product actually gets hired.

## Why JTBD Beats Demographics

Demographics tell you *who* is buying. Jobs tell you *why*. The same person can hire completely different products for the same job on different occasions — or hire the same product for completely different jobs.

This means:
- Competitor analysis should map *jobs*, not product categories (your real competitor for the morning milkshake is a banana, not a smoothie)
- Innovation should start with the job, not the customer profile
- Segmentation by job is more predictive than segmentation by age, income, or even stated preference

## Jobs to Be Done in Hiring

Christensen extends JTBD to management: when you hire someone, ask what *job* you are hiring them for. Not just the role description — what specific challenge, at this specific moment, in this specific organisation, needs to be solved?

Then look for candidates whose *experiences* (not just credentials) match that job. Experience builds capability in a way that credentials cannot.

This also applies to strategy: before building, buying, or partnering, ask — what job is this capability being hired to do?

## JTBD and Disruption

JTBD is the diagnostic tool that makes [disruptive innovation](./innovators-dilemma.md) legible from the customer side.

Disruptors succeed because incumbents define the job incorrectly — they optimise for what existing customers *say* they want (sustaining innovation), not for the jobs that non-consumers and low-end users need done. The disruptor enters by doing the right job for an ignored population, not by doing the incumbent's job better.

## Applications

- **Product development:** Design for the job, not the user persona
- **Marketing:** Communicate how the product does the job, not its features
- **Strategy:** Identify non-consumption by finding jobs with no good current hire
- **Career:** Understand what job your employer is actually hiring you to do; what job your manager needs solved
