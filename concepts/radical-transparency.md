# Radical Transparency

One of [Ray Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)'s two foundational operating principles (alongside radical truth — see [Ray Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)). The practice of making information, reasoning, and feedback fully visible across an organisation — rather than filtering it through hierarchy.

## What It Means

- All meetings at Bridgewater are recorded and available to all employees
- Performance reviews, disagreements, and critiques are made explicit and shared
- Reasoning behind decisions is surfaced, not just the decisions themselves
- Problems are named openly rather than managed privately

## Why It Works

Dalio's premise: **bad decisions come from distorted information**. Hierarchy, politics, and social niceties all distort information before it reaches decision-makers. Radical transparency eliminates the distortion at the source.

> "Operate by principles that are so clearly laid out that their logic can easily be assessed and you and others can see if you walk the talk."

## The Psychological Cost

Radical transparency is uncomfortable. Dalio acknowledges it feels brutal to new Bridgewater employees. Being told bluntly that your reasoning was poor, in front of your colleagues, runs against most social norms.

His argument: the discomfort is the point. The alternative — polite silence, filtered feedback, avoided conversations — is far more costly in the long run. You trade short-term comfort for long-term ignorance.

## Contrast with Naval's Approach

[Naval Ravikant](../people/naval-ravikant.md) reaches a related conclusion from a different direction: **[specific knowledge](./specific-knowledge.md) cannot be faked**. Attempting to appear knowledgeable rather than be knowledgeable is a losing strategy. Both approaches ultimately reward honest reckoning with reality over performance.

The difference: Naval's framework is individual; Dalio's is institutional. Radical transparency is an organisational technology for surfacing truth at scale.
