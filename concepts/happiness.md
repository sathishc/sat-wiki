# Happiness

[Naval Ravikant](../people/naval-ravikant.md)'s framework treats happiness not as something to pursue, but as a **default state** you return to by removing friction — mainly desires, anxiety, and the need for external validation.

## Core Thesis

> "Happiness is the state you're in when you have nothing missing."

This is a subtractive view: you don't add happiness by achieving things. You reveal it by dropping what covers it up.

## Happiness Is Learned

Happiness is a **skill**, not a trait. It can be practised and improved. The main practices:
- **Meditation** — trains the observer, creates distance from reactive thoughts
- **Presence** — the mind in the past suffers (depression); in the future, it anxieties; in the present, it is at peace
- **Acceptance** — not passive resignation, but clear-eyed acknowledgment of what is

## Every Desire Is a Chosen Unhappiness

Each desire is a signal to yourself that your current state is insufficient. The more desires, the more dissatisfaction by definition.

This does not mean wanting nothing. It means being selective and deliberate about which desires are worth the unhappiness they carry.

## Success Does Not Earn Happiness

Achieving a goal does not produce lasting happiness. The mind quickly resets and finds a new target. This is the **hedonic treadmill** — a well-documented phenomenon in psychology. Naval's response: stop tying your peace to outcomes you don't fully control.

## Envy Is the Enemy

Envy is uniquely toxic because it offers no upside — you feel bad without gaining anything. It is a comparison function that always finds someone "ahead." The solution is not to stop comparing entirely but to find reference points where comparison breaks down (people who made fundamentally different trade-offs).
