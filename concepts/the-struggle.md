# The Struggle

> "The Struggle is when you wonder why you started the company in the first place. The Struggle is when people ask you why you don't quit and you don't know the answer." — Ben Horowitz

## One-Line Definition

Horowitz's name for the psychological reality of being a CEO in genuine crisis — the dark night of the soul that nearly every founder experiences and that no one talks about honestly.

## What It Is

The Struggle is not a phase you pass through and graduate from. It is an ongoing condition of building companies under uncertainty. It shows up as:

- No good options — every choice is bad in some way
- Everyone depends on you to have the answer, and you don't
- Isolation — you can't fully share the burden with your board, your team, or your family
- Doubt — not just tactical uncertainty, but existential ("why am I doing this?")
- Self-criticism that consumes energy you need for the actual work

## Why Horowitz Talks About It

Most CEO memoirs are written in hindsight, after success. They compress or omit the darkness. This creates **survivorship bias** in the cultural narrative around startups:

- Founders assume they're uniquely bad at this
- They feel shame about the psychological difficulty
- They don't seek help or acknowledge the struggle to their teams

Horowitz's insight: **naming the Struggle normalises it.** You're not broken. Nearly every founder of every significant company has been here. Knowing that helps.

## How to Get Through It

Horowitz's advice (notably not motivational platitudes):

1. **Don't put it all on yourself** — the team built this problem together; they can help solve it
2. **Play long enough to get lucky** — a bad day is not the end; survive long enough for the environment to change
3. **Don't take the "too hard" exit** — selling cheap or quitting early are permanent decisions made in temporary pain
4. **Focus on the move, not the game** — you can't see the whole game. Just find the next move.
5. **Talk about it** — with a trusted board member, an executive coach, a fellow founder. Not with your team; not in a way that destabilises. But don't be alone in it.
