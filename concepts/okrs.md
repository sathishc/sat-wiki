# OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

A goal-setting framework developed by Andy Grove at Intel in the 1970s, popularised by [John Doerr](../people/john-doerr.md) and widely adopted across the technology industry after Google implemented it in 1999.

OKRs separate two things that most goal-setting conflates:
- **Objective** — *what* you want to achieve (qualitative, inspirational, directional)
- **Key Results** — *how* you will know you achieved it (quantitative, measurable, time-bound)

> "An OBJECTIVE is simply WHAT is to be achieved. KEY RESULTS benchmark and monitor HOW we get to the objective. Objectives are significant, concrete, action-oriented, and (ideally) inspirational. Key Results are aggressive yet realistic, measurable and verifiable."

---

## The Components

### Objectives

An Objective describes a destination — where you want to go. It should be:
- **Qualitative** — directional, not numeric
- **Significant** — not business-as-usual; it should matter
- **Action-oriented** — strong verbs; creates momentum
- **Time-bound** — typically quarterly
- **Aspirational** — a good objective creates mild discomfort; it stretches

❌ Weak: "Improve user satisfaction"
✅ Strong: "Build a product users love and can't stop talking about"

### Key Results

Key Results are the measurable waypoints that define whether the Objective was achieved. They should be:
- **Quantitative** — numeric or binary (done/not done); no ambiguity
- **Outcomes, not activities** — "Launch the feature" is a task. "30% of users activate the feature within 7 days" is a Key Result.
- **Aggressive but realistic** — not trivially achievable; not fantasy
- **3–5 per Objective** — enough to define success; not so many that focus is lost

❌ Activity: "Conduct 20 customer interviews"
✅ Outcome: "Identify 3 validated customer pain points with >70% interview confirmation"

### The Pairing Discipline

The Objective without Key Results is vague ambition. Key Results without an Objective is metric-gaming. Together they enforce both inspiration and rigour.

## The Four Superpowers

### 1. Focus
OKRs require choosing. The constraint — typically 3–5 Objectives, 3–5 KRs each — forces prioritisation. You cannot have twenty OKRs and claim to be focused. The act of writing OKRs is the act of deciding what matters and what doesn't.

> "If we tried to focus on everything, we focus on nothing." — Andy Grove

### 2. Alignment
OKRs are typically made **public across the organisation**. Everyone can see what everyone else is working toward — including senior leaders. This creates:
- **Vertical alignment** — individual OKRs nest into team OKRs, which nest into company OKRs
- **Horizontal alignment** — teams with related OKRs can find each other and coordinate

Doerr recommends ~60% bottom-up (teams set their own OKRs) and ~40% top-down (leadership defines company-level OKRs). Pure top-down cascades destroy ownership; pure bottom-up loses coherence.

### 3. Tracking
OKRs are reviewed regularly — weekly check-ins, mid-cycle assessments, end-of-cycle scoring. Key Results are scored 0.0–1.0:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0.7–1.0 | Delivered |
| 0.4–0.6 | Progress, incomplete |
| 0.0–0.3 | Failed to advance |

A consistent 1.0 across all OKRs is a warning sign — the objectives weren't ambitious enough.

**Lead vs. lag:** OKR tracking emphasises **lead measures** (KR progress week-to-week) over **lag measures** (end-of-cycle outcomes). This enables course correction before it's too late — the same principle Reinertsen applies to [Cost of Delay](./cost-of-delay.md).

### 4. Stretching (Moonshots)
OKRs distinguish between two types:

| | Committed OKRs | Aspirational / Moonshot OKRs |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Operational; must be achieved | Strategic; 10x ambition |
| Expected score | 1.0 | ~0.7 |
| Failure consequence | Serious | Acceptable; expected |
| Example | "Ship v2.0 by March 31" | "Become the default ML platform for Fortune 500" |

The moonshot distinction is critical. If all OKRs are evaluated pass/fail, people set conservative targets — sandbagging to protect their score. Moonshot OKRs make bold ambition safe to declare.

## CFRs: The Companion Practice

OKRs work best alongside **CFRs** — Conversations, Feedback, Recognition:

- **Conversations** — regular 1:1s focused on OKR progress, blockers, and development
- **Feedback** — continuous, specific, timely (not annual reviews)
- **Recognition** — peer and manager acknowledgment of OKR contributions

CFRs are the human operating system that OKRs run on. OKRs without CFRs become mechanical and disconnected. CFRs without OKRs lose direction. Together they create a culture of continuous performance.

## OKRs vs. KPIs

| | OKRs | KPIs |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Direction + stretch | Health monitoring |
| **Timeframe** | Quarterly cycles | Ongoing / always-on |
| **Scope** | Change and progress | Steady-state operations |
| **Ambition** | Aspirational | Operational |
| **Acceptable failure** | Yes (moonshots) | No |

KPIs are the dials on the dashboard — they tell you if the engine is healthy. OKRs are the destination on the GPS — they tell you where you're heading. Both are needed; they answer different questions.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too many OKRs | Fear of leaving something out | Hard cap: 3–5 objectives maximum |
| Tasks as Key Results | Easier to write than outcomes | Ask: "Does this measure a result or describe an activity?" |
| Set-and-forget | No review cadence | Weekly check-ins; mid-cycle review mandatory |
| Sandbagging | OKRs tied to performance reviews | Separate OKRs from comp/perf entirely |
| Top-down-only cascade | Leaders write everyone's OKRs | 60% bottom-up; teams own their OKRs |
| Disconnected OKRs | No visibility across teams | Public OKRs; explicit alignment check |
| Gaming Key Results | Pressure to hit 1.0 on everything | Distinguish committed vs moonshot OKRs |

## Cross-Framework Connections

### ↔ Dalio (Five-Step Process)
Dalio's [Five-Step Process](./five-step-process.md) is OKRs expressed as a personal operating system. Step 1 (set clear goals) = the Objective. Step 2 (identify problems) + Step 3 (diagnose) = the tracking and review cadence. Step 4 (design a plan) = the Key Results. Step 5 (execute) = the weekly check-in discipline.

Both frameworks insist on ruthless honesty between ambition and reality — neither allows comfortable self-deception about progress.

### ↔ Reinertsen (Cost of Delay / Lead Measures)
Reinertsen's [Cost of Delay](./cost-of-delay.md) is the economic consequence of not having OKRs — or of having them but not tracking them. Misaligned priorities accumulate invisible delay costs. OKR tracking makes the lead measures visible before the lag measure (missed outcome) becomes undeniable.

Reinertsen's [WIP Limits](./wip-limits.md) are the operational expression of OKR focus — both say: constrain the number of things being pursued simultaneously to maximise quality and throughput.

### ↔ Newport (Deep Work / 4DX)
Newport's 4DX framework (*The 4 Disciplines of Execution*, which he applies to deep work) is an individual-level OKR system:
- Focus on wildly important goals = Objective
- Act on lead measures = track deep work hours (KR)
- Keep a compelling scoreboard = OKR tracking board
- Cadence of accountability = weekly review

OKRs operate at the team/organisation level; Newport's 4DX operates at the individual level. Same structure, different scope.

### ↔ Christensen (Emergent vs. Deliberate Strategy)
Christensen's warning about emergent vs. deliberate strategy maps onto OKR cycle design:
- **Early-stage / discovery:** Short cycles (monthly), more aspirational OKRs, looser key results — leave room for emergent learning
- **Execution-stage:** Quarterly cycles, mix of committed and moonshot OKRs
- **Steady-state:** Longer-horizon OKRs for strategic direction; KPIs for operational health

OKRs should be more deliberate as the strategy becomes more validated. Forcing committed OKRs too early locks in assumptions before you have data.

### ↔ Cialdini (Commitment and Consistency)
Public OKRs are deliberate **commitment devices** — they leverage [Commitment and Consistency](./commitment-and-consistency.md). Declaring a goal publicly to your organisation increases the psychological cost of abandoning it. OKR transparency is not just an alignment mechanism; it's a motivation mechanism. The social visibility creates accountability that internal resolve often cannot.

### ↔ Strategic Intent (Prahalad)
[Strategic Intent](./strategic-intent.md) is the long-range, aspirational Objective — the company OKR set at a 3–5 year horizon. Prahalad's "Encircle Caterpillar" (Komatsu) and "Beat Xerox" (Canon) are strategic intent statements that would translate directly into annual and quarterly OKRs at lower levels. OKRs are the execution cadence for a strategic intent.

### ↔ Flow Economics (Reinertsen)
[Flow Economics](./flow-economics.md) asks: what is the economic value of delivering this work sooner? OKRs answer: these are the things delivering the most economic value — focus here. Without OKRs, teams optimise for utilisation or comfort; with OKRs, they optimise for the outcomes that matter most.
