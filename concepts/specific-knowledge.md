# Specific Knowledge

A concept central to [Naval Ravikant](../people/naval-ravikant.md)'s framework for building wealth. Specific knowledge is what makes you irreplaceable — and therefore, highly valuable.

## Definition

Specific knowledge is knowledge that:
- **Cannot be taught** — if it can be taught in school or a training course, it can be commoditised and arbitraged away
- **Is built from genuine curiosity** — it arises from following what genuinely interests you, not chasing market demand
- **Looks like play to you, work to others** — the signal that you are on the right track
- **Lives at the frontier** — built through primary sources, direct experience, and experimentation

## Why It Matters

In a world of commoditised labour (anything trainable can be automated or offshored), the only durable source of income is something uniquely yours. Specific knowledge cannot be replicated because it is inseparable from your specific combination of interests, experiences, and dispositions.

## How to Find It

1. Look at what you obsess over without external reward
2. Find the intersection of your curiosity and what the world values
3. Build it through practice, not study — reading is secondary to doing
4. Let it compound over time — specific knowledge gets more specific and more valuable

## Examples

- A self-taught programmer who built tools for their own problems
- A writer who developed a unique voice through years of honest observation
- A trader whose intuition was built through thousands of hours of pattern recognition
