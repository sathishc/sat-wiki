# Mental Models

Frameworks for understanding how the world works. A good mental model lets you map a new situation onto a known pattern and reason about it quickly. Multiple mental models applied together reduce the chance of blind spots.

## Why They Matter

Without mental models, every new situation must be reasoned from scratch. With them, you pattern-match to a known structure and ask: does this situation fit? What does the model predict?

> "To see things from the top down is the best way to understand. Find the one code/law that drives them all."
> — [Ray Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)

Naval Ravikant frames it similarly: **collect mental models from across disciplines**. The person who only knows one framework (economics, say) will force every problem into that shape. The person with 20 frameworks can pick the right one.

## Dalio's Approach

Dalio treats mental models as **algorithms**: explicit, back-testable, refinable. When you make a decision, write down the reasoning. When the outcome arrives, compare. If your model was wrong, update it. Over time this produces a personal library of tested principles.

Key models Dalio uses:
- **The Machine Model** — organisations are machines with people and design as parts. When output fails, trace back to the broken part.
- **Higher-Level Thinking** — step back from the situation and observe yourself within it. What type of situation is this? What pattern does it match?
- **Two-Loop Thinking** — first loop: what happened and why? Second loop: why did I think what I thought?

## Naval's Approach

Naval recommends building a broad base from **foundational disciplines**:
- Mathematics (logic, probability)
- Physics (how reality works at a base level)
- Microeconomics (incentives, game theory)
- Evolution (how complex systems adapt)
- Psychology (how humans actually behave, not how they say they do)

The goal is not to memorise models but to internalise them — so they operate automatically when you encounter the right situation.

## The Risk of Over-Reliance

Dalio warns against **confusing rationalising with rational**. A mental model can become a filter that distorts reality rather than reveals it — especially if you're attached to a conclusion before you've done the analysis.

> "Don't get hung up on your views of how things should be because you will miss out on learning how they really are."
