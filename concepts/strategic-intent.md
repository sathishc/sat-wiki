# Strategic Intent

Introduced by [Prahalad](../people/ck-prahalad.md) and Hamel in their 1989 HBR article. Strategic intent is an **obsessive, long-term ambition** that stretches far beyond current capabilities — and creates the energy, focus, and urgency to build those capabilities over time.

> "Strategic intent captures the essence of winning. It is stable over time. It sets a target that deserves personal effort and commitment."

## The Three Attributes

Strategic intent has three characteristics:

1. **Direction** — a clear sense of where the organisation wants to go; an unambiguous destination
2. **Discovery** — the journey itself will require invention; you don't yet know exactly how you'll get there
3. **Destiny** — a personal and emotional commitment; it's not just a goal, it's a cause

The classic examples cited by Prahalad and Hamel:
- **Komatsu** in the 1970s: "Encircle Caterpillar" — a simple, obsessive goal to overtake the world's dominant construction equipment maker
- **Canon**: "Beat Xerox" — Canon had 1/100th of Xerox's resources, but the intent focused all capabilities on the target
- **Honda**: "We will destroy Yamaha" — aggressive, specific, energising

## Strategic Intent vs Strategic Plan

| Strategic Plan | Strategic Intent |
|---|---|
| Detailed, near-term | Ambitious, long-term |
| Matches resources to opportunities | Creates a gap between ambition and resources |
| Defensive (protect what you have) | Offensive (capture what you want) |
| Reactive to competitive landscape | Reshapes the competitive landscape |
| Loses relevance as environment changes | Stable through environmental change |

The plan optimises from current position. The intent sets a destination so ambitious it forces continuous innovation to close the gap.

## The Stretch Goal

The power of strategic intent lies in the **gap it creates**. When ambition exceeds current resources, the organisation must:
- Innovate to find asymmetric approaches (do more with less)
- Build new [core competencies](./core-competency.md)
- Find creative alliances and partnerships
- Motivate people to transcend their current assumptions

A goal that can be achieved with current resources is not strategic intent — it is a plan. Strategic intent requires a leap.

## Cross-References

**[Naval Ravikant](../people/naval-ravikant.md)'s [Think Big](../concepts/amazon-leadership-principles.md):**
Naval's emphasis on choosing career paths with high leverage and ceiling is individual strategic intent — setting an ambition so large it forces capability development. Amazon LP #8 "Think Big" is the same principle institutionalised.

**[Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)'s [Five-Step Process](./five-step-process.md):**
Step 1 of Dalio's process is "set clear goals." Strategic intent is the version of this at corporate/industry scale — the audacious goal that precedes everything else.

**[Competing for the Future](./competing-for-the-future.md):**
Strategic intent is the motivational engine; competing for the future is the strategic framework. You need foresight (where the future is going) + intent (the burning ambition to get there first).

**[Bezos](../people/jeff-bezos.md):**
"The most customer-centric company on Earth" is strategic intent in Prahalad's sense. It's not a plan — it's an obsessive ambition that reshapes every decision and forces continuous innovation.

**[Kahneman](../people/daniel-kahneman.md)'s contrast:**
Strategic intent is deliberately [System 2](./system-1-and-2.md) — it overrides System 1's comfort with the status quo and forces deliberate thinking about a future that is hard to visualise. The [planning fallacy](./planning-fallacy.md) is the enemy of strategic intent: detailed inside-view planning replaces ambitious outside-view foresight.
