# Intrinsic Motivation

## One-Line Definition

Motivation that comes from within — driven by genuine interest, curiosity, or satisfaction in the work itself — rather than from external rewards like pay, status, or recognition.

## The Deci & Ryan Foundation

The concept is most rigorously grounded in **Self-Determination Theory** (Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, 1985), which identifies three core psychological needs that drive intrinsic motivation:

| Need | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Autonomy** | The sense that you are the author of your actions; doing something because you choose to |
| **Competence** | The feeling of being effective and growing in your capabilities |
| **Relatedness** | Connection to others; doing work that matters to people you care about |

When these three needs are met, intrinsic motivation emerges naturally. External rewards, if poorly structured, can *undermine* intrinsic motivation (the "overjustification effect").

## In Goleman's EQ Model

Goleman identified motivation as the **third domain of Emotional Intelligence**, noting that high-EQ individuals show:
- A drive to achieve for the sake of achievement, not for external recognition
- Persistence in the face of failure and frustration
- Optimism — a tendency to attribute setbacks to manageable causes, not permanent ones
- Long-horizon thinking about goals

This maps closely to Deci & Ryan's autonomy and competence needs.

## In Christensen's Work

In *How Will You Measure Your Life?*, Christensen draws directly on Frederick Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory:
- **Hygiene factors** (salary, status, job security) prevent dissatisfaction but do not create satisfaction
- **Motivators** (challenge, recognition, growth, the work itself) are intrinsic — they create genuine engagement

The practical lesson: if you're managing for hygiene factors (pay, title, perks), you can't create a motivated team. You can only remove dissatisfaction. Genuine engagement requires intrinsic motivators.
