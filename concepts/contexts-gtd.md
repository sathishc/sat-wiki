# Contexts (GTD)

In [GTD](./gtd-workflow.md), a **context** is the tool, location, or condition required to execute a next action. Next Actions are organised by context, not by project.

## Classic Contexts

Allen's original examples were physical:
- `@phone` — needs a phone
- `@computer` — needs a computer
- `@errands` — tasks done while out and about
- `@office` — requires being at the office
- `@home` — requires being at home
- `@agenda: [person]` — items to discuss with a specific person when you meet

## Why Context Matters

On any given day, your situation — location, tool, energy, available time — constrains what you can do. Context-based lists let you:
- **Batch similar work**: all calls in one burst, all errands in one trip
- **Opportunistically execute**: when you find yourself near a phone, scan `@phone` and clear the list
- **Match task to energy**: when low-energy, scan `@low-focus`; when in flow, scan `@deep-work`

A project-based to-do list doesn't help you answer "what can I do *right now* given that I'm in a taxi with 10 minutes?" A context-based list does.

## Modern Adaptations

In a world where most knowledge work happens at a computer, location-based contexts lose some value. Modern GTD practitioners often use:
- **Energy-based**: `@high-energy`, `@low-focus`, `@braindead`
- **Application-based**: `@email`, `@code`, `@writing`, `@reading`
- **Time-based**: `@15-min`, `@1-hour`
- **Collaboration-based**: `@agenda-[name]` (retained from original)

The principle is unchanged: contexts make the right next action visible at the right moment.

## Contexts vs. Projects

A common GTD confusion: mixing contexts and projects. In GTD:
- **Project list** — all desired multi-step outcomes (the "what")
- **Next Actions lists by context** — physical actions, sorted by situation (the "how/when")

Each project lives on the Projects list. Its current next action lives on a context list. This separation is what prevents the classic "I have 50 projects open and I don't know what to do" paralysis.
