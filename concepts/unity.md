# Unity

The seventh and newest of [Cialdini's](../people/robert-cialdini.md) [principles of influence](./principles-of-influence.md), added in the 2021 expanded edition. We are strongly influenced by people we perceive as *us* — members of our in-group, our tribe, our family. Unity goes beyond [Liking](./liking.md) (which is about the individual) — it is about **shared identity**.

## Liking vs. Unity

| | Liking | Unity |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | The individual person | The group / identity |
| Trigger | Similarity, familiarity, attractiveness | Shared identity: kin, place, experience, co-creation |
| Depth | I like this person | We are the same kind of person |
| Durability | Situational | Identity-level; more durable |

Unity is more powerful than liking because it operates at the level of self-concept. "One of us" triggers obligations and generosity that "someone I like" does not.

## Sources of Unity

**Kinship and family:** The most powerful unity trigger. We extend resources, effort, and forgiveness to family members we'd withhold from strangers — even when the family bond is tenuous.

**Shared place:** Hometown, neighbourhood, alma mater, nation. "Oh, you're from Bangalore too?" triggers immediate in-group warmth with a stranger.

**Shared experience:** Having gone through the same thing — military service, a difficult project, a disaster, an intense training program. Shared adversity creates some of the strongest unity bonds.

**Co-creation:** Being involved in making something together. When someone participates in creating a decision, product, or plan, they feel ownership and investment in its success.

## The "Ask for Advice" Technique

The most practically powerful application of Unity. Asking someone for their *advice* — not their opinion, not their help, their *advice* — transforms the relationship from target to collaborator.

When you ask for advice:
- The other person feels respected and valued (not just useful)
- They become cognitively invested in your success (they've contributed)
- A sense of "we" is created through the consultation act itself
- They are more likely to support the outcome because they shaped it

This is why smart negotiators and leaders ask for input before announcing decisions rather than after.

## Brand and Tribal Identity

Unity explains why strong brands create communities, not just customers:
- Apple users don't just prefer Macs — they identify as "Apple people"
- CrossFit members don't just exercise — they belong to a tribe with shared language, rituals, and values
- Political affiliation: "one of us vs. one of them" is the most reliable compliance trigger in mass persuasion

The brand or movement creates a shared identity that makes compliance feel like loyalty rather than persuasion.

## Cross-references

**↔ [Social Proof](./social-proof.md)**
Social proof works through *similarity* — we follow similar others. Unity is the amplified version: we follow *us*. The similarity effect in social proof is the mild version of Unity's in-group identity trigger. When social proof and Unity align (peers in our in-group are doing something), compliance is maximised.

**↔ [Black Swan](./black-swan.md) / [Antifragility](./antifragility.md) (Taleb)**
Tribal in-group thinking is one of the fragility generators Taleb identifies. Groups that make decisions based on in-group loyalty rather than merit create correlated error — everyone in the tribe is wrong in the same direction at the same time. Unity is a genuine social good; Unity as a decision filter is a fragility.

**↔ [Idea Meritocracy](./idea-meritocracy.md) (Dalio)**
Bridgewater's explicit goal is to override in-group loyalty in decision-making. "Who said it" (Unity + Liking + Authority) is systematically separated from "how good the idea is." The believability-weighted system is a Unity antidote: your in-group membership gets you no special weight.

## Defence

Recognise when "we" is being manufactured. Shared identity is genuine and valuable in relationships; it is exploitable in commercial and political contexts. Ask: is this sense of "we" real and relevant to this decision, or is it being invoked to bypass independent evaluation?
