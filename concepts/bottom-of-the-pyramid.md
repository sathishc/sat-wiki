# Bottom of the Pyramid

A concept popularised by [C.K. Prahalad](../people/ck-prahalad.md) in his 2004 book *The Fortune at the Bottom of the Pyramid*. The argument: the ~4 billion people living on less than $2.50 a day are not just aid recipients — they are a massive, underserved **market** AND a source of breakthrough **innovation**.

> "Stop thinking of the poor as victims or as a burden, and start recognising them as resilient and creative entrepreneurs and value-conscious consumers."

## The Core Idea

The global wealth pyramid has:
- **Top** (~100 million people): annual income >$20,000
- **Middle** (~2 billion): $2,000–$20,000
- **Bottom** (~4 billion): <$2,000 / year, often earning less than $2/day

Conventional business strategy targets the top and middle. Prahalad argued this was both a missed opportunity and a false dichotomy between profit and social good.

## Why the BOP Is a Real Market

The poor are not price-insensitive — they are **highly** price-sensitive, but they do buy. They often pay *more* per unit than the wealthy, because:
- They can't afford to buy in bulk
- They lack access to formal financial services
- They pay monopoly prices to local intermediaries

**The "poverty penalty":** A poor family in Mumbai may pay:
- 10x more per litre for safe drinking water than a middle-class family with piped supply
- 20x more per unit for electricity from a generator vs the grid
- Enormous % of income in interest to informal moneylenders vs bank rates

Prahalad's argument: eliminate the poverty penalty through scale, appropriate product design, and distribution innovation — and you have a massive market.

## The BOP as Innovation Lab

Companies that serve BOP markets are forced to innovate in ways that create value everywhere:

- **Hindustan Unilever** developed single-use sachets of shampoo and detergent for daily wage workers — this "sachet innovation" then spread to premium markets globally
- **ITC's e-Choupal** built a direct digital supply chain from farmers to market — eliminating intermediaries and raising farmer incomes while reducing ITC's procurement costs
- **Jaipur Foot** prosthetic limb, designed for India's roads and culture: now among the most used prosthetics globally, by volume
- **Microfinance** (Grameen Bank): demonstrated that the poor are creditworthy, transforming global finance

## The Debate

Prahalad's thesis has been challenged:
- **Aneel Karnani** (University of Michigan): "there is no fortune at the BOP" — the market is too fragmented and marginal for most multinationals
- Critics argue the primary impact should be on poor as **producers** (jobs, wages) rather than as **consumers**
- Multinational entry sometimes disrupts local micro-entrepreneurs rather than helping them

Prahalad acknowledged these tensions. His later work emphasised co-creation with BOP communities rather than selling to them.

## Connection to the Wider Wiki

**[Naval](../people/naval-ravikant.md)'s [Leverage](../concepts/leverage.md):**
BOP innovation often creates permissionless leverage — sachet models, mobile banking, and digital platforms scale at near-zero marginal cost, exactly the leverage Naval describes.

**[Reinertsen](../people/donald-reinertsen.md)'s [Batch Size](../concepts/batch-size.md):**
The sachet model is literally radical batch size reduction. Instead of selling a 500ml bottle (large batch), sell a 5ml sachet (tiny batch). Lower barrier to entry, faster feedback, reduced queue (the customer doesn't wait to accumulate enough money for the big pack).

**[Kahneman](../people/daniel-kahneman.md)'s [Availability Heuristic](../concepts/cognitive-biases-kahneman.md):**
Why did companies ignore BOP for so long? Cognitive bias — the BOP consumer is not easily recalled (not visible in executive experience), so the market size is systematically underestimated.

**[Hazlitt](../people/henry-hazlitt.md)'s [Seen vs Unseen](../concepts/seen-vs-unseen.md):**
The seen: fragmented, impoverished consumers. The unseen: the aggregate purchasing power of 4 billion people, the innovation catalysed, the network effects once infrastructure reaches them.
