# Room for Error

> "The most important part of every plan is planning on your plan not going according to plan." — Morgan Housel

## One-Line Definition

Build a margin of safety into every financial (and life) plan — not because you expect things to go wrong, but because things you don't expect will go wrong.

## The Argument

Financial models assume the future will be like a well-specified version of the past. It won't be. History is a guide to the *range* of outcomes, not a map of what will actually happen. The most important events of the next 30 years will be unprecedented.

This means: **the optimal plan for a predictable future is not the optimal plan for an unpredictable one.** Room for error is the structural acknowledgment that you don't know what's coming.

## What Room for Error Buys You

1. **Survival** — you stay in the game through unexpected events instead of being wiped out
2. **Optionality** — a buffer gives you choices when others have none (the 2008 investor who kept cash could buy at the bottom)
3. **Emotional stability** — knowing you have a buffer reduces the panic that triggers bad decisions
4. **Time** — room for error buys you more time, and time enables compounding

## Practical Applications

| Context | Room for Error means |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Emergency fund** | 6–12 months of expenses in cash; not for expected expenses — for the unexpected ones |
| **Investment allocation** | Less [Leverage](leverage.md), more liquidity than optimal models suggest |
| **Retirement planning** | Plan to need more, live longer, and earn less than your base case |
| **Business** | Runway of 18+ months, not 6; debt capacity held in reserve |
| **Career** | Develop skills that work in multiple scenarios, not just the current one |

## The Taleb Connection

This is Housel's accessible version of Taleb's [margin of safety / barbell strategy](../concepts/barbell-strategy.md). Taleb formalises it: maximum exposure to upside, structural protection from ruin. Housel operationalises it for everyday personal finance: just keep more buffer than you think you need.

The key shared insight: **the cost of room for error (slightly suboptimal returns in good times) is almost always worth the benefit (survival in bad times).**
