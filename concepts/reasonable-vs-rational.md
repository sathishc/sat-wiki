# Reasonable vs Rational

> "Aim to be mostly reasonable rather than coldly rational." — Morgan Housel

## One-Line Definition

In finance, a strategy that is *reasonable* (psychologically sustainable for you) beats a strategy that is *rational* (theoretically optimal) — because you'll actually stick to it.

## The Distinction

| | Rational | Reasonable |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis** | Mathematical optimisation | Human psychology and sustainability |
| **Question** | "What is theoretically best?" | "What can I actually do, consistently, for 30 years?" |
| **Example** | 100% global equities, never sell | 80% equities / 20% bonds; sell nothing in crashes |
| **Problem** | Correct in theory; abandoned in practice | Slightly suboptimal in theory; executed in practice |

## The Argument

Financial models can tell you the optimal asset allocation. They can prove that holding through every drawdown, never market timing, and maximising equity exposure produces the best long-run outcome.

The models are right. But they assume a robot, not a human.

**A strategy you abandon is worse than an imperfect strategy you maintain.** If your theoretically optimal portfolio causes you so much anxiety that you sell at the bottom of every bear market, you'd have been better off with a slightly less optimal but sustainable allocation.

> "You don't need to maximise returns. You need to achieve *your* goals with *your* risk tolerance. Those are different problems."

## Application

- Hold some cash even though it earns less — it prevents you from panic-selling stocks when markets crash
- Own your home even if renting + investing the difference is mathematically better — the psychological stability is real value
- Hold some home-country stocks even though global diversification is technically superior — familiarity reduces panic
- Invest in companies you understand even if the market says otherwise — you'll hold through volatility because you believe the story

## Connection to Kahneman

This is essentially Housel naming the gap between [System 2](../people/daniel-kahneman.md) (rational, deliberate) and [System 1](../people/daniel-kahneman.md) (emotional, fast) in a financial context. The optimal strategy is designed by System 2. It gets abandoned by System 1 under pressure. Reasonable strategies build in enough System 1 comfort to survive execution.
