# Habit Loop

The habit loop is the neurological pattern underlying every habit: a four-step cycle that the brain runs automatically once a behaviour becomes routine. James Clear's formulation in *Atomic Habits* extends Charles Duhigg's original **cue → routine → reward** model into a richer four-step loop.

## The Four Steps

```
Cue → Craving → Response → Reward
```

1. **Cue** — A trigger that initiates the habit. Anything that predicts a reward: a time, a place, an emotional state, other people, or a preceding action.
2. **Craving** — The motivational force. You don't crave the habit itself; you crave the change in state it delivers. Without craving, there is no reason to act.
3. **Response** — The actual habit — the thought or action performed. Complexity of the response is a function of your motivation and the friction involved.
4. **Reward** — The end goal of every habit. Rewards satisfy the craving and teach the brain which actions are worth remembering in future.

## The Four Laws of Behaviour Change

Each step maps to one of Clear's four laws — a practical design principle for building good habits:

| Step     | Law (building)         | Inversion (breaking)      |
|----------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Cue      | Make it obvious        | Make it invisible         |
| Craving  | Make it attractive     | Make it unattractive      |
| Response | Make it easy           | Make it difficult         |
| Reward   | Make it satisfying     | Make it unsatisfying      |

## Building vs Breaking

**Building a good habit:** Stack all four laws. Put the cue in plain sight (habit scorecard, implementation intention), pair it with something you enjoy (temptation bundling), reduce friction to near zero (two-minute rule, environment design), and reward yourself immediately.

**Breaking a bad habit:** Invert the laws. Remove or hide cues, reframe the craving negatively, add friction to the response, and eliminate the reward or replace it with an unsatisfying substitute.

## Origins

Duhigg's *The Power of Habit* (2012) introduced the three-part cue-routine-reward loop. Clear added **craving** as a distinct motivational step, drawing on BJ Fogg's work on motivation and on neuroscience showing that dopamine spikes at *anticipation* of reward, not just at receipt of it.
