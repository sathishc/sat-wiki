# Habit Stacking

Habit stacking is a technique for building new habits by anchoring them to existing ones using a structured **implementation intention**:

> "After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."

The existing habit acts as the cue for the new behaviour, creating a chain of linked routines.

## Why It Works

Implementation intentions exploit three mechanisms:

1. **Existing cues are already reliable** — An established habit fires automatically and predictably, making it an ideal trigger for a new one. You don't need to create a new cue from scratch.
2. **Specificity collapses decision costs** — "I will meditate for five minutes after I pour my morning coffee" requires zero in-the-moment decision-making. Vague intentions ("I'll meditate more") leave the brain to figure out when and where, and it usually doesn't.
3. **Temporal and spatial anchoring** — Research shows that specifying *when*, *where*, and *how* dramatically increases follow-through, even for people who are not highly motivated.

## Building Habit Stacks

Chains can be extended:
```
Wake up
  → Make coffee (existing habit)
  → Meditate 5 min (new habit)
  → Write in journal (new habit)
  → Review to-do list (new habit)
```

Key design principle: the frequency of the anchor habit must match the desired frequency of the new habit. Stacking a daily habit onto a weekly trigger won't produce daily behaviour.

## Environment Design

Habit stacking works best when the environment is also engineered: placing the journal next to the coffee maker makes the new behaviour obvious at exactly the right moment (see the *Make it obvious* law of the [Habit Loop](../concepts/habit-loop.md)).

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

### Donald Reinertsen — Reducing Friction
Reinertsen's work on flow in product development treats activation energy as a cost to be minimised. Habit stacking is the personal-behaviour equivalent: by attaching a new habit to an existing cue, you reduce the activation energy required to start. See [Donald Reinertsen](../people/donald-reinertsen.md).

### [Jonathan Haidt](../people/jonathan-haidt.md) — Shaping the Path
Haidt's metaphor of shaping the path for the elephant applies directly: habit stacking restructures the environment and sequence of events so the elephant (automatic behaviour) naturally arrives at the right action without requiring the rider (willpower) to intervene. See [Rider and Elephant](../concepts/rider-and-elephant.md).
