# Zero to One vs One to N

## One-Line Definition

**Zero to One** = creating something genuinely new; **One to N** = copying what already exists and scaling it.

## The Distinction

| | Zero to One | One to N |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Invention; vertical progress | Globalisation; horizontal progress |
| **Example** | Building the first typewriter | Making more typewriters |
| **Risk** | High (unknown unknowns) | Medium (known execution risk) |
| **Reward** | Monopoly rents if successful | Commodity margins |
| **Requires** | Contrarian insight (a secret) | Operational excellence |

Thiel's argument: most business advice is about One to N — how to compete, scale, and optimise. But the most valuable companies are built by doing Zero to One. *Copying a business model is not a startup strategy.*

## Why It Matters

Every time something new is created, it's a **singular event**. The next Mark Zuckerberg won't build a social network. The next Larry Page won't build a search engine. The next Gates won't build an operating system.

This is uncomfortable because:
- Zero to One requires a **secret** — a truth others don't see yet
- It requires tolerance for high-variance outcomes
- It requires resisting the temptation to benchmark against competitors

## Connection to Monopoly

Zero to One is the *path*; [Monopoly](../concepts/monopoly-vs-competition.md) is the *destination*. If you create something genuinely new and valuable, you escape competition entirely — at least temporarily. That's where the returns are.
