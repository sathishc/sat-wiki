# One-on-Ones

## One-Line Definition

Regular, structured one-to-one meetings between a manager and each direct report — the foundational tool of people management, running on the **employee's agenda**, not the manager's.

## The Horowitz Rule

> "The key to a good one-on-one is that it's the employee's meeting, not the manager's."

Most managers treat one-on-ones as status updates — a chance to check in on tasks and deliverables. Horowitz argues this is backwards. The value of the one-on-one is not what the manager learns about tasks; it's what the manager learns about the *person*:

- What are they concerned about?
- What are they excited about?
- Where do they feel blocked or undervalued?
- What problems do they see that you don't?

The manager should come prepared to listen, not to present.

## Structure

**Cadence:** Weekly for most direct reports; never less than bi-weekly.

**The employee's agenda:**
- They set the topics
- They control the conversation
- Manager provides reactions, coaching, decisions, unblocking

**Manager's preparation:**
- What feedback do I need to give?
- What decisions can I make in this meeting that would unblock them?
- What do I need to understand about their situation?

## Why It Matters

The one-on-one is the primary mechanism through which:
1. **Problems surface early** — issues that would become crises in six months get caught now
2. **Trust is built** — consistent, reliable attention is how managers earn the right to give hard feedback
3. **Retention is protected** — people rarely quit out of nowhere; they quit because they felt unseen; one-on-ones prevent that
4. **Coaching happens** — skill development requires conversation, not just performance reviews

Skipping one-on-ones is [management debt](../concepts/management-debt.md) — it feels efficient now and is expensive later.
