# Psychological Safety

The belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns, or mistakes. Coined and researched by Amy Edmondson (Harvard Business School, 1999).

Psychological safety is the **individual and team-level experience** of what [Westrum's](../concepts/westrum-cultural-typologies.md) generative culture describes at the organisational level: an environment where information flows freely because speaking up is safe and valued.

> "Psychological safety is not about being nice. It is about candour — the willingness to speak up when something is wrong." — Amy Edmondson

---

## Why It Matters

Without psychological safety:
- People withhold problems they've spotted
- People don't ask for help when stuck
- People don't challenge decisions they think are wrong
- People don't report failures honestly
- The organisation receives a distorted picture of reality

With psychological safety:
- Problems surface early, when they are cheapest to fix
- Learning from failure is possible (blameless post-mortems work)
- Better decisions are made from honest inputs
- Innovation is possible (people propose ideas that might fail)

---

## Google Project Aristotle

Google studied 180+ teams from 2012–2015 to identify what makes teams effective. They measured dozens of variables — individual performance, tenure, personality, management structure.

The single strongest predictor of team effectiveness: **psychological safety**.

More important than:
- Individual talent or credentials
- Group cohesion
- Clear goals
- Work structure

Teams with high psychological safety outperformed on virtually every metric Google measured — quality, productivity, creativity, retention.

---

## The Distinction: Safety ≠ Comfort

Psychological safety is often misunderstood as making work comfortable or conflict-free. Edmondson is clear:

> "Psychological safety is not about being nice. It is about candour."

High psychological safety means people feel safe to:
- Disagree with the leader
- Say "I don't know"
- Point out a flaw in a plan
- Admit a mistake
- Raise an uncomfortable concern

It does not mean everyone agrees, criticism is avoided, or standards are lowered. In fact, high psychological safety combined with high standards produces the best outcomes — people push each other hard because they trust the environment is safe.

## Cross-Framework Connections

### ↔ Westrum Cultural Typologies
Psychological safety is the micro (team-level) expression of [Westrum's](./westrum-cultural-typologies.md) generative culture (organisational level). An organisation can declare a generative culture while individual teams have low psychological safety. Both levels matter: organisational norms set the context; team leaders set the local climate.

### ↔ Cialdini (Authority / Unity)
[Authority bias](./authority-bias.md) is the primary destroyer of psychological safety. In hierarchical environments, deference to authority is automatic — people don't speak up because the person in charge *seems* to know. [Unity](./unity.md) can work in either direction: strong in-group identity can either create safety (we're all on the same team, it's safe to be honest) or suppress it (don't embarrass the team by raising a problem).

### ↔ Deep Work (Newport)
Psychological safety is a prerequisite for [deep work](./deep-work.md) in collaborative contexts. If people fear judgment or interruption, they cannot enter and sustain [flow](./flow-optimal-experience.md). The cognitive overhead of self-monitoring in an unsafe environment consumes exactly the attentional bandwidth that deep work requires.

### ↔ Learning Organisation (Senge)
Senge's [dialogue](./learning-organisation.md) — collective inquiry where assumptions are suspended — is only possible in psychologically safe environments. Discussion (advocacy, defending positions) doesn't require safety; dialogue requires it. Team learning, the Fourth Discipline, has psychological safety as its prerequisite.

### ↔ OKRs (Doerr)
Moonshot OKRs — where a score of 0.7 is acceptable — only function in psychologically safe environments. If people fear that a 0.7 will damage their career, they will set conservative OKRs. Psychological safety is the cultural substrate that makes ambitious goal-setting safe to practise.
