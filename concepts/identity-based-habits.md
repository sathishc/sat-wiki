# Identity-Based Habits

The deepest level of behaviour change, according to James Clear, is not changing what you do — it is changing **who you believe you are**. Identity-based habits are habits rooted in self-conception rather than desired outcomes.

## The Three Layers of Behaviour Change

Clear describes three concentric layers:

```
Outcomes  (what you get)
    ↑
Processes (what you do)
    ↑
Identity  (what you believe)
```

Most people work from the outside in: "I want to lose 10 kg" (outcome) → "I'll go to the gym" (process). This is fragile because the identity ("I am not a fit person") is unchanged and reasserts itself under stress.

Identity-based habits work from the inside out: "I am an athlete" (identity) → naturally leads to the processes and outcomes that follow.

## Every Vote Counts

The mechanism: **each action is a vote cast for your identity.** You don't build an identity through a single grand gesture; you accumulate evidence. Each time you keep a small promise to yourself, you add a vote. Enough votes shift the narrative you tell about yourself.

This is why the two-minute rule works: doing a scaled-down version of a habit still casts a vote. Showing up matters more than the magnitude of effort on any given day.

## Why Identity is the Deepest Level

- Outcomes change situations; identity changes the person.
- Identities are self-reinforcing — once adopted, they create confirmation bias in favour of supporting evidence.
- Identity explains why external pressure (accountability, incentives) often fails long-term: it addresses behaviour without addressing the underlying self-model.

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

### [Robert Cialdini](../people/robert-cialdini.md) — Commitment and Consistency
Once a person accepts a self-image ("I am someone who…"), internal psychological pressure drives behaviour into alignment with that image. Habit formation exploits this mechanism deliberately. See [Commitment and Consistency](../concepts/commitment-and-consistency.md).

### [Jonathan Haidt](../people/jonathan-haidt.md) — The Elephant
Identity operates at the elephant level, not the rider level. Trying to change behaviour through willpower (rider) without changing self-concept (elephant) is exhausting and unsustainable. Identity-based habits are a method for retraining the elephant. See [Rider and Elephant](../concepts/rider-and-elephant.md).

### [Daniel Goleman](../people/daniel-goleman.md) — Self-Awareness
Self-awareness is prerequisite to identity change: you can't reshape an identity you haven't observed. Goleman's work on emotional intelligence — particularly the self-awareness cluster — provides the observational foundation that makes identity-based habit-building possible. See [Self-Awareness](../concepts/self-awareness.md).
