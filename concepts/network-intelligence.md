# Network Intelligence

**Your network is your net worth. Opportunities, information, and resources flow through relationships.**

The strategic use of professional and personal networks to access information, opportunities, and resources. From [Reid Hoffman](../people/reid-hoffman.md)'s [*The Start-up of You*](../sources/hoffman-startup-of-you.md), building on [Mark Granovetter](../people/mark-granovetter.md)'s network sociology.

## The Core Idea

**Success is not purely individual.** Your career depends on:
- Who you know
- Who knows you
- Who can introduce you to whom
- What information flows through your network
- What opportunities your network surfaces

**Network intelligence = leveraging relationships systematically to:**
1. Access novel information
2. Discover hidden opportunities
3. Get help and resources
4. Build reputation and visibility

## Why Networks Matter

### 1. The Hidden Job Market

**80% of jobs are never publicly posted.** They're filled through:
- Internal referrals
- Personal recommendations
- "I know someone who..."

If you're only applying to posted jobs, you're competing in a crowded market for 20% of opportunities. The other 80% flow through networks.

### 2. Information Advantages

- Your close friends know what you know (you're in the same echo chamber)
- **Novel information comes through [weak ties](weak-ties.md)** — acquaintances who bridge you to different networks
- The person who hears about opportunities first wins
- Network intelligence is an information arbitrage strategy

### 3. Resources and Help

- Need an introduction? Your network provides it.
- Need advice? Your network has expertise.
- Need a co-founder, investor, first customer? Your network.
- **You access resources you don't personally possess through your network.**

### 4. Reputation and Trust

- People hire/invest in/partner with people they trust
- Trust is built through networks: "I trust you because someone I trust vouches for you"
- Your reputation spreads through your network
- Building in public amplifies this (see [leverage](leverage.md) through media)

## The Three Layers of Network

From [Reid Hoffman](../people/reid-hoffman.md):

### Layer 1: Allies (Strong Ties)

- **5-10 people** you trust deeply
- Regular contact, mutual commitment, high reciprocity
- Your inner circle: you help each other succeed
- **Investment strategy:** Deep, ongoing, proactive
- Examples: Co-founders, best friends, close mentors, key colleagues

### Layer 2: Weak Ties (Acquaintances)

- **Infrequent contact, lower trust, but HIGH value**
- See [weak ties](weak-ties.md) for full explanation
- **Why they matter:** They bridge you to different networks, providing novel information and opportunities
- Most career breakthroughs come through weak ties, not close friends
- **Investment strategy:** Maintain, reactivate, expand
- Examples: Former colleagues, conference contacts, alumni network, LinkedIn connections

### Layer 3: Extended Network (2nd/3rd Degree)

- People you don't know but can access through intermediaries
- "Friends of friends"
- LinkedIn makes this visible (2nd and 3rd connections)
- **Activation strategy:** Ask your network for introductions
- "Who do you know who works at X?"
- "Can you introduce me to Y?"

## How to Build Network Intelligence

### 1. Give First

**Don't only reach out when you need something.**

- Help others before you need help
- Build relationship capital (goodwill bank account)
- Be useful: share information, make introductions, provide feedback
- Generosity compounds over time

**The law of reciprocity:** When you help someone, they want to help you back (see [reciprocity](reciprocity.md)). But don't be transactional; be genuinely helpful.

### 2. Be a Connector

**Become an information hub by connecting people who should know each other.**

- Introduce two people per week
- "You should meet X because..."
- Value to you: Both parties remember you as the connector
- Value to them: You just solved a problem or created an opportunity

**Brokers who bridge [structural holes](../people/mark-granovetter.md) have outsized influence.**

### 3. Maintain Weak Ties

- Attend conferences, industry events, alumni gatherings
- Reactivate dormant ties: reach out to people you haven't spoken to in years
- Regular check-ins: quarterly coffee with acquaintances
- Don't just deepen existing relationships; **expand to adjacent networks**

### 4. Network Diversity > Network Size

**1000 connections in one industry < 100 connections across 10 industries**

- Homogeneous networks give redundant information
- Diverse networks give novel information and unexpected combinations
- Bridge different worlds: tech + finance, engineering + design, academia + industry

### 5. Tap the Extended Network

**Ask your network who they know.**

- "I'm looking to break into X industry. Who do you know there?"
- "I need advice on Y. Do you know anyone who's done this?"
- Your network's network is exponentially larger than your direct network

### 6. Build in Public

- Write, speak, ship projects publicly
- Create a public track record that attracts opportunities
- [Leverage](leverage.md) through code and media: your work reaches people you've never met
- Example: This wiki, blog posts, open-source contributions, talks

## The I^We Principle

See [I^We](i-we.md) for full concept.

**"I" to the power of "We"** — individual capabilities are amplified by the network.

- Your intelligence = your brain + access to others' brains through your network
- Your resources = your assets + access to others' resources
- Your opportunities = what you see + what your network sees

**Success is not solo.** Even individual achievements depend on networks.

## Network Intelligence in Practice

### Job Search
- Don't just apply online
- Map your network to target companies
- Ask for introductions to hiring managers
- "Who do I know at Company X?"

### Starting a Company
- Co-founder: usually comes from your network
- First customers: usually your network
- Investors: introductions through your network
- Advisors: tapped from your network

### Career Pivots
- Want to break into a new field? Talk to people in that field (your weak ties or extended network)
- **Informational interviews:** Learn from people doing what you want to do
- Build bridges into the new field before you jump

### Learning and Growth
- Want to learn X? Find someone who knows X (in your network or extended network)
- Mentorship usually comes through networks, not formal programs
- Peer learning: allies who are on similar journeys

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Networking Only When You Need Something
- **Red flag:** You only reach out when you want a job/favor/introduction
- **Fix:** Maintain relationships during good times; give first

### Mistake 2: Focusing Only on Strong Ties
- Your close friends are valuable, but they know what you know
- **Fix:** Invest in [weak ties](weak-ties.md) — attend events, reactivate dormant connections

### Mistake 3: Networking "Up" Only
- Don't just network with powerful people
- **Fix:** Network across (peers in different fields) and down (help people earlier in the journey)
- The junior person today may be the senior person tomorrow

### Mistake 4: Transactional Mindset
- Networking feels slimy when it's purely transactional
- **Fix:** Be genuinely interested in people; help without expecting immediate return
- Build relationships, not transactions

### Mistake 5: Waiting for "The Right Time"
- "I'll network when I have more to offer"
- **Fix:** Start now. Everyone has something to give (even if it's just enthusiasm and curiosity)

## Metrics That Matter

**Weak proxy:** Number of connections (LinkedIn)
**Better proxy:** Network diversity (how many different clusters do you bridge?)
**Best proxy:** Network activation rate (when you reach out, how many people respond and help?)

**The real test:** When you need something (introduction, advice, help), can you get it through your network within 2-3 hops?

## Key Insight from Reid Hoffman

> "In a world where careers are less predictable, building your network is the single best investment you can make."

Why?
- Skills decay; relationships compound
- Jobs are found through networks, not job boards
- Opportunities surface through networks before they're public
- Resources flow through relationships
- **Your network is portable** — it goes with you across jobs, companies, industries

**Career insurance = strong, diverse network.**

## Practical Action Steps

1. **Audit your network:** Who are your allies? Weak ties? Where are the gaps?
2. **Give first:** Help 3 people this week without expecting anything back
3. **Be a connector:** Introduce 2 people who should know each other
4. **Attend one event per quarter:** Conference, meetup, alumni gathering
5. **Reactivate 3 dormant ties per month:** "Hey, been a while, what are you working on?"
6. **Build in public:** Ship one public project this quarter (blog post, talk, open-source contribution)
7. **Ask your network:** "I'm trying to learn X / break into Y / meet someone who does Z — who do you know?"

Network intelligence is not schmoozing. It's strategic relationship-building that compounds over time.
