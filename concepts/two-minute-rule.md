# Two-Minute Rule

If a clarified action takes less than two minutes to complete, **do it immediately** — during the clarify step, not later.

> "If the action will take less than two minutes, it should be done the moment it's defined." — David Allen

## The Logic

The overhead of adding an item to a list, reviewing the list later, finding the item, remembering context, and executing — exceeds the cost of just doing a two-minute task now. Below two minutes, deferral has negative expected value.

This applies only during the **clarify** phase of [GTD](./gtd-workflow.md), when you're processing inboxes. It is not an invitation to switch tasks at random throughout the day.

## Boundary Conditions

- **During capture/clarification**: Apply the rule freely — process inboxes and knock out <2-minute items
- **During focused work**: Do *not* apply the rule — protect the current context; instead capture the item for later
- **The threshold is approximate**: Two minutes is a heuristic, not a law. Some use three minutes. The point is: very short tasks should not get deferred.

## Why It Feels Significant

The two-minute rule delivers immediate, visible results. In your first GTD inbox sweep, dozens of small items get cleared that have been psychologically nagging for weeks. The psychological relief is disproportionate to the time spent — each cleared item removes a small open loop.

## Relationship to Habits

The rule is most powerful as a **default policy** rather than a per-item decision. When you've internalised "if it's <2 minutes I just do it," you stop agonising over whether to defer the quick reply or the short task.

James Clear ([Atomic Habits](./habit-loop.md)) has a related concept — the **Two-Minute Rule** for habit formation: scale any habit down to a version that takes two minutes, to lower the activation barrier. Allen's and Clear's two-minute rules are different in purpose but both reduce friction as a first principle.
