# Seen vs Unseen

The master framework of [Bastiat](../people/frederic-bastiat.md) and [Hazlitt](../people/henry-hazlitt.md): **every economic (and policy) decision has two classes of effects — the immediately visible ones, and the diffuse, delayed, invisible ones.** Almost all economic fallacies arise from reasoning only about the seen.

> "The art of economics consists in looking not merely at the immediate but at the longer effects of any act or policy; it consists in tracing the consequences of that policy not merely for one group but for all groups."
> — Henry Hazlitt

## The Asymmetry

The seen and unseen are systematically asymmetric in how they appear:

| Seen | Unseen |
|---|---|
| Immediate | Delayed |
| Concentrated in one group | Diffuse across many groups |
| Visible and measurable | Invisible and counterfactual |
| Creates an advocate (the beneficiary) | Has no advocate |
| Makes news | Never reported |

This asymmetry is why the same fallacies persist for centuries. The beneficiary of a policy is organised, motivated, and visible. The cost-bearers are scattered, unaware they're bearing costs, and invisible.

## The Broken Window Applied Universally

The [Broken Window Fallacy](./broken-window-fallacy.md) is the simplest case. But Hazlitt shows it scales:

**Tariffs:**
- Seen: steel workers keep their jobs
- Unseen: every industry using steel pays more; consumers pay higher prices; export industries are harmed by retaliatory tariffs

**Government stimulus:**
- Seen: construction jobs created by the bridge project
- Unseen: the private jobs and investments that the taxes or borrowing crowded out

**Rent control:**
- Seen: existing tenants pay lower rent
- Unseen: landlords stop building; maintenance falls; housing supply shrinks; future tenants have no options

**Minimum wage:**
- Seen: workers who keep jobs get higher wages
- Unseen: workers priced out of employment; reduced hiring; substitution toward automation

**Inflation:**
- Seen: debtors benefit; government can spend
- Unseen: creditors are robbed; the price system is distorted; long-run investment calculation is impaired

## Why This Is Hard

The seen is emotionally compelling. A factory worker who loses a job to a trade agreement is a person with a face. The million consumers who each save $2 on a product because of that same trade agreement are invisible. Newspapers cover the one; nobody covers the million.

This is not a failure of intelligence — it is a structural feature of how attention works. The concentrated, immediate, visible effect always wins the emotional argument against the diffuse, delayed, invisible one.

## The Broader Application: Beyond Economics

The seen vs unseen framework extends far beyond economics:

**In product development ([Reinertsen](../people/donald-reinertsen.md)):**
- Seen: the team is fully utilised, everyone is busy
- Unseen: the invisible [queues](./queues-in-product-development.md) that are doubling cycle time

**In risk ([Taleb](../people/nassim-taleb.md)):**
- Seen: the strategy has worked for 10 years, smooth returns
- Unseen: the tail risk that will eventually detonate — the [Black Swan](./black-swan.md)

**In management ([Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)):**
- Seen: the manager who smoothed over the conflict, kept the meeting positive
- Unseen: the unaddressed problem that will compound quietly

**In personal decisions ([Naval](../people/naval-ravikant.md)):**
- Seen: the high-paying job that costs your time
- Unseen: the [specific knowledge](./specific-knowledge.md) you didn't build; the [compounding](./compounding.md) you didn't start

## The Meta-Lesson

**The most important costs and consequences are almost never the ones being measured.** Good thinking in any domain requires asking: what is not being seen here? What is the counterfactual? Who is bearing a cost that has no advocate?
