# Rational Buddhism

[Naval Ravikant](../people/naval-ravikant.md)'s personal philosophy — a secular distillation of Buddhist insight stripped of metaphysical claims, kept for its empirical usefulness.

## What It Takes from Buddhism

- **Impermanence** — everything changes; suffering comes from resisting this
- **Non-attachment** — holding things lightly, not grasping
- **The constructed self** — the "I" is a story the mind tells, not a fixed entity
- **Present-moment awareness** — the only real locus of experience

## What It Drops

- Reincarnation, karma as cosmic law, supernatural claims
- Religious ritual and hierarchy
- Any metaphysics that cannot be empirically tested

## The Self

The self is not a thing — it is a process, a narrative the brain constructs to maintain continuity. Recognising this makes it easier to:
- Not over-identify with past failures or future fears
- Change — if the self is not fixed, neither are your habits or patterns
- Reduce ego friction in decisions

## Impermanence as a Tool

Accepting that everything is temporary — relationships, achievements, moods, life itself — does not produce nihilism. It produces clarity. When you stop expecting permanence, you stop being surprised and hurt by change.

Naval uses death awareness directly: "If you're not scared to die, you're not scared of anything." Contemplating mortality collapses trivial anxieties.

## Relationship to [Happiness](./happiness.md)

Rational Buddhism is the philosophical foundation underneath Naval's happiness framework. Desire causes suffering → impermanence means no outcome will satisfy permanently → presence is the only available peace.
