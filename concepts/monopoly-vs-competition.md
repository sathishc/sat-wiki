# Monopoly vs Competition

> "Competition is for losers." — Peter Thiel

## The Contrarian Claim

Conventional economics teaches that competition is good and monopoly is bad — for consumers and society. Thiel inverts this for the entrepreneur:

- **Perfect competition** → no pricing power → commodity margins → no profits → no R&D → no future
- **Monopoly** → pricing power → profits → investment in innovation → long-term value creation

The best companies — Google, Apple, Amazon — are monopolies. They just don't call themselves that.

## How Monopolies Hide

Monopolies downplay their dominance to avoid regulation. They define their market *broadly*:
- "Google is just a small player in the global advertising market"

Competitive companies exaggerate their uniqueness to attract investment. They define their market *narrowly*:
- "We're the only British-Indian fusion restaurant in Palo Alto"

**The entrepreneur's job is to actually *be* a monopoly, not to sound like one.**

## The Four Characteristics of Monopoly

Thiel identifies four properties that durable monopolies share:

| Property | What It Means |
|----------|--------------|
| **Proprietary technology** | 10× better than nearest substitute (not incremental improvement) |
| **Network effects** | More users → more valuable (Facebook, credit cards, WhatsApp) |
| **Economies of scale** | Fixed costs spread over more units; marginal cost approaches zero (software) |
| **Branding** | Deep customer identity/loyalty; hard to commoditise |

Most durable monopolies have at least two of these. The best have all four.

## How to Build One: Start Small

Don't start by trying to capture a big market. Start by **dominating a small niche** — then expand:

1. Find a small market you can win completely
2. Dominate it (achieve monopoly position)
3. Expand to adjacent markets from a position of strength

*Example:* Amazon started with books. PayPal started with eBay power sellers. Facebook started with Harvard students.

This is Thiel's version of what [Geoffrey Moore](../people/geoffrey-moore.md) calls the bowling alley strategy.

## The Lies Both Sides Tell

| Who | What they say | What it means |
|-----|--------------|---------------|
| Monopolists | "We're in a huge competitive market" | Deflecting regulatory attention |
| Competitors | "We're totally unique in our niche" | Fundraising pitch; actually losing to margins |
