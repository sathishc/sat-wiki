# Barbell Strategy

A risk management approach from [Nassim Taleb](../people/nassim-taleb.md): instead of targeting a "middle ground" of moderate risk, go **bimodal** — maximum safety on one end, maximum upside on the other, with nothing in the middle.

## The Shape

```
[Very Safe] ————————————— [Highly Speculative]
   80-90%      nothing          10-20%
```

The middle (moderate risk) is the danger zone — it feels safe but carries catastrophic downside with limited upside.

## Why the Middle Is Dangerous

"Medium risk" positions tend to have hidden fragility:
- They appear stable but can blow up
- They don't protect you from [Black Swans](./black-swan.md)
- They sacrifice upside without truly eliminating downside

A barbell eliminates this: the safe end cannot blow up; the speculative end has **capped downside** (you can only lose what you put in) but **uncapped upside**.

## Applications

### Investing
- 90% in treasury bills / cash (extremely safe)
- 10% in highly speculative bets (startups, options, asymmetric payoffs)
- Result: worst case you lose 10%; best case a single bet pays 10-100x

### Career
- Keep a stable, boring income source (the safe bar)
- Spend discretionary time on high-variance experiments — writing, side projects, building things
- Avoid the "medium risk" career: a prestigious but fragile corporate role with no optionality

### Health
- Mostly conservative (sleep, diet, avoid obvious harms)
- Occasionally push hard (intense exercise, fasting) — the controlled stressor that builds [antifragility](./antifragility.md)

### Information
- Deep expertise in a narrow domain (safe anchor)
- Wide, curious reading across unrelated fields (the speculative bar that generates unexpected connections)

## Connection to Naval's [Leverage](./leverage.md)

Naval's permissionless leverage (code, media) has barbell-like properties: creating content or software costs time upfront (capped downside) with potentially unlimited distribution (uncapped upside). This structural asymmetry is why both Taleb and Naval converge on it.
