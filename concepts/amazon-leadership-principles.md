# Amazon Leadership Principles

Amazon's 16 operating principles — the explicit cultural code governing how every employee is expected to think, decide, and behave. Used in hiring, performance reviews, promotion decisions, and daily operations. Unlike most corporate values, these are **mechanisms**, not aspirations.

> "We use our Leadership Principles every day, whether we're discussing ideas for new projects or deciding on the best way to solve a problem."

## The 16 Principles

### 1. Customer Obsession
Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They obsess over customers, not competitors. Every product decision, every trade-off, starts with: *what does the customer want?*

### 2. Ownership
Leaders are owners — long-term, company-wide. They never say "that's not my job." They don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results.

### 3. Invent and Simplify
Leaders demand innovation and always simplify. They are not limited by "not invented here." They accept being misunderstood for long periods.

### 4. Are Right, A Lot
Leaders have strong judgment and good instincts. Critically: they actively seek diverse perspectives and work to **disconfirm** their own beliefs. Being right requires intellectual honesty.

### 5. Learn and Be Curious
Leaders are never done learning. Always improving, always exploring new possibilities.

### 6. Hire and Develop the Best
Leaders raise the bar with every hire and promotion. They develop other leaders. They move talent to where it creates the most value.

### 7. Insist on the Highest Standards
Relentlessly high standards — many will think unreasonably so. Defects don't get passed downstream. Problems are fixed so they stay fixed.

### 8. Think Big
Small thinking is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Leaders create bold direction that inspires results. They look around corners.

### 9. Bias for Action
Speed matters in business. Many decisions are reversible. A bias toward action over deliberation — calculated risk-taking is valued over cautious paralysis.

### 10. Frugality
Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention. Headcount, budget, and fixed costs are not proxies for value.

### 11. Earn Trust
Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully — even when it's uncomfortable. They benchmark themselves against the best.

### 12. Dive Deep
Leaders operate at all levels and stay connected to the details. No task is beneath a leader. Data and anecdote are both treated with scepticism until verified.

### 13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
Leaders respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree — even when it's uncomfortable or unpopular. They commit fully once a decision is made. They do not capitulate to avoid social friction.

### 14. Deliver Results
Leaders focus on the key inputs and deliver results with the right quality and in a timely fashion. They rise to meet challenges. Setbacks don't define them.

### 15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
Leaders work every day to create a safer, more productive, higher-performing, more diverse, and more just work environment.

### 16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility
Amazon's reach creates obligations beyond profit. Leaders act as good citizens and think about the broader impact of their decisions on customers, employees, communities, and the planet.

## Underlying Patterns

Several principles cluster around deeper meta-themes:

**Long-term thinking over short-term comfort:**
Ownership (#2), Think Big (#8), Insist on Highest Standards (#7), Deliver Results (#14) all encode patience with the long game — discomfort today for value tomorrow.

**Intellectual honesty:**
Are Right A Lot (#4), Dive Deep (#12), Have Backbone (#13), Earn Trust (#11) — Amazon's culture actively punishes the social lubricant of agreeing when you don't. Epistemic courage is required.

**Bias toward action under uncertainty:**
Bias for Action (#9), Frugality (#10), Invent and Simplify (#3) — the default is move, not wait. "Most decisions are reversible" is the justification.

**Customer as North Star:**
Customer Obsession (#1) is explicitly first for a reason. When in doubt, ask: what does the customer want?

## The "Disagree and Commit" Mechanism

Principle #13 is especially distinctive. It solves a real organisational problem: how do you get fast decisions without suppressing dissent?

The answer: **you are required to voice disagreement, then required to commit**. You can't silently comply. You can't relitigateafter the fact. The mechanism forces intellectual honesty upfront while preventing paralysis after.

[Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)'s [idea meritocracy](./idea-meritocracy.md) addresses the same problem differently — weight opinions by track record. Amazon's version is more binary: speak now or fully commit.
