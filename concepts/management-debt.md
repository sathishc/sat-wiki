# Management Debt

## One-Line Definition

Shortcuts taken in management and organisational design that feel fine now but accumulate [Compounding](compounding.md) "interest" — increasingly painful operational and cultural problems — over time.

## The Analogy to Technical Debt

Just as **technical debt** is code that works today but makes future changes harder and more expensive, **management debt** is organisational shortcuts that work today but make the company harder to run over time.

The metaphor is exact: you're borrowing against the future. You'll pay it back — with interest.

## Common Forms

| Debt Type | What It Looks Like | The Interest |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Two people in the same role** | Avoiding a hard conversation by giving two people overlapping titles | Confusion, politics, eventual explosion |
| **No performance management** | Not telling people when they're underperforming (too uncomfortable) | People who should improve don't; culture of low accountability |
| **Overcompensating to retain** | Paying above market to keep someone because you're scared to lose them | Sets precedents; others find out; resentment |
| **Brilliant jerks** | Keeping someone toxic because they're high-performing | Destroys team trust; drives out the non-jerks |
| **Title inflation** | Giving VP titles to avoid paying market rate | Forces premature VP layer; hard to hire real VPs later |

## Why It Accumulates

Management debt accumulates because:
1. The short-term pain of doing it right is visible and immediate
2. The long-term cost of the shortcut is invisible and deferred
3. Leaders are busy and optimise for this quarter

This is structurally identical to why technical debt accumulates.

## Horowitz's Rule

> "You will be required to pay your management debts sooner than you think."

In peacetime, you can carry moderate management debt. In wartime — when the company needs to execute flawlessly under pressure — management debt compounds fast and becomes existential.
