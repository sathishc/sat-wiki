# Weekly Review

The cornerstone habit of [GTD](./gtd-workflow.md). A dedicated time each week to clear, update, and elevate — ensuring the [trusted system](./trusted-system.md) stays current and the mind stays clear.

> "The Weekly Review is the master key to GTD. Without it, the whole system winds down." — David Allen

## The Three-Phase Structure

### Phase 1: Get Clear
- Process all physical inboxes to empty
- Get to email/digital inbox zero
- Capture stray notes, business cards, receipts
- Review previous calendar entries (any actions or commitments to capture?)
- Review upcoming calendar (any preparation needed?)

### Phase 2: Get Current
- Review all **Next Actions** lists — mark off completed, delete obsolete
- Review **Projects list** — verify every project has at least one next action
- Review **Waiting For list** — follow up on anything overdue
- Review **Someday/Maybe list** — anything to activate? Anything to delete?
- Review any relevant checklists or goals

### Phase 3: Get Creative
- Any new projects? Any wild ideas? Any life changes requiring capture?
- Optional: review higher [Horizons of Focus](../sources/allen-getting-things-done.md#horizons-of-focus)

## Why It's the Keystone Habit

The weekly review does what daily capture and clarification can't: it provides the **elevated perspective**. On a daily basis you're in the weeds. The weekly review is the time to look up.

Without it:
- Completed items stay on lists (lists become untrustworthy)
- Projects lose their next actions (they stall)
- New priorities don't get encoded (you're working from stale data)
- The [Someday/Maybe list](../sources/allen-getting-things-done.md#the-somemaybe-list) becomes a graveyard

The brain senses when the system is stale and rebuilds internal tracking — destroying mind like water.

## Frequency and Timing

Allen recommends weekly, typically at the end of the work week (Friday afternoon is common). The cadence matters more than the day. Some practitioners do:
- **Weekly** for the full review
- **Daily** for a lighter scan (process inboxes, confirm today's next actions)

## The Weekly Review as a Designed Habit

The weekly review is best treated as a [habit](./habit-loop.md) with a fixed cue (Friday at 4pm, for example), not as a task to be scheduled when convenient. Scheduling it "when I have time" means it won't happen.

It also has a ritualistic quality: a dedicated space, a cup of tea, no interruptions. The context cues the mode.
