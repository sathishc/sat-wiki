# Enough

> "The hardest financial skill is getting the goalpost to stop moving." — Morgan Housel

## One-Line Definition

Knowing what "enough" means for you — and being able to stop there — is rarer, harder, and more valuable than maximising returns.

## The Problem

Modern financial culture has no concept of enough. The implicit message is always *more*:
- More savings
- More returns
- More status
- More optionality

This produces a specific trap: **the goalpost moves as fast as you run.** When you reach the number you thought would feel like enough, it doesn't. You recalibrate upward. The feeling of sufficiency never arrives.

Housel calls this the hardest financial skill — not picking stocks, not timing markets. **Stopping.**

## Why the Goalpost Moves

Two forces drive it:

1. **Social comparison** — we don't evaluate our wealth in absolute terms; we evaluate it relative to peers. As peers earn more (or appear to), the reference point shifts.
2. **Hedonic adaptation** — the pleasure of any acquisition fades quickly; we return to baseline and need the next thing to feel the same way.

Both are features of human psychology, not bugs. But in a financial context, they're expensive.

## The Cost of Not Knowing Enough

Housel gives the example of Rajat Gupta (former Goldman Sachs director) and Bernie Madoff — both deeply wealthy by any objective measure, both destroyed by not knowing when to stop. The risk they took was not commensurate with their situation; they were already winning, but couldn't see it.

> "There is no reason to risk what you have and need for what you don't have and don't need."

## How to Define Enough

Housel doesn't give a formula (correctly — it's personal). The exercise is:

1. **Name what you actually want** — not what you think you should want, or what signals status; what would *actually* make your life better?
2. **Price it** — what does it actually cost, not what you'd spend if you had unlimited money?
3. **Stop there** — once you have it, the incremental returns on more money are low; the risks you'd take to get more are not worth it
