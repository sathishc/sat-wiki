# Five-Step Process

[Ray Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md)'s fundamental framework for achieving goals — applicable to individuals, teams, and organisations. He considers it the core engine of progress.

## The Loop

```
1. Goals
    ↓
2. Problems
    ↓
3. Diagnosis
    ↓
4. Design
    ↓
5. Do It
    ↓
(back to Goals)
```

## Each Step

### 1. Set Clear Goals
Know specifically what you want. Vague goals produce vague results. Dalio distinguishes between:
- **Goals** — what you want to achieve
- **Desires** — what feels good moment-to-moment (often in conflict with goals)

> "If you work hard and think creatively, you can have just about anything you want, but not everything you want."

### 2. Identify Problems
Don't tolerate problems. Don't paper over them. Surface them explicitly. The most dangerous problems are the ones people are too uncomfortable to name.

### 3. Diagnose Root Causes
This is the step most people skip. Distinguish:
- **Symptoms** vs **root causes**
- **People problems** vs **process problems** (is the machine broken, or the person running it?)

Dalio uses a **machine analogy**: an organisation is a machine with people and design as its components. When output is wrong, trace it back to which part of the machine is failing.

### 4. Design Solutions
Once you know the root cause, design a plan that actually addresses it. Not a workaround. Not a patch. A structural fix.

### 5. Execute
Do what you designed. Then loop back: measure results, identify new problems, diagnose, redesign.

## The Key Insight: Separate the Steps

Most failure happens when steps collapse into each other:
- Trying to solve a problem you haven't diagnosed
- Designing before you've correctly identified the root cause
- Acting while still in the goal-setting phase

Dalio is explicit: **each step requires a different mode of thinking**. The diagnoser and the executor are in different mental states. Confusing them is costly.

## Connection to Dalio's Principle Philosophy

The five-step process is the meta-principle — the engine that generates all other principles. Every time Dalio encountered a new type of problem and found a solution, he encoded it as a principle. Then he could run it as a subroutine when that problem type recurred.

> "Write down your criteria for making a decision. Reflect on what worked and what didn't. Refine."

## Cross-Reference: [Compounding](./compounding.md)

Dalio's loop, applied consistently over years, is a compounding system. Each iteration teaches something. Encoded as a principle, that learning becomes a permanent asset. Naval's concept of [compounding](./compounding.md) applies directly: the knowledge compounds, not just the decisions.
