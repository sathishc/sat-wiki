# Natural Planning Model

David Allen's five-phase framework for how the brain plans effectively — and a critique of how most organisational planning skips the most important phases.

## The Five Phases

```
1. Purpose & Principles
       ↓
2. Outcome Visioning
       ↓
3. Brainstorm
       ↓
4. Organise
       ↓
5. Identify Next Actions
```

### 1. Purpose & Principles
*Why are we doing this? What are our constraints?*

Defining purpose clarifies standards and creates the decision-making filter for everything downstream. Principles define the rules of engagement ("we won't sacrifice quality", "this must be done by the end of Q2"). Without purpose, people optimise for the wrong thing.

### 2. Outcome Visioning
*What does wild success look like?*

Before problem-solving, define the destination. What does "done" look like? What would someone see, hear, feel if the project succeeded completely? Allen advocates for **uninhibited visioning** — not the hedged, politically-safe version of success, but the real desired outcome.

This is the most commonly skipped step. Most teams jump straight to brainstorming or organising without a shared picture of success. The result: everyone is optimising for different things.

### 3. Brainstorm
*What comes to mind?*

Classic divergent thinking. No judgment, no editing. Capture all ideas, risks, considerations. The goal is to externalise all relevant thinking — not to produce the plan yet.

Allen notes that the brain generates ideas in parallel, non-sequentially. Trying to organise while brainstorming kills idea generation. The phases must be kept separate.

### 4. Organise
*What are the components, sequences, priorities?*

Now you structure the brainstorm output. Identify:
- Key components (what are the major chunks?)
- Sequences (what has to happen before what?)
- Priorities (what matters most if trade-offs are needed?)

This phase produces a rough plan. Most organisational "planning" starts here — which is why it produces mediocre plans.

### 5. Identify Next Actions
*What moves this forward right now?*

For each component of the organised plan: what is the next physical, visible action? This is the bridge from plan to execution — the step that makes everything actionable in the [GTD workflow](./gtd-workflow.md).

## The Reactive Planning Model (Contrast)

When a project is in crisis, planning happens in reverse:
- You start with next actions ("just fix the immediate fire")
- Organise only when the fire is out
- Never revisit purpose or vision

Allen argues this isn't just inefficient — it's why projects stay in crisis. The missing step is almost always **outcome visioning**: nobody has a shared, clear picture of what success looks like, so every decision is contested.

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

- **[Dalio's Five-Step Process](./five-step-process.md)** — similar loop, different altitude. Dalio's process is for iterative goal pursuit; Allen's natural planning model is for project initiation.
- **[OKRs](./okrs.md)** — Outcome visioning maps to the "Key Results" definition step; purpose maps to the "Objective".
- **[Strategic Intent](./strategic-intent.md)** — Hamel & Prahalad's concept of an "envisioned future" is Phase 2 applied at company level.
- **[Competing for the Future](./competing-for-the-future.md)** — strategic visioning as a core competency.

## Why It Works

The natural planning model reflects how the brain *actually* plans (as opposed to how most meeting agendas are structured). The brain:
1. Asks "why?" (purpose)
2. Imagines success (vision)
3. Generates ideas (brainstorm)
4. Structures them (organise)
5. Decides on first steps (next actions)

Bypassing any phase means the plan is built on an unstable foundation.
