# Managerial Leverage

**Origin:** Andy Grove, *High Output Management* (1983)

## Core Idea

A manager's output is not the work they personally produce. Grove's formula:

> **Manager's Output = Output of their team + Output of adjacent teams they influence**

This reframes the manager's job entirely. If your value is measured by what you personally do, you are thinking like an individual contributor. If your value is measured by what your *team* produces — and by how much you multiply the effectiveness of others — you are thinking like a manager.

Since a manager's time is finite and fixed, the job becomes one of **leverage**: identifying and concentrating on the activities that produce the greatest amplification of team output per hour invested.

## High-Leverage Activities

Grove identifies three categories of high-leverage activity:

1. **When one person's action affects many people's work** — a training session, a decision that unblocks a team, a process change that improves how everyone operates
2. **When a small action has a large impact on a key output** — removing a bottleneck, making a hire, setting a clear direction at a critical juncture
3. **When the timing is right** — the same conversation has different leverage at different moments; early intervention in a crisis beats late intervention in a catastrophe

Low-leverage activity looks like: attending meetings where your presence adds nothing, doing work that a direct report could handle, optimising tasks that are not on the critical path.

## Task-Relevant Maturity and Management Style

Leverage is not just about *what* you do — it's about *how* you manage. The correct management style is not fixed; it should be calibrated to the **[Task-Relevant Maturity](task-relevant-maturity.md)** of the person you are managing for a given task. Applying a delegating style to a low-TRM employee wastes leverage; applying a directive style to a high-TRM employee destroys it by undermining autonomy and motivation.

## The Manager as Information Node

Another high-leverage role for a manager is as an **information hub**: someone who synthesises signals from inside and outside the organisation, identifies what matters, and routes the right information to the right people at the right time. This is why one-on-ones, staff meetings, and reviews are not interruptions — they are the primary information infrastructure of a team.

## Delegation and Monitoring

Delegation increases leverage but introduces risk. Grove's principle: you can delegate authority, but you cannot delegate accountability. The manager who delegates and disappears has not increased leverage — they have created a blind spot. Effective delegation requires **monitoring**: checking in at a frequency appropriate to the employee's TRM and the stakes of the task.

## Practical Implications

- **Audit your calendar** for leverage: are you spending time on high-leverage activities or low-leverage ones?
- **Batch low-leverage work** to protect time for high-leverage thinking and coaching
- **Meetings are production:** a 30-minute staff meeting that aligns ten people on a key decision is extremely high leverage
- **The bottleneck question:** where is the constraint in your team's output? Relieving that constraint is almost always the highest-leverage move available
