# "Founder Mode" — Paul Graham (2024)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** September 2024
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay, sparked by a talk Brian Chesky gave at a YC event

## Central Argument

There are two fundamentally different ways to run a company: **founder mode** and **manager mode**. The conventional wisdom — "hire good people and give them room to do their jobs" — is advice for professional managers, not founders. Founders who follow this advice often damage their companies. The world doesn't yet have a well-articulated theory of founder mode, but we can infer its existence from the pattern of founders who escaped manager mode and succeeded.

## Key Ideas

1. **Manager mode treats the org chart as black boxes.** You tell direct reports what to do and don't get involved in the details — no micromanagement. This works for professional managers but suppresses what makes founders effective.
2. **Founder mode breaks the rule about engaging only via direct reports.** Skip-level meetings become the norm. Steve Jobs held annual retreats for the 100 most important people at Apple — not the 100 highest on the org chart.
3. **Founders feel gaslit from both sides.** Advisors tell them to run the company like managers; and when they do, the people they hire make it worse. This is one of the rare situations where the universal default assumption ("when everyone disagrees with you, you're probably wrong") doesn't apply.
4. **"Hire good people and give them room" often becomes "hire professional fakers and let them drive the company into the ground."** C-level execs, as a class, include some of the most skillful operators of managing-up behavior.
5. **We know founder mode exists even without a theory of it.** Brian Chesky figured it out partly by studying how Steve Jobs ran Apple. Airbnb's free cash flow margin is now among the best in Silicon Valley.
6. **Founder mode is more complicated than manager mode**, and will vary company by company and even over time within a company as managers earn trust. But it works better.
7. **Founders who practiced it were often called eccentric.** Once we have a theory of founder mode, we'll recognize that many exceptional founders were already practicing it.

## Notable Quotes

> "What they were being told was how to run a company you hadn't founded — how to run a company if you're merely a professional manager. But this m.o. is so much less effective that to founders it feels broken. There are things founders can do that managers can't, and not doing them feels wrong to founders, because it is."

> "Hire good people and give them room to do their jobs. Sounds great when it's described that way, doesn't it? Except in practice, judging from the report of founder after founder, what this often turns out to mean is: hire professional fakers and let them drive the company into the ground."

> "Look at what founders have achieved already, and yet they've achieved this against a headwind of bad advice. Imagine what they'll do once we can tell them how to run their companies like Steve Jobs instead of John Sculley."
