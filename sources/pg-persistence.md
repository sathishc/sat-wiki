# "The Right Kind of Stubborn" — Paul Graham (2024)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** July 2024
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Persistent people and obstinate people look similar from the outside but are behaviorally different. **The persistent are attached to their goals; the obstinate are attached to their ideas about how to reach them.** This difference has practical consequences: the persistent are flexible on means and firm on ends; the obstinate are firm on means (often their first ideas) and implicitly flexible on ends (because they'll fail to reach them).

## Key Ideas

1. **The question is whether persistence and obstinacy are actually different behaviors.** If they're just two labels we apply depending on whether someone succeeded, there's nothing to learn. But Graham argues they really are different — you can tell in real time which one you're dealing with.
2. **The key diagnostic: do they listen?** The Collison brothers (Stripe founders) are extremely determined, but when you point out a problem, they listen with "almost predatory intensity." Obstinate people's eyes glaze over when you point out problems.
3. **The persistent are attached to the goal. The obstinate are attached to their ideas about how to reach it.** This means the obstinate tend to be especially attached to their *first* ideas — the ones least informed by actual experience with the problem.
4. **Persistence requires energy, imagination, and resilience.** Energy to keep trying. Imagination to think of new approaches when old ones fail. Resilience to keep going when things are difficult.
5. **The goal itself matters.** Persistent people tend to define their goals somewhat loosely, because too specific a goal makes you rigid. But not so loosely that you have no direction. The right goal specification is narrow enough to create traction but wide enough to allow for discovery.
6. **You can tell at the time.** You don't have to wait for the outcome to know which you're dealing with. The behavioral signatures — listening, flexibility on methods, engagement with criticism — are observable.

## Notable Quotes

> "The persistent are attached to the goal. The obstinate are attached to their ideas about how to reach it."

> "I can't think of anyone more determined than the Collison brothers, and when you point out a problem to them, they not only listen, but listen with an almost predatory intensity."

> "Worse still, that means they'll tend to be attached to their first ideas about how to solve a problem, even though these are the least informed by the experience of working on it."
