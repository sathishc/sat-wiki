# "Keep Your Identity Small" — Paul Graham (2009)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** February 2009
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

**People can't think clearly about anything that has become part of their identity.** The reason politics and religion produce uniquely useless discussions is not that they're complicated — it's that they engage people's identities. And this effect generalizes: any topic people make part of who they are becomes impervious to clear thinking. The solution: let as few things into your identity as possible.

## Key Ideas

1. **Religion and politics discussions degrade because of identity, not complexity.** There's no expertise threshold for having strong opinions about them. Everyone feels entitled to weigh in. The result is argument driven by identity defense, not by evidence or reason.
2. **The source of the problem is identity, not the topic.** The same degradation happens with any topic once it becomes part of people's identity — programming languages, car brands, sports teams. The topic itself isn't the problem.
3. **People can never have a fruitful argument about something that's part of their identity.** By definition they're partisan. They're not evaluating evidence; they're defending themselves.
4. **The most intriguing implication: a guide to having better ideas.** If identity crowds out thinking, then minimizing the things in your identity is a way to think more clearly. "The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber they make you."
5. **The two failure modes:** Refusing to update beliefs because they're part of who you are. And worse, not even being able to evaluate evidence because you never separated the belief from your sense of self.
6. **The practical recommendation:** Not just being tolerant of people who believe differently, but not even labeling yourself as X. Identify as few things as possible. Even good labels — "I'm a scientist" or "I'm a rationalist" — can become anchors that constrain your thinking.

## Notable Quotes

> "If people can't think clearly about anything that has become part of their identity, then all other things being equal, the best plan is to let as few things into your identity as possible."

> "The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber they make you."

> "By definition they're partisan. When people say a discussion has degenerated into a religious war, what they really mean is that it has started to be driven mostly by people's identities."
