# "Startup Investing Trends" — Paul Graham (2013)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** June 2013
- **Origin:** Talk written for an audience of investors

## Central Argument

Startup investing is structurally changing due to two forces: **it's becoming cheaper to start a startup**, and **starting a startup is becoming a more normal thing to do**. These forces will increase the volume of startups, increase early-stage failures, and create new opportunities for investors — especially at the earliest stages. The shape of the startup landscape is shifting from an obelisk to a pyramid.

## Key Ideas

1. **The number of startups is increasing.** Starting a startup is becoming a real option — not just in Silicon Valley but globally. The cost of starting a company has dropped dramatically since the 1980s.
2. **Returns follow a power law.** A small number of big hits dominate. YC's top 10 startups accounted for the vast majority of the portfolio's value. This pattern won't change with volume — if anything, the top will grow only a little while the bottom grows a lot.
3. **The future shape: from obelisk to pyramid.** More startups, more early failures, but also some more big hits at the top. The total volume of startup activity is growing much faster at the bottom.
4. **Opportunity for early-stage investors.** As the pyramid widens at the bottom, there's a vacuum — not enough investors at the earliest stages. Super angels and seed funds emerged to fill this gap.
5. **The Series A crunch.** As seed investing increased, the number of companies seeking Series A grew, but Series A rounds didn't grow proportionately. This creates a crunch where many seed-funded startups struggle to raise their next round.
6. **The solution: startups should be default alive.** Given the Series A crunch, startups should aim to be profitable without needing another raise. The funding environment makes this even more important.
7. **Idea clashes will increase.** With more startups, more will be doing the same thing simultaneously. Being first matters less; execution and focus matter more.

## Notable Quotes

> "That's how you win: by investing in the right startups. That is so much more important than anything else that I worry I'm misleading you by even talking about other things."

> "It's common in technology for an innovation that decreases the cost of failure to increase the number of failures and yet leave you net ahead."

> "What used to be an obelisk will become a pyramid. It will be a little wider at the top, but a lot wider at the bottom."
