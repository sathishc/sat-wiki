# "What You Can't Say" — Paul Graham (2004)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** January 2004
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Every era has taboo beliefs — ideas that are true but unsayable. The test for whether your own society has taboos isn't whether you have them (all societies do), but whether you can identify them. The skill of thinking thoughts that can't be said is valuable and trainable. It requires epistemic courage: the willingness to notice when the crowd is wrong and to hold that thought privately, even if you can't say it publicly.

## Key Ideas

1. **Moral fashions are like clothing fashions.** What people believe is morally acceptable changes with time. Many things that were unsayable 100 years ago are now obvious; many things accepted today will seem monstrous in 100 years. This should make you suspicious of your current certainties.
2. **The mechanism of suppression is social, not logical.** Taboo beliefs aren't suppressed because they're refuted — they're suppressed because saying them causes social punishment. This means the strength of the reaction tells you almost nothing about whether the belief is correct.
3. **The test: what are people punished for saying?** Find the things that cause maximum social penalty when said aloud. These are the candidates for "what you can't say." Some will be wrong; some won't be. The important thing is to be able to examine them rather than reflexively avoid them.
4. **Disagree privately, if not publicly.** You don't have to say everything you think. But you do have to be able to think it. If you've trained yourself to not even think forbidden thoughts, you've destroyed part of your thinking apparatus.
5. **Keep a "heresy" file.** Ideas that seem interesting but unsayable deserve a private space, not reflexive dismissal. Some will turn out to be wrong. Some will turn out to be important.
6. **The crowd is often wrong, especially about the future.** Moral progress requires someone to disagree with the current consensus. Every moral advance in history was once heresy.

## Notable Quotes

> "If you could travel back in a time machine, one of the things that would strike you most forcibly is how peculiar the opinions of the wise, well-intentioned people of earlier times were. And the odd thing is, this is true of every era."

> "What scares me is that there are moral fashions too. They're just as arbitrary, and just as invisible to most people. But they're much more dangerous."

> "The most important thing is to be able to think what you want, not to say it. I've spent a lot of time thinking about what's unsayable in my time, not so I can say it, but to make sure I can think it."

> "I suspect the statements that make people maddest are the ones they worry might be true."
