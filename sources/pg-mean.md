# "Mean People Fail" — Paul Graham (2014)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** November 2014
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Among the most successful people Graham knows, meanness is strikingly rare. This is not coincidence. **Meanness causes failure in the kinds of work that are increasingly important**: it makes you stupid, prevents you from attracting the best people, and removes the spirit of benevolence that drives the most successful founders to keep going when others stop.

## Key Ideas

1. **Being mean makes you stupid.** Fighting focuses your brain on winning a specific situation, not on thinking about real problems. "You don't win fights by thinking of big ideas but by thinking of tricks that work in one particular case." Startups don't win by attacking — they win by transcending.
2. **Mean founders can't get the best people.** The best people have options. They won't work for mean founders unless they have no choice. And yet having the best people is critical for startups — more critical than for any other type of organization.
3. **The founders who keep going are driven by something other than money.** Those driven purely by money take the big acquisition offer. The founders who build lasting companies are usually trying to improve the world, whether they say so explicitly or not. Benevolence is fuel.
4. **Meanness is a relic of zero-sum games.** For most of history, success meant control of scarce resources — won by fighting. But increasingly the games that matter are positive-sum: having new ideas, building new things. In these games, meanness is a handicap, not an advantage.
5. **The territory where good people win is growing.** Startups are one example. Intellectual work more broadly is another. As civil order and property rights spread, the domain where ideas matter more than ruthlessness expands.

## Notable Quotes

> "Being mean makes you stupid. That's why I hate fights. You never do your best work in a fight, because fights are not sufficiently general."

> "Startups don't win by attacking. They win by transcending."

> "The startup founders who end up richest are not the ones driven by money. The ones driven by money take the big acquisition offer that nearly every successful startup gets en route. The ones who keep going are driven by something else."

> "Increasingly you win not by fighting to get control of a scarce resource, but by having new ideas and building new things."
