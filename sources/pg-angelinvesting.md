# "How to Be an Angel Investor" — Paul Graham (2009)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** March 2009
- **Origin:** Talk at AngelConf; expanded to essay

## Central Argument

Angel investing is less mysterious and complicated than it looks — the mechanics are simple. **The hard part is judgment: picking the right startups.** Everything else — deal terms, paperwork, syndicate structure — is noise. The way you win at angel investing is by investing in the right companies, period.

## Key Ideas

1. **The mechanics are simple.** You give a startup money; they give you stock (preferred stock or convertible debt). Don't spend mental energy on deal terms when starting out. The phrase you want to hear is "He invested in Google" — not "He got a 4x liquidation preference."
2. **Picking the right startups is everything.** This is both the key insight and the core difficulty. A small number of investments will dominate any portfolio (power law). All other optimization is second-order.
3. **What to look for in founders.** Graham looks primarily at founders, not the idea. Ideas change; founders don't. He looks for determination, intelligence, and flexibility (the ability to pivot without losing direction). The best indicator is often how the founders respond to challenges.
4. **Deal flow comes from reputation.** The Valley runs on referrals. You get good deal flow by being known as a good investor — someone who is helpful, reliable, and not high-maintenance. Investing in a few startups is the prerequisite for getting referred to better startups.
5. **How VCs think about deals.** VCs invest to get big hits. They know most investments will fail or be mediocre. They're optimizing for the outliers. Angels can operate similarly or invest for other reasons (to learn, to stay connected to startups, to help founders), but understanding the VC logic helps you understand the ecosystem.
6. **The difference between angel and VC.** Angels can make smaller bets, move faster, and focus on very early stages. They also don't have fund pressures or LPs to satisfy. This gives them flexibility VCs don't have.
7. **Be helpful to founders.** The best angels create genuine value beyond money — by making introductions, giving advice, helping with hiring. This makes founders want to work with you and refer others to you.

## Notable Quotes

> "That's how you win: by investing in the right startups. That is so much more important than anything else that I worry I'm misleading you by even talking about other things."

> "Don't spend much time worrying about the details of deal terms, especially when you first start angel investing. That's not how you win at this game."

> "The Valley basically runs on referrals. And once you start to become known as a reliable, useful investor, people will refer lots of deals to you."
