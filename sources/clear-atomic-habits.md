# Atomic Habits — James Clear

**Author:** James Clear
**Published:** 2018
**Genre:** Self-help / Behavioural psychology

## Summary

*Atomic Habits* argues that tiny, 1% improvements compound into remarkable results over time. The book provides a practical framework for building good habits and breaking bad ones, grounded in behavioural psychology and cognitive science.

Clear's central thesis: **systems beat goals**. Most people focus on what they want to achieve; *Atomic Habits* focuses on the systems that make achievement inevitable.

## Key Ideas

### The Four Laws of Behaviour Change
Mapped onto the habit loop (Cue → Craving → Response → Reward):
1. **Make it obvious** — design your environment to surface cues
2. **Make it attractive** — pair habits with things you enjoy (temptation bundling)
3. **Make it easy** — reduce friction; two-minute rule
4. **Make it satisfying** — immediate rewards reinforce the loop

Inversion of the four laws breaks bad habits: make it invisible, make it unattractive, make it difficult, make it unsatisfying.

### 1% Improvements (The Aggregation of Marginal Gains)
Getting 1% better every day for a year results in being 37× better. Getting 1% worse results in nearly zero. Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.

### Identity-Based Change
The three layers of behaviour change: Outcomes → Processes → Identity. The most durable habits are rooted in identity: "I am the type of person who…". Every action is a vote cast for your desired identity.

### Habit Stacking
Implementation intentions: "After I [current habit], I will [new habit]." Anchoring new behaviours to existing routines dramatically increases follow-through.

### Environment Design
Behaviour is a function of person and environment. Engineering your surroundings to make good habits obvious and bad habits invisible is often more powerful than willpower.
