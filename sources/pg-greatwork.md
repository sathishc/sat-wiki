# "How to Do Great Work" — Paul Graham (2023)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** July 2023
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay — one of Graham's longest and most ambitious

## Central Argument

There is a recipe for great work that applies across fields. It's not just "work hard." The intersection of what people who do great work have in common has a definite shape: **choose the right thing to work on (aptitude × interest × scope), get to the frontier, notice gaps, explore them — and let curiosity be both the engine and the rudder.** Everything else flows from genuinely caring about what you're working on.

## Key Ideas

1. **Four steps:** Choose a field. Learn enough to get to the frontier. Notice gaps. Explore promising ones. This is how practically everyone who's done great work has done it — from painters to physicists.
2. **Curiosity is the engine and the rudder.** There's a kind of excited curiosity that will not only drive you, but if you let it have its way, will show you what to work on. What you're looking for: what are you excessively curious about — curious to a degree that would bore most other people?
3. **Taste is necessary for originality.** You can't make something better than your mental model of what good looks like. Developing taste — by studying the best work in a field — is prerequisite to producing anything worth producing.
4. **Originality is a habit of mind.** Original thinkers throw off new ideas about whatever they focus on. It can't be turned on and off — it's a default mode. You cultivate it by allowing yourself to be interested in things.
5. **Compounding applies to learning and reputation.** Work that compounds creates exponential growth. Learning is an instance: the more you know, the faster you learn more. Growing an audience is another. The implication: start early, stay consistent, and let time work.
6. **Boldness is prerequisite.** The biggest mistakes people make in choosing what to work on are: being too conventional, being afraid to work on something that seems hard, and being afraid that someone else has already figured it out. If you can tell what everyone else is overlooking, that's as good a bet as you'll find.
7. **Morale is the fuel.** Doing great work requires sustaining the effort over years. Morale — caring about the work — is what makes that possible. Choosing the right problem is therefore a practical matter, not just an aesthetic one.
8. **Take breaks and let the unconscious work.** Many breakthroughs come when you're not actively working on the problem. Give your unconscious mind problems to work on and then step away.

## Notable Quotes

> "There's a kind of excited curiosity that's both the engine and the rudder of great work. It will not only drive you, but if you let it have its way, will also show you what to work on."

> "Four steps: choose a field, learn enough to get to the frontier, notice gaps, explore promising ones. This is how practically everyone who's done great work has done it, from painters to physicists."

> "Originality isn't a process, but a habit of mind. Original thinkers throw off new ideas about whatever they focus on, like an angle grinder throwing off sparks. They can't help it."

> "If you do work that compounds, you'll get exponential growth. Most people who do this do it unconsciously, but it's worth stopping to think about."
