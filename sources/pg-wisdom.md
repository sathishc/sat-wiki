# "Is It Worth Being Wise?" — Paul Graham (2007)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** February 2007
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Wisdom and intelligence are different shapes of performance, not just different amounts of the same thing. Wisdom is high average performance across all situations; intelligence is spectacular performance in a few. As knowledge becomes more specialised and more of valuable work requires creating new things rather than choosing between known options, intelligence has become relatively more valuable than wisdom. But wisdom may matter more than it looks: in a world of spiky intelligence, the bottleneck is often judgment.

## Key Ideas

1. **Wisdom = high average; intelligence = high peaks.** Wise people consistently know the right thing to do. Intelligent people occasionally do spectacularly well. These are genuinely different — a person can be one without the other.
2. **The shape matters, not just the magnitude.** The optimal mix of wisdom and intelligence depends on the type of work. Work that requires reliable good judgment (leadership, surgery, parenting) rewards wisdom. Work that requires breakthrough insights (research, founding) rewards intelligence.
3. **Historical shift toward intelligence.** In ancient societies, most work involved choosing between known options — the right answer existed, and wisdom helped you find it. As knowledge specialised, more work requires creating new things. Performance is now unbounded; spikes matter more.
4. **Wisdom comes from curing childish qualities; intelligence from cultivating them.** The recipe for wisdom: patience, emotional control, restraint, learning from mistakes. The recipe for intelligence: obsessive curiosity, willingness to ignore social convention, delight in ideas for their own sake. These are somewhat in tension.
5. **The bottleneck may be wisdom anyway.** Even in a world of spiky intelligence, the judgment to apply intelligence wisely may be scarce. Many highly intelligent people have made spectacular errors in domains outside their expertise — not from lack of intelligence but from lack of wisdom (overconfidence, poor emotional regulation, bad values).

## Notable Quotes

> "Wisdom is knowing what to do in a lot of different situations. Intelligence is knowing what to do spectacularly well in a few. These are genuinely different things."

> "The recipe for wisdom is largely about curing childish qualities: learn from mistakes, be patient, don't let emotions distort judgment. The recipe for intelligence is largely the opposite: be obsessive, be willing to ignore social convention, let curiosity run away with you."

> "In ancient societies, performance was mostly bounded. The king had to choose whether to invade his neighbor — but not to invent firearms. As knowledge has grown more specialised, performance has become increasingly unbounded. Intelligence has become more valuable relative to wisdom."
