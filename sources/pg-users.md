# "What I've Learned from Users" — Paul Graham (2022)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** September 2022
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

After funding thousands of startups through Y Combinator, PG reflects on what he's learned. The core insight: **most startups have the same problems**, and the most important thing YC does is help founders focus — to identify which problems actually matter, then move fast to solve them. Speed is the essence of a startup; focus enables speed.

## Key Ideas

1. **Most startups have the same problems.** Despite building different things, startups run into the same issues — in different forms — over and over. This is what makes YC's pattern-matching valuable. A YC partner in their first year sees more startup problems than most later-stage investors see in a career.
2. **Founders are often bad at diagnosing their own problems.** They come in to talk about fundraising; the real problem is the company isn't doing well. They worry about user acquisition; the real problem is the product isn't good enough. The surface problem is often a symptom of a deeper one.
3. **Founders don't listen — and there's a reason.** Startups are so counterintuitive that correct advice sounds wrong. Founders don't follow advice because they don't *believe* it, not just because they're stubborn. Experience eventually teaches them.
4. **Focus is doubly important for early startups.** They have a hundred problems and only 2-3 founders. If founders focus on things that don't matter, nobody is focusing on the things that do. The essence of YC: figure out which problems matter most, generate ideas for solving them, test, and measure.
5. **Speed defines startups. Focus enables speed. YC improves focus.** A small increment of navigational improvement has a double effect: shorter path + faster travel.
6. **Great colleagues are as important as the advice.** Great work clusters in specific places and institutions. YC was deliberately designed to be one of those clusters. The energy created by a room full of ambitious peers is something money can't replicate.
7. **Founders are surprisingly generous with each other.** The generosity between YC founders — helping batchmates with intros, advice, customers — was one of the most surprising and heartening things Graham observed.

## Notable Quotes

> "Speed defines startups. Focus enables speed. YC improves focus."

> "The reason startups are so counterintuitive is that they're so different from most people's other experiences. No one knows what it's like except those who've done it."

> "Many founders, especially young and ambitious ones, have been trained to win the wrong way. The educational system in most countries trains you to win by hacking the test instead of actually doing whatever it's supposed to measure."

> "If you correct course at a high enough frequency, you can be simultaneously decisive at a micro scale and tentative at a macro scale."
