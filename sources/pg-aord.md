# "Default Alive or Default Dead?" — Paul Graham (2015)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** October 2015
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Every startup past the early stage needs to answer one key question: **given current expenses and revenue growth, will the company reach profitability on the money it has, or will it run out of money first?** The answer — default alive or default dead — determines everything about what advice is relevant. Shockingly, most founders don't know the answer. Not knowing is itself a sign of danger.

## Key Ideas

1. **Default alive vs. default dead is the first question.** If default alive, you can talk about ambitious new directions. If default dead, everything else is irrelevant — you need to figure out how to get off that trajectory first.
2. **The fatal pinch:** Default dead + slow growth + not enough time to fix it. Founders end up here by not realizing they're headed there. By the time they notice, it's too late.
3. **Never rely on fundraising as Plan A.** Investor interest is a function of growth. Even with great growth, fundraising can fail because something spooks investors. Always know precisely what you'll do to survive if you can't raise.
4. **Hiring too fast is by far the biggest killer of funded startups.** Founders tell themselves they need people to grow. But growth usually comes from the product hitting a nerve, not from headcount. And overhiring makes the real problem (weak product-market fit) harder to fix.
5. **Spending and growth are not tightly correlated.** Startups grow fast because the product hits a nerve; they spend a lot because the product is expensive to build or they're wasteful. These are largely independent variables.
6. **VCs push founders to overhire.** Kill-or-cure strategies are optimal for VCs protected by portfolio effects. But founders don't have a portfolio — they have one company, and they need to survive.
7. **Start asking the question too early, not too late.** It's not dangerous to worry about being default dead before it's technically true. It's very dangerous to start worrying too late.

## Notable Quotes

> "The fatal pinch is default dead + slow growth + not enough time to fix it. And the way founders end up in it is by not realizing that's where they're headed."

> "Instead of thinking of the future with vague optimism, explicitly separate the components. Say 'We're default dead, but we're counting on investors to save us.' Maybe as you say that, it will set off the same alarms in your head that it does in mine."

> "Hiring too fast is by far the biggest killer of startups that raise money."

> "When a startup grows fast, it's usually because the product hits a nerve, in the sense of hitting some big need straight on."
