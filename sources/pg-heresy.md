# "Heresy" — Paul Graham (2022)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** April 2022
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Heresy — the idea that holding certain beliefs is morally wrong independent of whether they're true — has returned to Western culture after a long absence. This is surprising and troubling. It conflates empirical questions with moral ones, and it makes the intellectual environment worse by making certain ideas undiscussable. The test for heresy is simple: is the punishment for saying something proportional to its truth value, or to its social transgression value?

## Key Ideas

1. **Heresy is back.** For centuries, Western liberal culture distinguished between wrong beliefs and evil beliefs. Heresy conflates the two: certain ideas are not just wrong, they're wicked to hold. This conflation is back.
2. **The mechanism is category error.** Questions of empirical fact (is X true?) are different from questions of moral character (is the person who says X good?). Heresy blurs this by treating incorrect empirical claims as evidence of bad character.
3. **The test for heresy vs honest error.** If someone who says X is punished more than someone who says something equally wrong but less offensive, that's heresy. The punishment is for the transgression, not the error.
4. **Heresy chills discourse.** When people can be penalised for their beliefs rather than their actions, they learn to self-censor. Self-censorship makes the intellectual environment worse: you don't hear what people actually think, only what they're allowed to say.
5. **Scientists are unusually vulnerable.** Scientists study things that may produce heretical findings. This isn't rare — it's the nature of scientific inquiry. A culture that punishes heresy punishes science.
6. **The defence is distinguishing questions.** Empirical questions and moral questions require different methods. Collapsing them is the error. The defence is refusing to collapse them.

## Notable Quotes

> "One of the most surprising things I've witnessed in my lifetime is the rebirth of the concept of heresy. In the Middle Ages, certain ideas were not merely wrong — they were wicked. That concept is back."

> "The way to tell if you're dealing with heresy rather than honest disagreement is to ask: is the punishment proportional to the error, or to the transgression?"

> "If you're a scientist, you're in the business of finding things out. Some of what you find out will be heretical. That's not a bug — that's the nature of the work."
