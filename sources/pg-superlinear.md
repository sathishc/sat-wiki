# "Superlinear Returns" — Paul Graham (2023)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** October 2023
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

One of the most important and under-appreciated truths about the world: **returns for performance are superlinear, not linear.** "You get out what you put in" is almost never true. Small differences in quality create enormous differences in outcome — because of exponential growth and threshold effects. This has profound implications for how to live and what to work on.

## Key Ideas

1. **Two fundamental causes of superlinear returns: exponential growth and thresholds.** Things that grow exponentially (startups, knowledge, audiences) reward being ahead. Threshold effects ("winner take all") mean the difference between first and second place can be huge even when the performance gap is small.
2. **If your product is half as good, you don't get half the customers.** You get no customers and go out of business. The returns aren't proportional — they're dramatically asymmetric.
3. **At the far end of the curve, incremental effort is a bargain.** When returns are superlinear, going from very good to exceptional yields disproportionate rewards. And fewer people compete there — both because it's hard and because the prospect is intimidating.
4. **Learning compounds.** The more you know, the faster you can learn more. This is superlinear returns applied to knowledge. Early investment in skills pays off at an exponential rate over time.
5. **The implications for ambition:** In a world with superlinear returns, knowing what you're genuinely interested in is more valuable than ever. Mere diligence on the wrong thing doesn't get you to the far end of the curve.
6. **The democratization of superlinear returns.** Institutions used to mediate access to the activities with the highest returns. The internet and modern infrastructure increasingly let individuals bypass institutions and compete directly on merit.
7. **The recipe:** Choose work you have natural aptitude for and deep interest in. Work as hard as you can without burning out. Get to the frontier. Notice gaps. Take as much risk as you can afford. When in doubt, follow your curiosity.

## Notable Quotes

> "One of the most important things I didn't understand about the world when I was a child is the degree to which the returns for performance are superlinear."

> "If your product is only half as good as your competitor's, you don't get half as many customers. You get no customers, and you go out of business."

> "At the far end of the curve, incremental effort is a bargain. All the more so because there's less competition at the far end — and not just for the obvious reason that it's hard to do something exceptionally well, but also because people find the prospect so intimidating that few even try."

> "When in doubt, follow your curiosity. It never lies, and it knows more than you do about what's worth paying attention to."
