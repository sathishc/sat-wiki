# "Early Work" — Paul Graham (2020)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** October 2020
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

The biggest obstacle to great work is the **fear of making something lame**. This fear is not irrational — great projects really do go through an early stage where they don't seem impressive, even to their creators. The crucial insight is that **early work is a special case that deserves different treatment**: not lower standards, but a complete change of perspective — from listing reasons an idea won't work to trying to think of ways it could.

## Key Ideas

1. **All great projects go through an embarrassing early phase.** The ideas look rough and unimpressive. The creator is aware of all their flaws. This is normal, not a sign that the project is bad.
2. **Most people don't even reach the embarrassing stage.** They're too frightened to start. They judge the early version by finished-work standards and conclude it's not worth pursuing.
3. **The right response to new ideas:** Not lower standards, but polarity reversal. Instead of listing reasons an idea won't work, try to think of ways it could. Silicon Valley has learned this — unknown people with strange-sounding ideas are given benefit of the doubt because the cost of dismissing them is too high.
4. **The habits holding people back are not deeply rooted.** Making new things is itself a new thing for humans. We haven't developed good customs for dealing with early-stage work. This means we can change.
5. **Taking early work seriously is itself a skill.** It requires seeing the germ of something promising in rough form, and nurturing it rather than dismissing it. The people who are good at this — early investors, certain mentors, certain creative environments — provide enormous value.
6. **The creative courage to be embarrassed.** The willingness to show bad early work, to be thought foolish in the short term, is prerequisite to eventually producing good work. You can't get to the great version without going through the bad version.

## Notable Quotes

> "One of the biggest things holding people back from doing great work is the fear of making something lame. And this fear is not an irrational one."

> "The right way to deal with new ideas is to treat them as a challenge to your imagination — not just to have lower standards, but to switch polarity entirely, from listing the reasons an idea won't work to trying to think of ways it could."

> "Most people don't even reach the stage of making something they're embarrassed by, let alone continue past it. They're too frightened even to start."
