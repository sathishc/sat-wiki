# "How to Write Usefully" — Paul Graham (2020)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** February 2020
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

The standard advice — write to persuade — sets the bar too low. A better goal is to write usefully: to produce ideas that are simultaneously correct, novel, important, and strongly stated. Most writing fails not because it's wrong but because it's weak: vague enough to be technically true but not specific enough to be actionable or memorable. The recipe is to say something you believe is true that most people don't already know, stated as directly as possible.

## Key Ideas

1. **Useful = (correct × important × novel) × strength.** All four factors multiply. A correct, important, novel idea written weakly is almost as useless as a wrong one. Vagueness is the enemy — it's a way of hedging that preserves formal correctness while draining practical value.
2. **Novelty is relative to the reader.** An idea that's old news to experts may be completely new to a non-expert reader. What matters is whether the idea is new to the audience you're trying to help.
3. **Correctness is a floor, not a ceiling.** Being technically correct isn't enough. Academic writing often fails here: it's carefully hedged into correctness but practically useless because the hedges remove all informational content.
4. **Strength requires commitment.** You have to actually take a position. Weasel words ("it could be argued," "some have suggested") are a tax on the reader — they make them work harder for less payoff.
5. **Importance is about stakes.** Write about things that matter to your reader. If you're writing about something that doesn't affect anyone's life or work, the essay may be technically fine and practically worthless.
6. **The test: would someone who reads this do something differently?** If not, the writing may be correct but not useful.

## Notable Quotes

> "What should an essay be? Many people would say persuasive. But I think we can aim for something more ambitious: that an essay should be useful."

> "The recipe for useful writing: correct + novel + important + strong. These are like nutrients — you need all of them."

> "Vagueness is a polite word for cowardice in writing. Saying 'it depends' or 'there are many perspectives' is technically unchallengeable and practically worthless."
