# *Zero to One* — Peter Thiel & Blake Masters (2014)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Authors:** Peter Thiel with Blake Masters
- **Origin:** Stanford CS183 course notes (2012), edited into book form
- **Published:** 2014, Crown Business
- **Genre:** Startup strategy / contrarian business philosophy

## Central Argument

The path to building a truly valuable company is to create something *new* (Zero to One), not to copy what already works (One to N). New creation requires finding a **secret** — a truth that most people don't yet believe — and building a **monopoly** around it. Competition destroys value; monopoly creates it.

## Key Ideas

1. **Zero to One vs One to N** — vertical progress (invention) vs horizontal progress (copying)
2. **The Founding Question** — "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"
3. **Competition is for losers** — perfect competition destroys margins and prevents long-term investment
4. **The four monopoly characteristics** — proprietary tech (10× better), network effects, economies of scale, branding
5. **Last mover advantage** — it's not about being first; it's about being last and durable
6. **The power law** — venture returns follow power law; so do startup strategies and career choices
7. **Secrets** — great companies are built on secrets; the world is still full of them
8. **Definite vs Indefinite Optimism** — the rarity and value of having a concrete plan for the future
9. **The founding team** — complementarity, trust, and the danger of conflict at the top
10. **The sales problem** — distribution is underrated; great products need great sales

## The Contrarian Frame

Every chapter of *Zero to One* is structured as an inversion of received wisdom:
- "Competition is good" → Competition destroys value; seek monopoly
- "Be a first mover" → Be the last mover
- "Diversify your portfolio" → Concentrate on the power-law winner
- "Keep options open" → Have a definite plan
