# "Be Good" — Paul Graham (2008)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** April 2008
- **Origin:** Talk at Startup School 2008; expanded to essay

## Central Argument

"Make something people want" + "don't worry too much about the business model" = a description of a charity. This isn't a bug — it's a discovery. **Being genuinely good to users is a powerful and underrated startup strategy**, not just an ethical stance. It improves morale, attracts great people, builds goodwill, and — crucially — provides a reliable compass when you have a thousand decisions to make.

## Key Ideas

1. **The surprising logic of benevolence.** If you "make something people want" and "don't worry about the business model," you've described a nonprofit. But many of the most successful startups (Google, Craigslist) looked like charities in the beginning. The reverse is also true: many good nonprofit projects would make great startups.
2. **Benevolence improves morale.** Startups are emotional roller-coasters. What keeps founders working when everything looks doomed? Caring about users. If people need you, you feel obligated to keep going. The tamagotchi effect: once you have users to take care of, you're forced to figure out what makes them happy.
3. **It makes other people want to help you.** Investors, customers, potential employees — people rally around founders who are clearly trying to do good. The best hackers want to work on things that improve the world; benevolent startups attract them.
4. **It's a compass.** With a thousand choices, a stateless algorithm is invaluable. "Do whatever's best for your users" is such an algorithm. You don't have to remember anything else. It's like telling the truth vs. lying — lying requires tracking everything you've said; the truth is self-consistent.
5. **You grow big by being nice, but you can stay big by being mean.** Microsoft and IBM are examples of companies that were Robin Hood-like in the beginning, then became predatory. Their decline tracks this shift.
6. **"Don't just not be evil. Be good."** The difference matters. Not being evil is a constraint; being good is a direction.

## Notable Quotes

> "Make something people want. Don't worry too much about making money. What you've got is a description of a charity."

> "If you feel you're really helping people, you'll keep working even when it seems like your startup is doomed."

> "Here's the answer: Do whatever's best for your users. You can hold onto this like a rope in a hurricane, and it will save you if anything can."

> "Don't just not be evil. Be good."
