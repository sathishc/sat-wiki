# *What You Do Is Who You Are* — Ben Horowitz (2019)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Ben Horowitz
- **Published:** 2019, HarperBusiness
- **Genre:** Leadership / organisational culture

## Central Argument

Culture is not what you say. It's not your values poster, your all-hands deck, or your mission statement. **Culture is what people do when you're not in the room** — the behaviours your organisation actually rewards, tolerates, and punishes. Building culture means changing those three things deliberately.

## Key Ideas

1. **Culture = behaviour, not declaration** — the stated values are irrelevant; the lived behaviours define the culture
2. **Historical case studies** — Horowitz draws on Toussaint Louverture (Haitian Revolution), Genghis Khan, Shaka Senghor, and samurai bushido as unconventional models of culture creation under extreme conditions
3. **Shocking rules** — specific, counterintuitive norms that encode values behaviourally (e.g. "No brilliant jerks" — stated simply and enforced visibly)
4. **Actions > words** — what the CEO *does* in ambiguous situations defines what the culture believes; every decision is a culture transmission
5. **Inclusion** — culture that excludes people who look or think differently is fragile; the historical case studies all built resilient cultures by deliberately incorporating outsiders

## The Horowitz Culture Test

Ask: *"What would a new employee conclude about what we value, based only on what they observe — not what we tell them?"*

That answer is your actual culture. If it differs from your stated culture, you have work to do.

## Connection to *Hard Thing*

*What You Do Is Who You Are* is the peacetime complement to *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*. Hard Thing is about surviving; this book is about building the culture that will make survival possible when the next crisis comes.
