# "How to Start Google" — Paul Graham (2024)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** March 2024
- **Origin:** Talk given to 14 and 15 year olds about what to do now if they might want to start a startup later

## Central Argument

The way to start a company like Google is not to aim at starting a company like Google — it's to develop your skills and your taste by working on projects that genuinely interest you. **The best preparation for starting a startup is not studying startups; it's becoming really good at something.** The path runs through projects, curiosity, and technical depth — not business planning.

## Key Ideas

1. **Starting a company is the standard way to get really rich.** Almost all the people on the Forbes list got there by starting their own companies. This is a factual statement about where wealth comes from now.
2. **You can't know if your idea will be Google.** Larry Page and Sergey Brin didn't know. What you can do is pursue ideas that seem worth pursuing and develop the skills to execute on them.
3. **The path runs through projects.** Facebook, Apple, Google — all began as projects, not companies. Projects are both the best way to learn and the best source of startup ideas. An idea that turns into a project that turns into a company is the standard path.
4. **Get good at something, then look for problems.** The best startup ideas come from noticing problems that you're uniquely positioned to solve because of what you know. Mark Zuckerberg noticed Harvard's paper facebook was stupid because he was a programmer who could fix it.
5. **Work on things that seem important and not many people are working on.** This is the classic contrarian bet. Ideas that everyone agrees are good already have competition. The valuable ones look weird or premature.
6. **The counterintuitive insight for young people:** Don't try to figure out what investors will fund. Get interested in something, work on it seriously, and build things. The startup ideas and the investor interest will follow.
7. **School teaches you to perform; startups require you to actually do things.** The habits of gaming metrics and tests are counterproductive. Building real things is what matters.

## Notable Quotes

> "The companies at the Google end of the continuum are called startups when they're young."

> "Projects aren't just the best way to learn about technology. They're also the best source of startup ideas."

> "Work on things that seem important and that not many people are working on."
