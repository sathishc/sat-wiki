# "Inequality and Risk" — Paul Graham (2005)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** August 2005
- **Origin:** Talk at Defcon 2005, expanded into essay

## Central Argument

Economic inequality and risk-taking are structurally connected. A society that wants to eliminate inequality must also accept less risk-taking — and therefore less innovation. The people who take the biggest economic risks (startup founders) also capture the most upside when they succeed. You can't have the latter without allowing the former. Attempts to compress the wealth distribution without understanding this mechanism will inadvertently suppress the risk-taking that creates economic dynamism.

## Key Ideas

1. **Two ways to reduce inequality, same consequence.** Give to the poor (requires taking from the rich) or take from the rich (makes risk less attractive). Both reduce the expected return on risk-taking.
2. **Wealth correlates with risk taken.** The very rich are mostly people who took large risks — startup founders, early employees, investors in early-stage companies. This is not coincidence.
3. **Startups are the mechanism.** The reason tech produces so much inequality is that startups are a risk-amplification machine: small probability of enormous payoff. That structure produces very unequal outcomes but also produces enormous value.
4. **The tax on risk is real.** Progressive taxation is effectively a tax on successful risk-taking. This doesn't mean it's wrong, but it means there's a real tradeoff that policymakers must acknowledge.
5. **Benign vs malign inequality.** Not all inequality is the same. Inequality produced by creating value (new companies, new products) is structurally different from inequality produced by rent-seeking or extraction.
6. **Comparison to evolution.** A society that allows variance (different outcomes for different choices) explores the fitness landscape. One that minimises variance converges to a local optimum and stops exploring.

## Notable Quotes

> "I think we'll find that 'eliminate economic inequality' doesn't mean what it sounds like. If you have a society with low economic inequality, that probably means less variance in outcomes — which means less risk-taking, which means less innovation."

> "The people who are getting rich are mostly people who are taking risks. Which means if you make the rich less rich, you also make the risk less attractive."

> "Startups are a mechanism for taking a high-risk, high-reward bet. That structure produces very unequal outcomes — and also produces enormous value."
