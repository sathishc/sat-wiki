# "Good and Bad Procrastination" — Paul Graham (2005)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** December 2005
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Procrastination is unavoidable — you can't work on everything simultaneously. The question is not whether to procrastinate but **how to procrastinate well**. There is good procrastination (putting off small things to work on big things) and bad procrastination (putting off big things to work on small things or nothing). The most impressive people are often the worst procrastinators in the conventional sense — because they're too focused on important problems to attend to errands.

## Key Ideas

1. **Three types of procrastination.** You can avoid working by doing: (a) nothing, (b) something less important, or (c) something more important. Type C — doing something more important — is good procrastination.
2. **The absent-minded professor is type C.** He forgets to shave and eat because he's focused on something important. He is procrastinating on errands in order to do real work. This is the right kind.
3. **"Small stuff" = what will never appear in your obituary.** Errands, administrative tasks, routine communications. These have a zero (or near-zero) expected value on what you'll accomplish in life. Don't prioritize them over real work.
4. **The impressive people are bad at errands.** "The mildest seeming people, if they want to do real work, all have a certain degree of ruthlessness when it comes to avoiding errands." This ruthlessness is a feature, not a bug.
5. **The real danger is type B procrastination.** Doing something less important (busy-work, low-stakes tasks) feels productive but isn't. It's the worst kind because it fools you into feeling like you're working while avoiding the important thing.
6. **What is "big stuff"?** Roughly: the kind of work you'd be proud of. The kind that makes a real difference. The kind that is hard and uncertain and requires sustained focus.

## Notable Quotes

> "The most impressive people I know are all terrible procrastinators. So could it be that procrastination isn't always bad?"

> "There are three variants of procrastination, depending on what you do instead of working on something: you could work on (a) nothing, (b) something less important, or (c) something more important. That last type, I'd argue, is good procrastination."

> "What's 'small stuff?' Roughly, work that has zero chance of being mentioned in your obituary."

> "The mildest seeming people, if they want to do real work, all have a certain degree of ruthlessness when it comes to avoiding errands."
