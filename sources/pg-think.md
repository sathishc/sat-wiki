# "How to Think for Yourself" — Paul Graham (2020)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Author:** Paul Graham
- **Published:** November 2020
- **Origin:** paulgraham.com essay

## Central Argument

Some kinds of work require you to think differently from your peers — to be not just correct, but **novel**. Scientists, investors, startup founders, and essayists all need independent-mindedness to succeed. This essay explains what independent-mindedness is, who has it, and how to cultivate it.

## Key Ideas

1. **There's a sharp distinction between work that requires independent thinking and work that doesn't.** An administrator needs to be right; a scientist needs to be both right and novel. If you want to do the former kind of work, conventional-mindedness is a minor handicap. If you want to do the latter, it's fatal.
2. **Independent-mindedness is partly innate, partly cultivated.** Some people are naturally more skeptical of received wisdom. But everyone can cultivate it — by reading broadly, by spending time with independent thinkers, by paying attention to when you feel social pressure to believe something.
3. **Markers of independent-mindedness:** Being contrarian on the right things (not just for the sake of it). Finding heterodox ideas interesting rather than threatening. Being willing to disagree with authority figures. Noticing when you're being pushed to believe something by social pressure rather than evidence.
4. **The mistake to avoid: being conventionally minded while doing work that requires independent thinking.** Conventional-mindedness is comfortable and socially rewarded. Independent-mindedness is uncomfortable and socially risky. This means people who need to be independent often default to being conventional unless they deliberately fight it.
5. **To cultivate independent-mindedness:** Seek out heterodox ideas. Notice when you feel the urge to dismiss something without actually examining it. Spend time with people who aren't afraid to disagree. Read widely and from unconventional sources.
6. **The value is both practical and intrinsic.** Independent thinkers tend to do better work in fields that reward novelty. But independent thinking is also intrinsically valuable — it's the foundation of intellectual integrity.

## Notable Quotes

> "There are some kinds of work that you can't do well without thinking differently from your peers. To be a successful scientist, for example, it's not enough just to be correct. Your ideas have to be both correct and novel."

> "You don't want to start a startup to do something that everyone agrees is a good idea, or there will already be other companies doing it. You have to do something that sounds to most other people like a bad idea, but that you know isn't."

> "If you want to do the kind of work where it's essential to be independent-minded, you need to know that about yourself, and act accordingly."
