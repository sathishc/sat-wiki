# Systems Thinking Primer

A navigational map of the systems thinking content in this wiki. These frameworks answer: *why do problems persist despite good intentions, and where do you actually intervene?*

---

## The Core Premise

> **"Structure influences behaviour. Different people in the same structure tend to produce qualitatively similar results."** — Peter Senge

Most problem-solving focuses on events and actors: *what happened?* *who caused it?* Systems thinking shifts the question: *what structure is producing this behaviour — and what would have to change to produce a different behaviour?*

This reframe is simultaneously more humbling (the person isn't the problem) and more powerful (you can actually fix the structure).

## The Two Loop Types

**Reinforcing loops** amplify change in the same direction. They produce growth — or collapse.

Real examples in this wiki:
- [Amazon Growth Flywheel](../concepts/amazon-growth-flywheel.md) — the canonical business reinforcing loop
- [Compounding](../concepts/compounding.md) — wealth, knowledge, and skill all compound through reinforcing loops
- [Social Proof](../concepts/social-proof.md) — more adopters → more visible adoption → more adopters
- [Disruptive Innovation](../concepts/innovators-dilemma.md) — the disruptor's loop grows silently while the incumbent focuses elsewhere

**Balancing loops** oppose change and seek equilibrium. They produce stability — or oscillation when delayed.

Real examples:
- [WIP Limits](../concepts/wip-limits.md) — impose a balancing loop on the work system
- [OKR review cadence](../concepts/okrs.md) — course-correct toward the goal
- Price mechanisms, thermostats, predator-prey dynamics

**The key:** Most interesting system behaviour comes from *multiple loops interacting* at different time scales.

## The Six Archetypes

Recognising the pattern lets you predict the trajectory and find the right intervention:

| Archetype | Signature | You're in this if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Limits to Growth** | Growth slows despite pushing harder | You keep adding resources but results plateau |
| **Shifting the Burden** | Quick fixes accumulate; root cause worsens | You keep solving the same problem |
| **Tragedy of the Commons** | Shared resource degrades despite rational individual behaviour | Everyone is doing the right thing individually but the system is getting worse |
| **Escalation** | Two parties ratchet past rational limits | A conflict keeps intensifying despite neither party wanting it to |
| **Success to the Successful** | Winner-takes-all concentration | The already-successful keeps getting more resources |
| **Eroding Goals** | Standards drift downward | "Good enough" keeps being redefined down |

See [System Archetypes](../concepts/system-archetypes.md) for diagnosis and interventions.

## Leverage Points: Where to Intervene

Meadows' hierarchy (12 = least effective, 1 = most effective):

```
12. Numbers/parameters          ← where most policy intervenes
11. Buffer sizes
10. Physical structure
 9. Delays                      ← shortening feedback loops (high value)
 8. Balancing loop strength
 7. Reinforcing loop gain
 6. Information flow structure  ← who gets what data (very high value)
 5. Rules                       ← incentives and constraints
 4. Power to change rules
 3. Goals                       ← what the system optimises for
 2. Paradigm/worldview          ← the assumptions behind the system
 1. Power to transcend paradigms
```

**The political paradox:** The highest-leverage interventions are the most resisted. Most management acts on Level 12; the real work is Level 6 and above.

**Applied examples from this wiki:**
- [Radical Transparency](../concepts/radical-transparency.md) = Level 6 (change information flow structure)
- [OKRs](../concepts/okrs.md) = Level 5/3 (change rules + goals)
- [Westrum culture change](../concepts/westrum-cultural-typologies.md) = Level 2 (change paradigm)
- [WIP limits](../concepts/wip-limits.md) = Level 8 (strengthen balancing loop)
- [Batch size reduction](../concepts/batch-size.md) = Level 9 (shorten delay)

## Systems Thinking Across the Wiki

Systems thinking is the framework that *connects* many other frameworks:

**Reinertsen (Flow):** Product development flow is applied systems dynamics. WIP is a stock; throughput is a flow; the [utilisation trap](../concepts/queues-in-product-development.md) is a reinforcing loop. [Little's Law](../concepts/littles-law.md) is a stock-flow equation.

**Christensen (Disruption):** The [Innovator's Dilemma](../concepts/innovators-dilemma.md) is the "Success to the Successful" archetype. The incumbent's loop (best customers → most investment) and the disruptor's loop (ignored customers → new market) run in parallel until the disruptor's loop overtakes.

**Cialdini (Influence):** [Social proof](../concepts/social-proof.md) and [commitment](../concepts/commitment-and-consistency.md) are reinforcing loops in human social behaviour. [Escalation](../concepts/system-archetypes.md) (the archetype) is what happens when reciprocity runs without a balancing constraint.

**Taleb (Antifragility):** [Black Swans](../concepts/black-swan.md) are the collapse of a reinforcing loop that has been accumulating silently. [Antifragility](../concepts/antifragility.md) is a design response: build systems that gain when the loop breaks.

**Westrum (Culture):** Organisational culture is an information processing architecture. A [generative culture](../concepts/westrum-cultural-typologies.md) has fast, undistorted feedback loops. A pathological culture has systematically corrupted ones.

**Amazon Flywheel:** The flywheel Bezos drew on a napkin is a reinforcing feedback loop diagram. Every moat is a reinforcing loop that hasn't hit its constraint yet.

## The Systems Thinker's Questions

When facing any persistent problem, ask:

1. **What are the stocks?** What is accumulating or depleting?
2. **What are the flows?** What is changing the stocks?
3. **What feedback loops are active?** Which are reinforcing? Which are balancing?
4. **Where are the delays?** How long between action and feedback?
5. **What archetype does this resemble?** Limits to Growth? Shifting the Burden?
6. **What leverage point am I acting on?** Am I adjusting a number (Level 12) when I should be changing an information flow (Level 6)?
7. **What is the system's goal?** Is it the stated goal, or the goal revealed by actual behaviour?
