# Wealth & Money

A navigational map of the wealth, money, and personal finance content in this wiki. These frameworks answer three questions: *how do you build wealth? how do you keep it? and what is it actually for?*

---

## The Right Question

Most financial advice answers: *"How do I get more money?"*

The better question is: **"What is money actually for?"**

Housel's answer: **freedom** — control over your time and choices. Naval's answer: **specific knowledge → leverage → financial freedom** so you never have to do anything you don't want to do.

Once you have the right question, the frameworks snap into place.

## The Wealth Building Stack

```
Define what "enough" looks like for you
    ↓
Build Specific Knowledge (the input that earns outsized returns)
    ↓
Apply Leverage (multiply the output of that knowledge)
    ↓
Invest the surplus: stay in the game, don't interrupt compounding
    ↓
Manage the psychology: Reasonable > Rational; Room for Error; tail events
    ↓
Spend well: buy time and freedom, not status
    ↓
Stop at enough (the goalpost can always move; choose not to let it)
```

## The Housel / Taleb Overlap

Both Housel and Taleb are fundamentally about **surviving long enough to benefit from rare positive events:**

| Concept | Housel framing | Taleb framing |
|---|---|---|
| Tail events | A few big wins drive all returns | Black Swans; Extremistan |
| Room for error | Buffer larger than your model says | Margin of safety; avoid ruin |
| Reasonable vs rational | Stick to what you can psychologically hold | Barbell: safe + asymmetric upside |
| Compounding | Time is the master variable | Antifragility benefits from time and volatility |

The Taleb version is more formal and philosophical. The Housel version is more accessible and behavioural. Both reach the same place: **protect your downside absolutely; give yourself maximum exposure to positive tails.**

## The Housel / Naval Overlap

| Concept | Housel | Naval |

| What money is for | Freedom / control over time | Freedom from having to do things you don't want to do |

They're complementary: Naval tells you how to get there; Housel tells you how to behave once you're building and what to do when you arrive.

## Common Mistakes, Diagnosed

**"I earn well but never feel financially ahead."**
→ [Wealth vs Rich](../concepts/wealth-vs-rich.md): you may be spending income rather than converting it to assets. Wealth is what you *don't* spend.

**"I keep changing my investment strategy."**
→ [Reasonable vs Rational](../concepts/reasonable-vs-rational.md): you may be using a theoretically optimal strategy you can't psychologically maintain. Find something you can stick to.

**"I always feel like I need more money before I can relax."**
→ [Enough (Housel)](../concepts/enough-housel.md): the goalpost moves. Define enough deliberately, or it will never arrive.

**"I got wiped out in a market crash."**
→ [Room for Error](../concepts/room-for-error.md): insufficient buffer. The model said you were fine; the unexpected event wasn't in the model.

**"I'm wealthy but I'm not happy."**
→ [Art of Spending Money](../concepts/art-of-spending-money.md) + [Happiness](../concepts/happiness.md): wealth enables freedom; it doesn't automatically produce it. Are you spending on things that actually improve your life, or hoarding unnecessarily?

**"I spend a lot but it never feels satisfying."**
→ [Art of Spending Money](../concepts/art-of-spending-money.md): you're likely spending on status/things rather than time/experiences/freedom. The returns on status spending are low; the returns on time-buying are high.

## Paul Graham on Wealth

Graham's wealth essays are empirical and structural: he's observed hundreds of companies and thousands of founders, and he writes about wealth as a by-product of creating value rather than as a goal in itself. They complement Housel (psychology and behaviour) and Naval (mechanism and philosophy).

| Essay | Core insight |
|---|---|
| [How People Get Rich Now](../sources/pg-richnow.md) | Inheritance dominated in 1982; startups dominate in 2020. Tech collapsed capital requirements; founders now hold the leverage |
| [Inequality and Risk](../sources/pg-inequality.md) | Economic inequality is the footprint of risk-taking; you can't reduce one without reducing the other |
| [What the Bubble Got Right](../sources/pg-bubble.md) | The dot-com bubble was wrong on price and timing but right on direction; the web did change everything |

### Graham / Naval overlap on wealth

Both Graham and Naval see startup founding as the primary mechanism for wealth creation in the technology era:

| Question | Graham | Naval |
|---|---|---|
| How do people get rich now? | Start a company that creates value | Specific knowledge + leverage + equity |
| What changed? | Technology collapsed startup costs to near zero | Code and media give zero-marginal-cost leverage |
| What's the mechanism? | Compressing decades of value creation into years | Assets that earn while you sleep |
