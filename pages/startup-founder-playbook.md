# Startup & Founder Playbook

A navigational map of the startup strategy and founder leadership content in this wiki. These frameworks answer two questions: *how do you build a company worth building?* (Thiel) and *what do you do when it's trying to kill you?* (Horowitz).

---

## The Two Essential Reads for Founders

> **[Peter Thiel](../people/peter-thiel.md)** tells you what kind of company to build and why.
> **[Ben Horowitz](../people/ben-horowitz.md)** tells you what it actually feels like to build it.

These books are complementary, not competing. Thiel operates at the level of business theory and strategy. Horowitz operates at the level of operational reality and psychological truth. A founder who has read only Thiel knows what to aim for but may be blindsided by the human difficulty. A founder who has read only Horowitz knows how to survive but may be building the wrong thing.

## Part 1: What to Build — Peter Thiel

### The Founding Question

Before writing a business plan, answer this:

> **"What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"**

A great startup answer has the form: *"Most people believe X. I believe not-X, and here's why it will be true."* This is a [secret](../concepts/secrets-thiel.md) — a still-undiscovered truth about the world or about what people want. All great companies are built on one.

### The Strategic Framework Map

| Framework | Core insight |
|---|---|
| [Zero to One vs One to N](../concepts/zero-to-one.md) | Create something new (vertical progress) vs. copying what works (horizontal progress) |
| [Monopoly vs Competition](../concepts/monopoly-vs-competition.md) | Aim for monopoly; competition destroys value; start small, dominate completely |
| [Last Mover Advantage](../concepts/last-mover-advantage.md) | Not first mover — *last* mover; 70–80% of value in year 10+ cash flows |
| [Power Law (Startups)](../concepts/power-law-startups.md) | One outcome dominates; applies to VC funds, company strategy, individual career choices |
| [Definite vs Indefinite Optimism](../concepts/definite-vs-indefinite.md) | Have a specific plan; "things will work out" is not a strategy |
| [Secrets (Thiel)](../concepts/secrets-thiel.md) | The world is under-explored; great companies begin with truths others don't yet believe |

### The Monopoly Building Sequence

```
Find a secret
    ↓
Start with a small, ownable market
    ↓
Build proprietary tech (10× better, not incremental)
    ↓
Layer in network effects and/or economies of scale
    ↓
Establish brand
    ↓
Expand to adjacent markets from position of strength
    ↓
Last mover: define the category so no one can displace you
```

### Thiel's Four Monopoly Characteristics

| Characteristic | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Proprietary technology** | 10× better than nearest substitute | Google search vs. Excite |
| **Network effects** | More users → more valuable | Facebook, WhatsApp, Visa |
| **Economies of scale** | Fixed costs spread; marginal cost → 0 | Software, platforms |
| **Branding** | Deep customer identity/loyalty | Apple |

Durable monopolies typically have 2–4 of these. One alone is fragile.

## Part 2: How to Lead — Ben Horowitz

### The Honest Truth About Being CEO

Most business books are written by successful people in hindsight, after the difficulty has been sanitised into lessons. Horowitz writes from inside the difficulty. His central contribution: **naming the Struggle**.

> The Struggle is not a phase you pass through. It is the ongoing condition of building something real under uncertainty.

### The Leadership Framework Map

| Framework | Core insight |
|---|---|
| [Wartime CEO vs Peacetime CEO](../concepts/wartime-vs-peacetime-ceo.md) | Two opposite leadership modes for two opposite contexts; most advice covers only peacetime |
| [The Struggle](../concepts/the-struggle.md) | Psychological reality of crisis leadership; naming it normalises it |
| [Lead Bullets](../concepts/lead-bullets.md) | No silver bullet exists; do the hard work |
| [Management Debt](../concepts/management-debt.md) | Organisational shortcuts compound like financial debt; pay it early |

### Are You in Wartime or Peacetime?

**Peacetime indicators:**
- Clear path to market leadership
- Team is growing and energised
- Revenue model is working
- Competition is manageable

**Wartime indicators:**
- Existential threat to the company (financial, competitive, or technical)
- Core assumptions of the business are invalidated
- Team morale is in freefall
- You're not sure the company will survive the year

**The trap:** Using peacetime behaviours (consensus-seeking, inclusive decision-making, OKR cycles) in wartime. It feels civilised. It's slow and potentially fatal.

### Horowitz's Rules for Wartime

1. **Focus relentlessly** — one priority, not five
2. **Communicate urgency without panic** — people need to know the truth, but not in a way that causes them to flee
3. **Make decisions with incomplete information** — waiting for certainty is a luxury you don't have
4. **Protect your best people** — they have options; if they believe you've lost the plot, they leave
5. **Don't confuse activity with progress** — lead bullets require the right work, not just more work

## Part 3: Where Thiel and Horowitz Converge

### Culture is What You Do, Not What You Say

Thiel: *"A startup is a conspiracy to change the world. What unites the team is belief in the secret."*
Horowitz: *"Culture = what people do when you're not in the room."*

Both are saying: culture is behavioural, not declarative. The values poster is irrelevant. What matters is what the organisation actually rewards, tolerates, and punishes — and whether the founding story (the secret, the mission) is still alive in day-to-day behaviour.

### The Founding Team

Thiel dedicates significant attention to founding dynamics: complementarity, trust, shared belief in the secret. A founding team that's aligned on the secret is resilient; one that's misaligned will fracture under the first serious stress.

Horowitz: the first serious stress will come. It always does. The founding team's alignment is what determines whether you survive it.

## Connections to the Rest of the Wiki

### Thiel ↔ Porter
Porter says: choose a good industry, pick a generic strategy, defend it. Thiel says: *escape* competitive industries entirely. These are complementary, not contradictory — [Porter's Five Forces](../concepts/porters-five-forces.md) can help you *identify* where monopoly conditions are achievable; Thiel's framework tells you what to do once you're there.

### Thiel ↔ Moore
Thiel: start with a small niche and dominate it before expanding. Moore: the bowling alley — dominate one segment, use it as a reference to crack the next. Both say: **say no to the big market until you've won the small one.**

### Thiel ↔ Christensen
Christensen: disruptors enter below incumbent radar. Thiel: secrets give you an asymmetric starting position. Both are theories of how to win from behind. Christensen explains the structural opening; Thiel explains the epistemic advantage you need to exploit it.

### Horowitz ↔ Goleman
Horowitz's Wartime/Peacetime maps directly to Goleman's six leadership styles. Wartime demands Commanding and Pacesetting. Peacetime enables Visionary, Coaching, Affiliative, Democratic. The best leaders are fluent in both — which requires the [self-awareness](../concepts/self-awareness.md) to know which mode they're in.

### Horowitz ↔ Westrum
Horowitz describes how culture is built through specific actions and decisions (especially hard ones). Westrum describes what the output looks like (pathological → bureaucratic → generative). Horowitz is the mechanism; Westrum is the diagnostic.

### Horowitz ↔ Doerr
OKRs are a peacetime tool. They require a stable enough context to set quarterly goals, align on key results, and run reviews. In wartime, the OKR cycle is a luxury. Horowitz's operational reality is the constraint within which Doerr's system operates.

## Practical Guide

**"Should I build this company?"**
→ [Secrets (Thiel)](../concepts/secrets-thiel.md): What's your contrarian truth? Is there a secret here?
→ [Monopoly vs Competition](../concepts/monopoly-vs-competition.md): Is there a path to monopoly, or are you entering a crowded commodity space?
→ [Power Law](../concepts/power-law-startups.md): Can this be the power-law outlier in your market?

**"My company is in trouble. What do I do?"**
→ [Wartime CEO](../concepts/wartime-vs-peacetime-ceo.md): Are you behaving like a wartime CEO? Are you moving fast enough?
→ [The Struggle](../concepts/the-struggle.md): Name it. It's real. You're not uniquely broken.
→ [Lead Bullets](../concepts/lead-bullets.md): Stop looking for the clever move. Do the work.

**"How do I build a culture that holds?"**
→ [Secrets (Thiel)](../concepts/secrets-thiel.md): Is the founding secret still alive in the team?
→ [Management Debt](../concepts/management-debt.md): What shortcuts have you taken that are accumulating?
→ [Psychological Safety](../concepts/psychological-safety.md): Can people tell you the truth?

**"How do I think about our market?"**
→ [Zero to One](../concepts/zero-to-one.md): Are you creating something new or copying?
→ [Last Mover Advantage](../concepts/last-mover-advantage.md): What does your cash flow picture look like in year 10?
→ [Crossing the Chasm](../concepts/crossing-the-chasm.md): Where are you on the adoption curve?

## Paul Graham on Startups

Graham's essays are the third essential pillar of startup thinking alongside Thiel and Horowitz — more empirical, more tactical, and grounded in running YC for two decades. Where Thiel asks *what to build* and Horowitz asks *how to survive building it*, Graham asks *what actually happens when you do*.

| Essay | Core insight |
|---|---|
| [Founder Mode](../sources/pg-foundermode.md) | Founders should manage like founders, not like professional managers; skip-level engagement is a feature, not a bug |
| [Default Alive or Default Dead?](../sources/pg-aord.md) | The question every startup past early stage must answer about burn vs. growth trajectory |
| [The Hardest Lessons for Startups](../sources/pg-startuplessons.md) | Launch early, do what users want, spend little, focus — the full empirical picture from hundreds of YC companies |
| [Be Good](../sources/pg-good.md) | Benevolence is startup strategy, not just ethics; it attracts better people and better decisions |
| [What I've Learned from Users](../sources/pg-users.md) | The actual lessons from funding thousands of startups: what separates the ones that work |
| [Mean People Fail](../sources/pg-mean.md) | Why good founders outperform mean ones — surprising given startup mythology |
| [How to Start Google](../sources/pg-google.md) | What it takes to start a world-changing company; contrarian bets and the willingness to look stupid |
| [Why Startups Condense in America](../sources/pg-america.md) | Why talent density, immigration, and culture create [Compounding](../concepts/compounding.md) advantages for certain startup ecosystems |
| [Startup Investing Trends](../sources/pg-invtrend.md) | How startup financing evolved; the rise of super angels; the Series A crunch |
| [How to Be an Angel Investor](../sources/pg-angelinvesting.md) | How angel investing actually works; what VCs look for and how deals happen |

### Graham ↔ Thiel
Graham's "do what users want" seems to contradict Thiel's "secrets give you monopoly." But they operate at different scales of time: Graham is describing how to get early traction; Thiel is describing where you want to be in year 10. The two are complementary: Graham's iteration engine, aimed at Thiel's destination.

### Graham ↔ Horowitz
Graham's "Founder Mode" is the abstract principle; Horowitz's *Hard Thing About Hard Things* is the lived experience. Graham identifies the pattern; Horowitz shows what it costs. Both argue that conventional management advice fails founders — for the same reason.
