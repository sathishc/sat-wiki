# Ben Horowitz

> "In good times, 'how do I get the most out of my people?' In bad times, 'how do I keep my company alive?'"

## Who He Is

Ben Horowitz (b. 1966) is an American entrepreneur, venture capitalist, and author. Co-founder of Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), one of Silicon Valley's most influential VC firms. Before that, he was CEO of Opsware (née Loudcloud), which he navigated from a dot-com implosion to a successful acquisition by HP for $1.6B.

*The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (2014) is the unsanitised account of what it actually feels like to be a CEO in crisis. Unlike most business books, it doesn't pretend things go cleanly.

## Core Philosophy

Horowitz's worldview is fundamentally about **wartime vs. peacetime** — two completely different modes of leadership that require different people and different behaviours. Most management advice is written for peacetime. Horowitz writes for war.

His deeper insight: **the skills that make a great leader in crisis are not the same as the skills that make a great leader in growth.** Founders need to know which situation they're in, and act accordingly.

## Key Frameworks

- **[Wartime CEO vs Peacetime CEO](../concepts/wartime-vs-peacetime-ceo.md)** — the defining distinction; different contexts require opposite leadership behaviours
- **[The Struggle](../concepts/the-struggle.md)** — Horowitz's name for the psychological reality of being a CEO in crisis; it's real, it's normal, and knowing it's normal helps
- **[One-on-Ones](../concepts/one-on-ones.md)** — structural management tool; the employee's agenda, not the manager's
- **[Lead Bullets](../concepts/lead-bullets.md)** — there is no silver bullet; when the company is in trouble, you have to do the actual hard work
- **[Taking Care of the People](../concepts/taking-care-of-people.md)** — the first obligation of a CEO; the company, the customers, and the shareholders depend on it
- **[Management Debt](../concepts/management-debt.md)** — the operational equivalent of technical debt; shortcuts in management that accrue compounding interest

## The Horowitz Test for Culture

In *What You Do Is Who You Are*, Horowitz argues:

> Culture = what your people do when you're not in the room.

A values poster means nothing. The question is: what behaviour does the organisation actually reward, tolerate, and punish? That *is* your culture. You change culture by changing those three things — not by writing new values.
