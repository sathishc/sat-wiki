# C. K. Prahalad

Coimbatore Krishnarao Prahalad (1941–2010). Indian-American strategy professor at the University of Michigan Ross School of Business. One of the most influential business thinkers of the 20th century — coined "core competency", "strategic intent", and "bottom of the pyramid." Born in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu; educated at Loyola College Chennai, IIM Ahmedabad, and Harvard Business School.

> "The most dangerous moment for a bad company is when it starts to succeed."

Collaborated extensively with Gary Hamel to produce ideas that redefined how companies think about strategy, competition, and the future.

## Major Contributions

| Work | Year | Core Idea |
|---|---|---|
| "Strategic Intent" (HBR, with Hamel) | 1989 | Companies win by setting an ambitious goal and building capabilities toward it — not just reacting to current position |
| "The Core Competence of the Corporation" (HBR, with Hamel) | 1990 | Competitive advantage comes from bundles of skills and technologies, not products or business units |
| *Competing for the Future* (with Hamel) | 1994 | The battle is for future market space — not today's market share; reshape industry, don't just compete in it |
| *The Fortune at the Bottom of the Pyramid* | 2004 | 4 billion people earning <$2/day are a viable market AND a force for innovation — not just aid recipients |
| *The New Age of Innovation* (with Krishnan) | 2008 | Co-creation as the new model; N=1 (personalised) and R=G (global resources) |

## The Strategic Framework

Prahalad and Hamel argued that most companies were fighting the wrong battle. They were:

- Competing to **protect** today's market position
- Optimising existing businesses
- Benchmarking against current competitors
- Asking "what business are we in?"

The correct battle is:
- Competing to **create** tomorrow's market space
- Building capabilities for future opportunities
- Imagining industries that don't yet exist
- Asking "what new benefits can we deliver to customers?"

This reframing — from **portfolio of businesses** to **portfolio of competencies** — is the book's central contribution.

## Tamil Nadu Connection

A note of personal relevance: Prahalad was from Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. His father was a Tamil scholar and judge. He studied at Loyola College Chennai before IIM Ahmedabad and Harvard. He remained deeply connected to India's development throughout his career — his "India@75" vision articulated aspirations for India by 2022.

## Contrast with Other Thinkers in the Wiki

| Dimension | Prahalad | Naval | Dalio | Bezos | Reinertsen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit of analysis** | Corporation / industry | Individual | Organisation | Corporation | Team / product |
| **On competitive advantage** | Core competencies; hard to imitate | [Specific knowledge](../concepts/specific-knowledge.md); cannot be commoditised | Principles-based edge | Flywheel + customer obsession | [Flow Economics](../concepts/flow-economics.md) + speed |
| **On the future** | Compete to shape it; don't just prepare for it | Build [Compounding](../concepts/compounding.md) assets | Model it; stress-test | Day 1; always early | [Cost of Delay](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md); time is money |
| **On resources** | Leverage across business units | Permissionless [leverage](../concepts/leverage.md) | Capital allocation by principles | AWS subsidises retail | Capacity management |
| **On industry** | Reshape the industry; redefine the game | Exit industries with low leverage | Not primary focus | Disrupt from within | Not primary focus |

**Key tension with [Michael Porter](./michael-porter.md):** This is the central debate in strategy scholarship. Porter's "outside-in" view says industry structure determines profitability — choose your industry and position carefully. Prahalad's "inside-out" (resource-based) view says durable [Competitive Advantage](../concepts/competitive-advantage.md) comes from building distinctive internal capabilities ([core competency](../concepts/core-competency.md)) that let you reshape industries rather than merely adapt to them. Porter asks "what industry should we be in?"; Prahalad asks "what capabilities should we build?" Both are right, but Prahalad's view is more useful in dynamic, technology-driven markets where industry boundaries are unstable.

**Key convergence with [Naval](../people/naval-ravikant.md):** Core competency (Prahalad) and [specific knowledge](../concepts/specific-knowledge.md) (Naval) are the same insight at different scales. Both argue the durable source of value is something hard to imitate — built from deep capability, not surface features.

**Key convergence with [Dalio](../people/ray-dalio.md):** Both argue for explicit, systematic thinking about where you want to be in the future, and building the capabilities to get there. Dalio's five-step process and Prahalad's strategic intent share the same structure: define the goal first, then build backward.

## Key Quotes

> "The companies that get to the future first are the companies that are willing to ask: 'Are we going to let someone else create this future, or are we going to create it ourselves?'"

> "A company is not a portfolio of businesses — it is a portfolio of competencies."

> "Stop thinking of the poor as victims or as a burden, and start recognising them as resilient and creative entrepreneurs and value-conscious consumers."

> "Strategy is not about defending what you have but about creating the future."
