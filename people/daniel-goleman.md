# Daniel Goleman

> "In a very real sense we have two minds, one that thinks and one that feels."

## Who He Is

Daniel Goleman (b. 1946) is an American psychologist, science journalist, and bestselling author. A former science writer for the *New York Times*, he popularised the concept of **Emotional Intelligence** in his landmark 1995 book of the same name. Goleman synthesised neuroscience and psychology research — particularly the work of Peter Salovey and John Mayer, who first coined the term — into a framework accessible to the general public and business world.

His work reshaped how organisations think about leadership, hiring, and performance, arguing that EQ matters as much as IQ — often more — in predicting life and career success.

## Core Framework: The Five Domains of EQ

Goleman's original model (1995) broke Emotional Intelligence into five domains:

1. **[Self-Awareness](../concepts/self-awareness.md)** — knowing your own emotions, strengths, weaknesses, drives
2. **[Self-Regulation](../concepts/self-regulation.md)** — managing disruptive emotions and impulses; adaptability
3. **[Motivation](../concepts/intrinsic-motivation.md)** — drive to achieve beyond external rewards
4. **[Empathy](../concepts/empathy.md)** — understanding others' emotional makeup; skill in treating people
5. **[Social Skills](../concepts/social-skills.md)** — managing relationships, building networks, finding common ground

Later (with Boyatzis and McKee in *Primal Leadership*), he reorganised these into **four clusters**: Self-Awareness, Self-Management, Social Awareness, Relationship Management — with 18 competencies mapped to them.

## Why It Matters

- **Star performers** in nearly every field show higher EQ than average performers, independent of IQ
- **Leadership effectiveness** correlates more strongly with EQ competencies than technical skills
- **Neurological basis**: the amygdala (emotional brain) can hijack the prefrontal cortex (rational brain) — "amygdala hijack" — making emotional regulation a literal brain-management skill
- The framework gave organisations a vocabulary for the "soft skills" that were always known to matter but hard to name
