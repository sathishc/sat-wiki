# Daniel Kahneman

Israeli-American psychologist (1934–2024). Winner of the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics — remarkable because he is a psychologist, not an economist. His work with Amos Tversky systematically dismantled the assumption of human rationality that underpinned classical economics, replacing it with an empirically grounded model of how humans actually think and decide.

> "A reliable way to make people believe in falsehoods is frequent repetition, because familiarity is not easily distinguished from truth."

## Core Contribution

Kahneman's central thesis: **human judgment and decision-making are systematically biased**, not just randomly wrong. The biases are predictable, consistent, and exploitable. They arise from the architecture of the mind — specifically from the interaction of two cognitive systems.

His work gave behavioural economics its empirical foundation and produced a vocabulary (System 1/2, loss aversion, anchoring, availability heuristic, planning fallacy, WYSIATI) that has become standard across psychology, economics, management, and policy.

## The Two Systems

The organising framework of *Thinking, Fast and Slow*:

| | [System 1](../concepts/system-1-and-2.md) | [System 2](../concepts/system-1-and-2.md) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fast | Slow |
| Effort | Effortless, automatic | Effortful, deliberate |
| Consciousness | Unconscious | Conscious |
| Character | Intuitive, emotional | Logical, analytical |
| Examples | Reading faces, driving familiar routes, 2+2 | Complex maths, evaluating arguments, planning |
| Errors | Systematic biases | Mistakes from fatigue or inattention |

The critical insight: **System 1 runs almost all the time**. System 2 is lazy — it accepts System 1's outputs unless there is a strong signal to engage. Most of our "thinking" is System 1 pattern-matching with System 2 occasionally rubber-stamping.

## Key Concepts

- **[System 1 and System 2](../concepts/system-1-and-2.md)** — the dual-process architecture
- **[Loss Aversion](../concepts/loss-aversion.md)** — losses feel ~2x worse than equivalent gains feel good
- **[Prospect Theory](../concepts/loss-aversion.md)** — the formal model of decision under risk; replaces rational expected utility
- **[Cognitive Biases](../concepts/cognitive-biases-kahneman.md)** — anchoring, availability heuristic, halo effect, WYSIATI, substitution
- **[Planning Fallacy](../concepts/planning-fallacy.md)** — systematic underestimation of time, cost, and risk; overestimation of benefit
- **[Thinking Noise](../concepts/cognitive-biases-kahneman.md)** — variability in judgments that should be identical

## WYSIATI — What You See Is All There Is

One of Kahneman's most important concepts, not widely known by name. System 1 builds the most coherent story it can from available information — and then acts as if that story is complete. **It does not flag what it doesn't know.**

> "The confidence people have in their beliefs is not a measure of the quality of evidence; it is a measure of the coherence of the story that System 1 has managed to construct."

WYSIATI is the root of overconfidence, jumping to conclusions, and many forms of bias. The mind doesn't say "I lack evidence" — it says "here is my best story" and then feels confident in proportion to the story's coherence, not its accuracy.

## The Experiencing vs Remembering Self

A less-known but profound distinction Kahneman makes:
- **Experiencing self** — who you are moment to moment; feels pain and pleasure in real time
- **Remembering self** — who you are in memory; reconstructs the past with the ending weighted heavily

We make decisions based on the remembering self (what we expect to remember), not the experiencing self (what we will actually feel). This explains why:
- A holiday ending badly feels worse than one ending well, regardless of the middle
- People choose longer procedures if the ending is less painful, even if total pain is higher
- "Peak-end rule" dominates memory: intensity at peak + intensity at end = remembered experience

## Contrast with Other Thinkers

| Dimension | Kahneman | Naval | Dalio | Hazlitt | Taleb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **On human rationality** | Sceptical; biases are systematic | Aspires to clear thinking | Build principles to overcome bias | Fallacies persist despite intelligence | Sceptical; humans misread probability |
| **On intuition** | Unreliable in unpredictable domains; valid only in pattern-rich expert environments | Build genuine [specific knowledge](../concepts/specific-knowledge.md) | Back-test your instincts | Apply the one lesson consistently | Bias toward doing over theorising |
| **On overconfidence** | The planning fallacy; WYSIATI | "Earn trust through competence" | Radical truth; seek disconfirmation | Policies built on visible effects only | Forecasters are charlatans |
| **On System 2** | Engage it deliberately; costly but necessary | [Judgment](./naval-ravikant.md) as a skill | Systematic principles = encoded System 2 | Economics is the discipline of seeing the unseen | — |

**Key convergence with [Dalio](./ray-dalio.md):** Dalio's explicit principles are essentially a System 2 backup system — a set of pre-committed rules that prevent System 1 from hijacking decisions in high-stakes moments. "Are Right, A Lot" (Amazon LP #4) and "seek to disconfirm" are both explicit System 2 disciplines.

**Key convergence with [Hazlitt](./henry-hazlitt.md):** WYSIATI explains *why* the [Broken Window Fallacy](../concepts/broken-window-fallacy.md) is so persistent. System 1 sees the glazier's work (vivid, concrete, immediate) and builds a complete story. The unseen forgone shoes are not in the data, so they don't enter the story. The fallacy is an artefact of System 1 architecture.

**Key convergence with [Taleb](./nassim-taleb.md):** The [availability heuristic](../concepts/cognitive-biases-kahneman.md) explains why people underestimate [Black Swans](../concepts/black-swan.md) — they cannot easily recall examples of unprecedented events.
