# Naval Ravikant

Co-founder and former CEO of AngelList. One of the most influential thinkers on wealth creation, startups, and the philosophy of living well. Known for distilling complex ideas into tweetable aphorisms.

## On Wealth

Naval's core thesis: **wealth is assets that earn while you sleep**. This is distinct from money (medium of exchange) and status (zero-sum game). He argues you should want wealth, not money or status.

### How Wealth Is Created

- Society gets wealthy through science, technology, and leverage
- You get wealthy by giving society what it wants at scale
- You can't be paid well to do what everyone else can do — value comes from **specific knowledge**

### Specific Knowledge

The central concept. Specific knowledge is:
- Found by following your genuine curiosity and passion, not by following the market
- Cannot be taught — if it could, it would be trained into someone else and arbitraged away
- Often looks like play to you but work to others
- Built at the edge of knowledge — reading primary sources, doing things, not copying

> "The internet has massively broadened the possible space of careers. Most people haven't figured this out yet."

### Leverage

Three forms of leverage:
1. **Labour** — other people working for you (oldest, least scalable)
2. **Capital** — money working for you (requires wealth to get wealth)
3. **Code & Media** — products with zero marginal cost of replication (**permissionless leverage** — the new superpower)

The last category — software and content — is the key insight for the modern era. One person can reach millions with code or writing at near-zero cost.

### Long-Term Games

- Compound interest applies to all domains: wealth, relationships, knowledge
- Play with people you trust over long time horizons
- Reputation compounds — be someone others want to play repeated games with
- Short-term thinking is the biggest destroyer of value

### Accountability

- Take on specific public accountability to get leverage
- "Embrace accountability and take business risks under your own name"
- Accountability means credit and blame both fall on you — that's the price of leverage

### Judgment

- Most important skill in the knowledge economy
- Good judgment is built through understanding first principles, not memorising rules
- "The fastest way to ruin your judgment is to over-index on recent events"
- Mental models > tactical knowledge

## On Happiness

Naval's view: happiness is not something to pursue — it is a **default state** that you return to by removing desires and anxiety, not by adding things.

### Core Positions

- **Happiness is learned**, not found — it is a skill you practise
- **Every desire is a chosen unhappiness** — you are signalling to yourself that your present state is insufficient
- Happiness requires **presence** — the mind is either in the past (depression) or future (anxiety); the present is where peace lives
- Success does not earn happiness — you will simply replace one desire with another

### Envy

Envy is called the enemy of happiness. It is uniquely destructive because it offers no upside — you feel bad without gaining anything. Naval's solution: find one person you are not envious of (usually someone who has made very different trade-offs) and use them as your reference point.

### Habits

- Meditation is a core practice — trains the observer, creates distance from thoughts
- Exercise is non-negotiable for mental clarity
- Good mood is the foundation — protect it like an asset

### Choosing Yourself

- Be authentic to your own nature, not a copy of someone else's success
- External validation is a trap — it is a treadmill, not a destination
- "The most important trick to be happy is to realise happiness is a choice you make and a skill you develop"

## On Philosophy

### Rational Buddhism

Naval describes his philosophy as "rational Buddhism" — taking the empirically useful parts of Buddhist insight (impermanence, non-attachment, the nature of desire) while dropping the metaphysical claims. Key ideas:

- The self is a story the mind tells — it is not fixed
- Suffering arises from resisting impermanence
- Acceptance is not passivity; it is clarity about what you can and cannot change

### The Present

- The present moment is all that actually exists — past and future are mental constructs
- Peace comes from accepting the present, not fighting it
- Death awareness is a useful tool: "If you're not scared to die, you're not scared of anything"

## Key Quotes

> "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich."

> "Earn with your mind, not your time."

> "Specific knowledge is knowledge you cannot be trained for. If society can train you, it can train someone else and replace you."

> "The desire for more positive experiences is itself a negative experience. The acceptance of one's negative experience is itself a positive experience."

> "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity."

> "A fit body, a calm mind, a house full of love. These things cannot be bought — they must be earned."
