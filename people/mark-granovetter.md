# Mark Granovetter

American sociologist; professor at Stanford. Best known for his groundbreaking 1973 paper "The Strength of Weak Ties," one of the most cited papers in social science.

## Background

- **PhD:** Harvard (1970)
- **Academic career:** Northwestern, Johns Hopkins, SUNY Stony Brook, Stanford (since 1995)
- **Field:** Economic sociology, social networks, organizational behavior

## Key Contribution: The Strength of Weak Ties (1973)

### The Core Insight

**Weak ties are more valuable than strong ties for accessing new information and opportunities.**

- **Strong ties:** Close friends, family — high trust, frequent contact, emotional intimacy
  - **Problem:** Redundant information. Your close friends know what you know; you're all embedded in the same social network.

- **Weak ties:** Acquaintances, former colleagues, infrequent contacts — lower trust, less frequent interaction
  - **Power:** Bridge structural holes. Weak ties connect different social networks, giving access to **novel information, diverse perspectives, and distant opportunities**.

### The Job Search Finding

Granovetter's empirical research on how people find jobs:
- Most people who found jobs through personal contacts did so through **acquaintances, not close friends**
- Weak ties were more effective because they bridged the job seeker to different networks and organizations
- Your close friends tend to know the same job openings you already know about

**Implication:** The people you barely know are often more valuable for career advancement than your closest friends.

### Structural Holes and Bridges

- **Structural hole:** A gap between two networks that don't communicate
- **Bridge:** A person or tie that connects across the hole
- Weak ties often act as bridges; strong ties are usually within the same dense cluster
- **Brokerage power:** People who bridge structural holes control information flow and have outsized influence

## Why This Matters

### Information Flow

- Information spreads through strong ties quickly but stays within clusters
- Information crosses clusters slowly, through weak ties
- Innovations and ideas often come from outside your immediate network

### Career and Opportunities

- Job openings, business opportunities, and valuable introductions come disproportionately through weak ties
- Your 500 LinkedIn connections matter more than you think
- Maintaining weak ties (conferences, alumni networks, casual check-ins) is high-ROI

### Network Strategy

Granovetter's work inverts the intuitive advice "deepen your closest relationships":
- **Don't just go deeper (strong ties); go wider (weak ties)**
- Attend conferences, join communities, maintain acquaintances
- Diversity of network trumps density of network

## Key Papers

- **"The Strength of Weak Ties" (1973)** — American Journal of Sociology; one of the most cited sociology papers ever
- **"Getting a Job" (1974)** — Book-length study of job search through networks
- **"Economic Action and Social Structure: The Problem of Embeddedness" (1985)** — How economic behavior is embedded in social relationships

## Quotes

> "Those to whom we are weakly tied are more likely to move in circles different from our own and will thus have access to information different from that which we receive."

> "Weak ties are more likely to link members of different small groups than are strong ones, which tend to be concentrated within particular groups."

## Why He Matters in This Wiki

Granovetter provides **the scientific foundation for network-based career strategy**. His work explains:
- Why you should invest in acquaintances, not just close friends
- Why LinkedIn works (it makes weak ties scalable and persistent)
- Why diverse networks beat homogeneous networks
- Why attending conferences and maintaining loose connections has outsized ROI

His insight is not just academic — it's **deeply actionable** and has been validated across decades of research and practice.
