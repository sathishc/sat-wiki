# Frédéric Bastiat

French economist, journalist, and member of the National Assembly (1801–1850). Called "the most brilliant economic journalist who ever lived" by economist Joseph Schumpeter. Died at 49, leaving an outsized intellectual legacy — his core insight underpins [Hazlitt](./henry-hazlitt.md)'s entire book and much of the Austrian School tradition.

## The Central Contribution: Seen vs Unseen

Bastiat's 1850 essay *"That Which Is Seen and That Which Is Not Seen"* (*Ce qu'on voit et ce qu'on ne voit pas*) introduced the framework that became the foundation of economic reasoning about unintended consequences:

> Every economic act has two effects: what is immediately visible (the seen) and what is displaced or foregone (the unseen). Good economics requires reasoning about both.

This is the direct ancestor of [Hazlitt](./henry-hazlitt.md)'s one lesson, and it predates modern opportunity cost theory.

## The Broken Window Parable

His most famous illustration — see [Broken Window Fallacy](../concepts/broken-window-fallacy.md). A shopkeeper's son breaks a window. Bystanders console the shopkeeper: "At least the glazier will get work!" Bastiat's response: you only see the glazier's gain. You don't see what the shopkeeper would have bought with those six francs if the window had not been broken. The destruction creates no net wealth — it redistributes it, with a loss.

## The Law (1850)

Bastiat's other major work. Argues that **law must be restricted to protecting individual rights** — not used to redistribute wealth from one group to another. He called legally-enforced redistribution "legal plunder":

> "When plunder becomes a way of life for a group of men in a society, over the course of time they create for themselves a legal system that authorizes it and a moral code that glorifies it."

## Opportunity Cost

Bastiat conceptualised opportunity cost before it had a formal name in economics: every choice forecloses alternatives; the cost of a choice is what you give up by making it. This framing makes the "unseen" visible — the unseen is always the foregone alternative.

## Connection to Other Thinkers

- **[Taleb](./nassim-taleb.md):** Bastiat's unseen = Taleb's hidden tail risk. Both argue the dominant cost is the one nobody is measuring.
- **[Reinertsen](./donald-reinertsen.md):** Invisible [queues](../concepts/queues-in-product-development.md) are the "unseen" in product development — everyone sees the work being done; nobody measures the work sitting in the queue.
- **[Dalio](./ray-dalio.md):** Second- and third-order consequences. Dalio's five-step process explicitly asks "what are the consequences?" — the Bastiat question formalised.
