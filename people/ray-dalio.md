# Ray Dalio

Founder of Bridgewater Associates — the largest hedge fund in the world. Made money for clients in 23 of 26 years. Author of *Principles: Life & Work* (2017), *Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises*, and *The Changing World Order*. His core contribution: the idea that success comes not from talent but from a **systematic, principle-driven approach** to reality.

> "Principles are ways of successfully dealing with reality to get what you want out of life."

## Background

- Started Bridgewater Associates in 1975 from a two-bedroom New York apartment
- Made an early call that Mexico would default — was right, but had bet wrong, nearly bankrupting himself
- That near-failure became the crucible: he rebuilt by systematising his thinking into explicit, testable principles
- Bridgewater became known for its radical culture: **radical transparency** and **idea meritocracy**

## The Core Framework

Dalio's system rests on a simple but demanding loop:

```
Goals → Problems → Diagnosis → Design → Do It → (repeat)
```

1. **Have clear goals** — know what you want
2. **Identify problems** — don't tolerate them, don't ignore them
3. **Diagnose root causes** — distinguish symptoms from causes; distinguish people problems from process problems
4. **Design solutions** — create plans that address root causes
5. **Execute** — and then do it again

He calls this the **5-Step Process** and considers it the fundamental mechanism of progress for individuals, teams, and organisations.

## Three Types of Principles

| Type | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| **Life Principles** | How to think, learn, make decisions, relate to others |
| **Work Principles** | How to build an organisation, culture, and management system |
| **Market Principles** | How to think about economics, investing, and macro trends |

## Key Ideas

### Radical Truth and Radical Transparency
The two pillars of Dalio's culture at Bridgewater:
- **Radical truth**: surface reality as it is, not as you wish it were. Suppress nothing uncomfortable.
- **Radical transparency**: make information available to all, not filtered by hierarchy. Record meetings. Show your reasoning.

The logic: bad decisions come from distorted information. Transparency eliminates distortion.

### Idea Meritocracy
Not democracy (all opinions equal) or autocracy (one person decides). An idea meritocracy **weights opinions by the demonstrated quality of thinking**. The best idea wins, regardless of who said it or how senior they are.

This is operationalised at Bridgewater through "believability ratings" — people's track records are tracked, and their input is weighted accordingly.

### Embrace Failure as a Teacher
> "I learned that if I worked hard and creatively, I could have just about anything I wanted, but not everything. I had to prioritise."

Dalio's near-bankruptcy early in his career was formative. He reframes failure: not something to be ashamed of, but signal — data about where your mental models are wrong.

### Algorithms for Decisions
One of his most distinctive ideas: **write down your decision criteria as explicit rules, then back-test them against history**. Make your thinking legible. Run it like a system, not a gut feeling.

> "The best thing you can do to improve your thinking is to think through your principles for making decisions, write them out in words and computer algorithms, back-test them if possible."

### Meaningful Work + Meaningful Relationships
His definition of a good life: not wealth or status, but meaningful work (absorbed in something that matters) and meaningful relationships (deep trust with others). Wealth is a byproduct of doing both well.

## Contrast with Naval and Taleb

| Dimension | Dalio | Naval | Taleb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary lens** | Systems + decision-making | [Leverage](../concepts/leverage.md) + specific knowledge | Risk + antifragility |
| **On failure** | Essential data; encode learnings into principles | Failure is fine if you avoid ruin | Embrace volatility; be antifragile |
| **On knowledge** | Write explicit principles; back-test them | Build through curiosity and play | Bias toward doing over theorising |
| **On organisations** | Radical transparency + idea meritocracy | Permissionless leverage (small teams) | Bureaucracies are fragile |
| **On uncertainty** | Model it; stress-test your models | Accept it; focus on what you can build | Benefit from it via antifragility |

**Where all three converge:** Long-term thinking, honest reckoning with reality, accountability, and deep scepticism of conventional wisdom and false certainty.

## Key Quotes

> "My success has more to do with knowing how to deal with not knowing than with anything I know."

> "Embrace the fact that you don't know everything you need to know."

> "Almost everyone expects the future to be a slightly modified version of the present, but it's usually very different."

> "To make money in markets you need to be an independent thinker who bets against the consensus and is right."
