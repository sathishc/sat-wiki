# Jeff Bezos

Founder of Amazon (1994), founder of Blue Origin, executive chairman of Amazon. One of the most consequential business builders of the last 30 years — not primarily as a visionary technologist but as a **systems thinker and culture architect** who encoded operating principles that scaled from 10 people to 1.5 million.

## Day 1 vs Day 2

Bezos's most repeated framework. **Day 1** = startup energy: customer obsession, experimentation, willingness to be misunderstood. **Day 2** = stasis, irrelevance, agonising decline, death.

> "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful decline. Followed by death. And that is why it is always Day 1."

The antidote to Day 2: customer obsession, resist proxies (metrics that substitute for real outcomes), embrace external trends early, make high-velocity decisions.

## Disagree and Commit in Practice

Bezos personally demonstrated "Disagree and Commit" regularly. His standard phrase when overruled or choosing to move forward despite reservations: *"I disagree and commit."* This modelled the principle for the entire organisation.

## High-Velocity Decision-Making

Bezos distinguishes two types of decisions:

- **Type 1 (one-way doors):** Irreversible, high-consequence. Requires careful deliberation.
- **Type 2 (two-way doors):** Reversible, low-consequence. Should be made fast, by small teams, without escalation.

The failure mode: treating Type 2 decisions like Type 1. This causes the "institutional no" — slow, risk-averse organisations that can't move.

This maps directly to [Reinertsen](../people/donald-reinertsen.md)'s [Bias for Action](../concepts/amazon-leadership-principles.md) and [Batch Size](../concepts/batch-size.md): make reversible decisions fast; reserve deliberation for irreversible ones.

## "Working Backwards" — The Press Release Method

Amazon's product development process: before writing a line of code, write the **press release** announcing the finished product as if it were already built. Then write the FAQ. This forces clarity about:
- What problem does this solve?
- Who is the customer?
- What is the customer experience?

Only after this is agreed does engineering begin. It's [Reinertsen](../people/donald-reinertsen.md)'s "start with the economic outcome" applied to product development.

## Contrast with Naval, Taleb, Dalio, Reinertsen

| Dimension | Bezos | Naval | Taleb | Dalio | Reinertsen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | Corporate (millions of employees) | Individual/small team | Individual/portfolio | Corporate (Bridgewater) | Team/organisation |
| **On risk** | Type 1 vs Type 2; most decisions reversible | Avoid ruin; [leverage](../concepts/leverage.md) | [Antifragile](../concepts/antifragility.md); embrace volatility | Model and stress-test | [Batch size](../concepts/batch-size.md); reduce irreversibility |
| **On culture** | Explicit principles as operating mechanisms | Intrinsic; curiosity-driven | Not primary focus | [Radical transparency](../concepts/radical-transparency.md) | Decentralised control |
| **On long-term** | Day 1; sacrifice margin for flywheel | [Compounding](../concepts/compounding.md) | [Lindy Effect](../concepts/lindy-effect.md) | Principles compound over time | [Cost of Delay](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md) |

**Where all five converge:** Unconventional long-term thinking, intellectual honesty over comfort, accountability, and systems that encode learning rather than depending on heroic individuals.
