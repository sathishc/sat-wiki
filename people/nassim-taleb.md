# Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Former derivatives trader turned philosopher and statistician. Author of the Incerto series — *Fooled by Randomness*, *The Black Swan*, *Antifragile*, *Skin in the Game*. Core obsession: how humans systematically misunderstand randomness, risk, and uncertainty — and how to build systems that benefit from them rather than break.

> "I'd rather be dumb and antifragile than extremely smart and fragile."

## The Incerto Series

| Book | Core Idea |
|---|---|
| *Fooled by Randomness* | Luck is systematically mistaken for skill |
| *The Black Swan* | Rare, high-impact events dominate history; we can't predict them |
| *Antifragile* | Some systems gain from disorder; build to benefit from uncertainty |
| *Skin in the Game* | Accountability requires exposure to your own recommendations |

## Core Philosophy

Taleb's worldview is built around a few linked ideas:

1. **The world is dominated by [Black Swans](../concepts/black-swan.md)** — rare, unpredictable, high-impact events that we rationalise away after the fact
2. **Most forecasting is noise** — we are pattern-matching creatures in a world that is less predictable than we think
3. **[Antifragility](../concepts/antifragility.md) is the goal** — not robustness (surviving shocks) but thriving *because* of them
4. **[Skin in the game](../concepts/skin-in-the-game.md)** — systems rot when decision-makers don't bear the consequences of their decisions

## Contrast with Naval Ravikant

Both Taleb and [Naval Ravikant](./naval-ravikant.md) value long-term thinking and are deeply sceptical of conventional wisdom, but they diverge in important ways:

| Dimension | Taleb | Naval |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary lens** | Risk & uncertainty | Wealth & leverage |
| **On knowledge** | Beware of theorising; bias toward practice | Build specific knowledge through curiosity |
| **On fragility** | Systems must be built to withstand shocks | Compound interest works — avoid ruin |
| **On institutions** | Deep scepticism; bureaucracies are fragile | Less focus on institutional critique |
| **Disposition** | Contrarian, combative | Serene, philosophical |

**Where they converge:** Both argue for long-term thinking, the power of compounding, deep scepticism of credentialism, and the importance of direct skin-in-the-game accountability.

## Key Quotes

> "The tragedy of modernity: as with neurotically overprotective parents, those trying to help are often hurting us the most."

> "We can live happily in a world we don't understand."

> "Don't confuse rationalising with rational — the two are almost always exact opposites."

> "Policies and actions in which the benefits are small and visible, and the side effects potentially severe and invisible."
