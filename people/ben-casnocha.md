# Ben Casnocha

Entrepreneur, writer, and co-author of business books with [Reid Hoffman](reid-hoffman.md). Known for applying entrepreneurial thinking to career and life strategy.

## Background

- **Founded Comcate at age 14:** Enterprise software company for local governments (civic engagement technology)
- **Writer and speaker:** Focused on entrepreneurship, career development, and personal growth
- **Collaborated with Reid Hoffman:** Co-authored multiple books applying startup thinking to careers and management

## Key Works

### The Start-up of You (2012, with Reid Hoffman)

See [sources/hoffman-startup-of-you.md](../sources/hoffman-startup-of-you.md) for full summary.

Core frameworks:
- [Permanent beta](../concepts/permanent-beta.md) — treat yourself as always evolving
- [ABZ planning](../concepts/abz-planning.md) — Plan A (current), Plan B (pivot), Plan Z (lifeboat)
- Competitive advantage = Assets + Aspirations + Market realities
- [Network intelligence](../concepts/network-intelligence.md) and [weak ties](../concepts/weak-ties.md)
- [I^We](../concepts/i-we.md) — individual success through collective networks

### The Alliance (2014, with Reid Hoffman and Chris Yeh)

Reframes the employer-employee relationship:
- Traditional model: lifetime employment (obsolete) or transactional free agency (no loyalty)
- **Alliance model:** "Tour of duty" — defined commitment period with mutual investment
- Both parties invest in each other with clear expectations
- Encourages employees to build external networks (good for both)

## Core Philosophy

**Apply startup thinking to everything:**
- Careers are startups: you're the founder, your career is the product
- Iteration over perfection: ship, learn, adapt
- Networks as core infrastructure: opportunities come through people
- Intelligent risk-taking: calculated bets with safety nets

## Writing Style

- Practical, action-oriented frameworks
- Synthesizes academic research (Granovetter, Christensen) with Silicon Valley practitioner wisdom
- Clear, accessible language; avoids academic jargon
- Heavy use of real examples from tech/business world
