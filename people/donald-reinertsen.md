# Donald G. Reinertsen

Consultant, lecturer, and author specialising in product development management. Principal of Reinertsen & Associates. Author of three influential books on product development — most notably *The Principles of Product Development Flow* (2009), which Reinertsen himself describes as exposing a paradigm that is "not just a little wrong, but wrong to its very core."

His core contribution: **applying queueing theory and economic thinking to product development**, revealing that the real enemy of speed and quality is invisible, unmanaged queues — and providing a systematic framework for eliminating them.

## The Core Thesis

> "The dominant paradigm for managing product development is wrong. Not just a little wrong, but wrong to its very core."

The standard approach to product development optimises for **resource utilisation** — keep everyone busy. Reinertsen shows this is precisely backwards. High utilisation causes queues. Queues cause delays. Delays destroy value. The correct optimisation target is **[economic flow](../concepts/flow-economics.md)**, not utilisation.

## The Eight Domains

The 175 principles are organised into eight areas:

1. **Economics** — frame everything in terms of economic outcomes; quantify [Cost of Delay](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md)
2. **Queues** — understand and manage invisible [queues](../concepts/queues-in-product-development.md); they are the root cause of slow delivery
3. **Variability** — not all variability is bad; manage it asymmetrically
4. **Batch Size** — smaller batches reduce cycle time, risk, and feedback latency
5. **WIP Constraints** — limit [Work in Progress](../concepts/wip-limits.md) to control queues
6. **Flow Control** — manage the rate of work entering the system
7. **Fast Feedback** — accelerate all feedback loops
8. **Decentralised Control** — push decisions to where information lives

## The Economic Frame

Reinertsen insists every product development decision is an economic decision — but most organisations lack the economic framework to make them correctly. The fix:

1. **Quantify [Cost of Delay](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md)** — make the cost of time visible
2. **Use [CD3 / WSJF](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md)** — prioritise by economic impact, not intuition
3. **Make trade-offs explicit** — speed vs cost vs quality are not conflicts; they are trade-offs with quantifiable values

## Contrast with Naval, Taleb, Dalio

| Dimension | Reinertsen | Naval | Taleb | Dalio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary lens** | Queueing economics | Leverage & knowledge | Risk & antifragility | Principles & systems |
| **On variability** | Manage asymmetrically; not all bad | Not primary focus | Embrace it; be antifragile | Model and stress-test |
| **On batch size** | Smaller is almost always better | Not primary focus | Small options (barbell) | Not primary focus |
| **On feedback** | Accelerate all feedback loops | Learn through doing | Reality-test constantly | 5-step loop |
| **On control** | Decentralise to where info lives | Individual leverage | Skin in the game | Idea meritocracy |

**Where they converge:** All four argue that **conventional management wisdom is systematically wrong** and that the correct approach requires uncomfortable counter-intuitive thinking based on first principles.
